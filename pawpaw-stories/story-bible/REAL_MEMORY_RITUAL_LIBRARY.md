# Real Memory & Ritual Library

These are source memories, not final episode assignments.

## 1. Throwing / "Baw"
Weston has a strong overhand throw, accuracy and distance. Around age one he and PawPaw threw toys back and forth; PawPaw once dozed and a large plastic block hit him between the eyes. Weston calls round things "baw"/ball, including things that should not be thrown (e.g. an orange).

## 2. Chest Charge
Weston runs across the room, plants both hands in PawPaw's chest, PawPaw falls backward, Weston falls onto him squealing, PawPaw rocks up and returns Weston to his feet. Repeat until PawPaw's back gives out.

## 3. PawPaw Rodeo
PawPaw on stomach; Weston climbs onto his back, arms/legs holding on. PawPaw rises to hands and knees and bucks/rears. Weston becomes brave enough to lift one or both hands, then grabs back on.

## 4. Flip / Couch Climb
PawPaw sits on floor, legs in a V. Weston climbs PawPaw toward couch behind him. PawPaw blindly reaches back; Weston dodges but wants to be caught. PawPaw catches, lifts and flips him to land upright facing the opposite direction. Weston runs away squealing and charges back.

## 5. Crawling / Finding
Since Weston first crawled, PawPaw crawls quickly and hides behind furniture; Weston follows, then roles reverse. PawPaw sometimes crawls directly over Weston, creating two moving levels. No winner; finding one another is the game.

## 6. Cowboy Boots
Weston loved PawPaw's boots before walking. He would put his legs down inside them and later try to walk in them. PawPaw bought Weston matching black boots for his first birthday and intends a matching pair every birthday. In the oversized boots Weston falls and tries again. PawPaw stands above him and gives BOTH hands for balance. Weston himself scoots each boot forward. PawPaw steadies him; he does not move the boots or carry Weston.

## 7. Dance Time
No music is required. PawPaw can sit, kneel or stand and simply move his hips side to side with arms bent. Weston instantly recognizes the signal, squeals, swings his arms, hops and twists with full commitment. A little song toy may sometimes be used, but PawPaw's body language is the real trigger.

## 8. Little-Man Lunch / Eating
Eating is part of every babysitting day. Weston loves cheese puffs and would keep eating them if allowed; PawPaw brings an absurdly large container and redirects him to limit quantity. They also share chicken nuggets/fries and sit together. The deeper ritual is fellowship.

## 9. Big Chair / Bottle / Quiet Time
After high-energy play, Weston still drinks a bottle. He crawls into PawPaw's lap in the big recliner, gets tightly under PawPaw's arm, and they look at the enormous 60-inch TV. Quiet/rest is part of the adventure.

## 10. Toy Box Explosion & Cleanup
Fake-leather toy box roughly 40 x 18 x 24–26 inches, in front of TV. Over a session Weston pulls nearly everything out; he may throw toys toward PawPaw or push the box around. PawPaw allows harmless energy/exploration. At the end they put the toys back. Room clutter is a visual clock.

## 11. Big Yoga Ball
Weston lies belly-down across a large yoga ball with arms/legs hanging off while PawPaw bounces him. PawPaw also rolls his own belly over it; Weston imitates him and tries.

## 12. Small Ball Roll
PawPaw and Weston sit far apart and roll a smaller rubber ball back and forth repeatedly. Calm reciprocity rather than throwing.

## 13. GiGi / Itsy Bitsy Spider
Kelly sang and performed Itsy Bitsy Spider with Grace when Grace was little. She later did it with Weston from the day he was born and has now begun doing it with Waylon. This is a multi-generation ritual: Kelly → Grace → Weston → Waylon.

## 14. Weston & Waylon / Gentle
Grace taught Weston "be gentle." Weston may approach Waylon quickly, then dramatically slow his hand, hover above his head/tummy, barely touch him, and run back to his own world. PawPaw redirects rambunctious throwing away from Waylon. The arc can move from PawPaw reminding Weston where his brother is to Weston noticing and adjusting on his own.

## 15. Waylon care transitions
Over 5–6 babysitting hours Waylon naturally needs 2–3 feedings plus diaper changes. These slow the room and redirect Weston. Weston often recognizes Waylon's need first and moves toward the relevant object.

## 16. PawPaw leaves and returns
Weston can briefly lose his smile when PawPaw leaves a room, softly seek him, and recover when PawPaw returns. PawPaw intentionally teaches that separation is temporary and reliable people return.
