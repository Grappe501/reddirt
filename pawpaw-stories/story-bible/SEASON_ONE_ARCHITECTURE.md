# Season One — The Babysitting Season

## Format
10 episodes. Cartoon target ~10 minutes, followed by unrestricted Memory Dissolve / A Note From PawPaw.

Most episodes heavily reuse the same living room. A few may expand to doctor, park or other family outings. Episode One remains entirely in one living room even when imagination transforms it.

## Deep season question
What does it mean to be there for somebody?

Showing up; playing; listening; boundaries; sharing attention; patience; trying; helping up; holding on and letting go; leaving and returning.

## Episode engine
All/most familiar rituals may recur, but nine are compressed into quick familiar beats while ONE featured ritual receives substantial screen time and carries the central lesson.

Do not finalize the ten featured slots until the real-memory library is sufficiently complete.

## Current strong episode engines
There is already enough real material for 10–12 strong premises without padding:
- PawPaw Always Comes Back / separation and return
- Throwing / aim

Big Chair, feeding, diapering, lunch and cleanup can also function as recurring anchor rhythms rather than featured episodes.

## Daily room rhythm
Arrival/order → glorious chaos → slowing/caregiving → cleanup/order → Big Chair/rest → return.

## Pilot
Working title: **PawPaw Always Comes Back**.
Five layers: play; trust; secure love; compressed time; mortality/legacy.
Recurring games can embody departure/return: thrown object, charge, rodeo, flip, crawling, attention moving between children, parents leaving/returning, PawPaw leaving/returning.
Pilot may hide visual seeds for the ten later featured lessons in chronological season order.

## GiGi placement
GiGi should remain almost invisible/background for approximately 6–7 episodes before her featured Itsy Bitsy Spider episode. Her featured episode should therefore land late enough for the background motif to accumulate.
