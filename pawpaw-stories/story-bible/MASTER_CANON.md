# PawPaw's Adventures — Master Canon

Status: living canon. Preserve real family memories first; invent only connective story material.

## Purpose
A children's cartoon/storybook series for Weston and Waylon, built from their real childhood with PawPaw. It should work as joyful children's entertainment now and as a family time capsule later. Season One is the Babysitting Season.

## Core cast
- PawPaw: bald, glasses, gray beard; signature black PawPaw Adventure Crew shirt; cowboy boots.
- Weston: older brother, exactly 2 in Story One; strawberry-blond hair; extremely happy, physical, curious.
- Waylon: 3 months in Story One; dark brown hair; no pacifier; happy, observant, coos and communicates nonverbally.
- GiGi (Kelly): recurring grandmother, primarily deep-background until her featured episode.
- Grace: mother of Weston and Waylon; Kelly's daughter.
- Gavin: father of Weston and Waylon.

Do not force physical resemblance between boys and PawPaw. Grace's adoption history is irrelevant to children's stories.

## Emotional canon
PawPaw = adventure plus safety. Weston is ecstatic when PawPaw arrives, settles into quiet closeness later, and learns that PawPaw can leave and return. The boys are exceptionally happy; crying is not their default dramatic language. Weston already has a deep sibling bond with Waylon and often recognizes his needs before PawPaw acts.

## Season One
Ten ~10-minute cartoon stories centered on babysitting/family caregiving, mostly reusing one living room. The deeper season question is: what does it mean to be there for somebody?

Each episode may contain many recurring rituals in short form, while one featured game/ritual expands and carries the episode's central lesson.

## Ending convention — Memory Dissolve
At roughly the end of the cartoon, the final animated composition dissolves into a REAL family photograph, never an AI recreation. Then PawPaw reads a full "A Note From PawPaw" in Steve's real voice. Use real photos with minimal movement. The letter is not squeezed into the cartoon runtime. End simply with "Love, PawPaw" and black.

Choose ending photos before animation so each episode can emotionally arrive at the real image.

## Storytelling laws
1. Experience first; lesson second.
2. Do not make Weston verbally older for exposition.
3. Movement, gesture, inflection and choices are dialogue.
4. Family rituals are learned by the audience through repetition, not exposition.
5. Recurring childhood rituals transform as the boys age rather than simply disappearing.
6. Background stories may carry meaning that is only understood on rewatch.
7. Real family details outrank invented convenience.
8. Keep lessons useful at age 2, adolescence and adulthood.
