# Adventure Crew Language Dictionary

## Rule
Never invent generic fake gibberish. Weston sounds like a real toddler: cadence, partial words, sounds, gestures, movement and inflection all carry meaning. Real recordings, when available, outrank phonetic spellings.

## N-ak
Canonical spelling: **N-ak**. No "y" sound. It is Weston's general word for food/eating/snack, not one specific food. Meaning comes from context and action.

Examples:
- sees cheese puffs: "N-ak"
- indicates bottle: "N-ak"
- Waylon makes hungry sound; Weston says "N-ak" and moves toward Waylon's bottle.

Do not give Weston the word "eat" merely to explain the scene.

## Baw
Weston's word family for ball/round things. It can sound like baw/ba/ball/bol depending on the moment. Inflection and context matter. He may call an orange "Baw?" and PawPaw can redirect: it is not for throwing.

## Waylon
Waylon communicates through coos, "ehh," "wah," "eee," "umm," looks, smiles, eyebrows, kicks, arms and body movement.

## Three-layer communication device
Early episodes:
Waylon signal → Weston understands → Weston sound/action → PawPaw interprets.

Over time, clues reveal PawPaw may understand Waylon directly. Eventually the audience realizes PawPaw understood all along. Never explain why; it should feel like attention and relationship, not supernatural magic.

## Movement is vocabulary
Examples:
- Weston moves toward bottle = hunger/feeding information.
- Weston moves toward diapers = diaper information.
- Weston slows his hand near Waylon = gentle.
- PawPaw hip movement = dance time.
- GiGi finger position = Itsy Bitsy Spider.
