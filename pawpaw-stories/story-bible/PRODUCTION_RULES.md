# Production Rules

## Visual approach
Warm simplified dimensional cartoon/storybook style; expressive silhouettes; animation-friendly; static/separable backgrounds and characters. Do not chase photorealism.

## Efficient animation
A 10-minute episode should use shot design and reusable choreography rather than independent generated frames. Target roughly 50–80 shots, with only a minority requiring complex hero animation.

Build reusable action libraries for Weston, PawPaw and Waylon and master choreography modules for recurring games. Reuse assets while varying camera, timing, interruption, wardrobe and outcome.

## Living-room geography
Primary axis: big recliner → open play floor → toy box → enormous TV.
Little recliner is a separate persistent prop.
Build permanent camera positions including floor/toddler eye, couch wide, recliner, doorway, behind chair/couch, PawPaw POV, Weston POV, Waylon floor POV, overhead, close conversation and crawling-height tracking.

## Waylon fantasy allowance
At three months, gentle cartoon fantasy permits Waylon to sit stationary independently or crawl a little when story needs it. Reusable states: sitting, tummy, crawl, held, lying, sleeping, with small head/eye/smile/arm/leg movements.

## Real-image rule
Memory Dissolve uses authentic family photos/video, not AI recreation. Ordinary clutter and imperfections are historical information and should not be sanitized away.

## Sound
Cartoon can carry full soundscape/music. At Memory Dissolve, sound drops substantially and Steve speaks as himself in his real voice, not as a performed cartoon character.
