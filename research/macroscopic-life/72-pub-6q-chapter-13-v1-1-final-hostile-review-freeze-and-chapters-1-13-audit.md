**CLOSED.**

The chapter now states:

## 2. Civilization as owner of sensing capability

**CLOSED.**

The chapter now distinguishes civilization as a container from the bounded systems inside it and explicitly asks whether capability belongs to an institution, technical-social network, or some larger boundary.

## 3. Archival persistence versus system memory

**CLOSED.**

The chapter now states that persistence through turnover becomes evidence for memory of a proposed larger system only when the retained information remains causally available to that same operationally defined system and alters later state or behavior.

The new firewall is decisive:

> **ARCHIVAL PERSISTENCE IS NECESSARY FOR SOME FORMS OF INSTITUTIONAL MEMORY. IT IS NOT BY ITSELF PROOF OF A HIGHER-ORDER REMEMBERER.**

## 4. Cyber-physical systems generalized to civilization

**CLOSED.**

The NIST example now explicitly says its strength is the ability to specify an engineered boundary and task, and warns against generalizing from bounded engineered systems to civilization without defining and testing the larger boundary.

## 5. Test 8 weak baseline / correlation problem

**CLOSED.**

Test 8 now contains explicit failure conditions:

- appropriate component-plus-interaction models must be compared;
- prediction must survive out-of-sample testing;
- outcome leakage invalidates the result;
- artificially weak baselines invalidate the inference.

The chapter's strongest Test 8 line is now:

> **TEST 8 IS NOT WON BY FINDING CORRELATION AT A LARGE SCALE. IT IS WON ONLY BY PREDICTIVE INFORMATION THAT SURVIVES FAIR MODEL COMPETITION.**

## 6. Test 9 bundled micro-intervention problem

**CLOSED.**

The chapter now distinguishes a genuinely discriminating higher-level intervention from merely changing many components and giving the bundle a macro-level name.

It also explicitly states that if an appropriate lower-level model predicts the same physical intervention equally well, the macro-description may remain useful but Test 9 has not established additional causal leverage.

This is the required burden of proof.
