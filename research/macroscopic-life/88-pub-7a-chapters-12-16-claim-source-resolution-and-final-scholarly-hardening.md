# SOURCE POLICY FOR ACT IV AND ACT V

These chapters carry the book's strongest inferential burden.

Therefore the source standard rises.

A source may support:

1. **an empirical phenomenon** — e.g., groups can outperform individuals under defined conditions;
2. **a theoretical framework** — e.g., distributed cognition, major transitions, interventionist causation;
3. **a methodological possibility** — e.g., multivariate information measures;
4. **a live controversy** — e.g., whether a macro-variable deserves autonomous causal status;
5. **a project synthesis** — e.g., Model C / Model D and the Eleven Tests.

No source may be cited as though it supports category 5 merely because it supports categories 1–4.

That rule is especially important in Chapters 12–16.

# CHAPTER 12 — INTELLIGENCE WITHOUT A LITTLE MAN

## Central scientific claim

> **THE WHOLE CAN SOLVE A PROBLEM THAT NO SINGLE COMPONENT SOLVES ALONE.**

This is defensible when `whole`, `problem`, and `performance` are operationally defined.

It does not require consciousness, central control, or a single inner executive.

## Source families

### 12.1 Collective intelligence

Use literature on collective decision-making, wisdom-of-crowds mechanisms, collaborative problem solving, distributed expertise, swarm intelligence, and information aggregation.

Strong anchor:

- Kameda, Toyokawa & Tindale (2022), *Information aggregation and collective intelligence beyond the wisdom of crowds*, **Nature Reviews Psychology** 1:345–357.

Use for:

- groups can aggregate heterogeneous information;
- collective performance depends strongly on aggregation rules and coordination;
- collective intelligence is not automatic;
- correlated errors and poor communication can reduce performance.

Do **not** use it to claim a group becomes one cognitive individual.

### 12.2 Collaborative synergy

Use review literature distinguishing simple aggregation from true performance gains attributable to collaboration.

Strong anchor:

- Tindale and collaborators / Annual Review literature on collective choice, collaboration, and communication.

Use to support:

- strong versus weak synergy;
- groups can exceed best-member performance under some conditions;
- information distribution and communication structure matter.

### 12.3 Swarm / decentralized intelligence

Use insect, flocking, robotic, neural-network, and decentralized-control literature as examples of problem solving without a central homunculus.

Do not imply one mechanism unifies all systems.

### 12.4 Distributed cognition

Strong anchor:

- Edwin Hutchins, *Cognition in the Wild* (MIT Press, 1995/1996).

Use narrowly:

- cognitive/computational properties can be studied at the level of socially organized systems;
- representations, tools, people, and procedures can form task-performing systems;
- system-level properties may differ from participant-level properties.

Important controversy note:

The distributed/extended cognition literature disagrees about when external artifacts should count literally as parts of cognition rather than as causally important tools.

The chapter correctly avoids needing to settle that dispute.

### 12.5 Collective intelligence in technologically mediated society

Use modern literature on networked collective intelligence and digital platforms.

A recent useful review anchor:

- literature on collective intelligence under digital mediation and AI-supported collaboration.

Use only to support that technology changes aggregation, access, coordination, and communication.

Do not infer one societal mind.

# CHAPTER 12 CLAIM HARDENING

## Claim: system-level capability

Safe form:

> A capability may be attributed to an organized system when the reproducible task performance depends on relationships among components and is not possessed by any isolated component in the same form.

This is a functional attribution.

It is not yet an individuality claim.

## Claim: whole-only information

This is more technically delicate.

Use information-theoretic literature on multivariate dependence, synergy, redundancy, and Partial Information Decomposition (PID) as methodological background.

A useful modern review:

- review literature on PID distinguishing unique, redundant, and synergistic information.

Required brake:

> The existence of synergistic or relational information in a multivariate system does not establish a new biological individual. It establishes that prediction may depend on relationships not recoverable from one variable alone.

## Test 8 status

**Whole-Only Information is a project-level test inspired by multivariate information and system-level prediction literatures. It is not a canonical biological individuality criterion.**

This sentence or equivalent belongs in the endnote apparatus or methodological appendix.

# TEST 8 — WHOLE-ONLY INFORMATION

This test carries high technical risk.

## Genealogy

Relevant fields:

- relational information;
- synergy;
- Partial Information Decomposition;
- network statistics;

## Required disclaimer

There is no single settled measure of `whole-only information` across all systems.

Therefore Book One should not imply:

> `science has a standard whole-information meter and civilization scores high.`

Instead:

> Test whether relational/macroscopic variables provide reproducible out-of-sample predictive information beyond appropriately constructed component-only models.

This is defensible and methodologically open.

## Exact scientific firewall

> **WHOLE-ONLY INFORMATION IS INFORMATION IN ORGANIZATION — NOT INFORMATION FLOATING ABOVE MATTER.**

Keep.

# TEST 9 — HIGHER-LEVEL INTERVENTION

This is the most philosophically contested test.

## Genealogy

Relevant fields:

- Woodward-style interventionism;
- causal modeling;
- multilevel causation;
- causal emergence;
- philosophy of higher-level explanation.

## Scholarly dispute

Higher-level causal explanation is common and often useful in science.

But interventionist frameworks face nontrivial questions concerning variable choice, level comparison, mixed-level models, proportionality, stability, and causal exclusion.

Useful counterweight literature includes work criticizing overly permissive high-level variable selection in interventionist accounts.

Therefore the chapter must not claim that finding a useful macro-variable automatically proves ontologically autonomous higher-level causation.

The existing test formulation is strong precisely because it demands comparison against matched lower-level descriptions.

## Required wording

> A useful macro-variable may provide explanatory or predictive leverage without thereby becoming a new biological individual.

And:

> **A HIGHER-LEVEL INTERVENTION IS NOT ESTABLISHED BY CHANGING MANY COMPONENTS AND GIVING THE BUNDLE A MACRO NAME.**

Keep.
