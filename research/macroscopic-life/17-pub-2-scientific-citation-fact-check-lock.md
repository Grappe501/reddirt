# Source doctrine

Priority order:

1. peer-reviewed primary research for specific empirical claims;
2. recent peer-reviewed reviews for field-level synthesis;
3. major scholarly reference works for contested conceptual terminology;
4. authoritative scientific/educational institutions for stable background;
5. popular science only for optional historical/color material, never as the sole support for a load-bearing scientific claim.

Every citation must support the sentence actually written. A source supporting a narrow phenomenon may not be used to support a larger Macroscopic Life inference.

The manuscript continues to distinguish:

- **A — Established science**
- **B — Plausible extrapolation / synthesis**
- **C — Macroscopic Life hypothesis**

No C claim may be made to appear A by attaching a citation to nearby established science.

## Chapter 4 — The Civilization Inside Your Skin

### Load-bearing claim: mitochondrial endosymbiosis

**Fact-check result: PASS WITH PRECISION.**

The endosymbiotic origin of mitochondria from a bacterial lineage is strongly established. Contemporary work supports a bacterial/proto-mitochondrial partner as a key event in eukaryogenesis, while the exact identity, ecology, sequence, and timing of host/endosymbiont interactions remain active research questions.

### Required wording
Do not narrate one detailed eukaryogenesis scenario as settled fact.

Safe formulation:

> Mitochondria descend from a bacterial endosymbiont incorporated during the evolutionary origin of eukaryotic cells. The exact sequence of events and the ecology of the ancestral partners remain debated.

### Additional support
Endosymbiotic gene transfer and loss of organelle autonomy are well supported; most mitochondrial proteins in modern eukaryotes are nuclear encoded.

Microbiome section must remain cautious: host-microbiome integration varies and does not automatically make a holobiont a single evolutionary individual.

## Chapter 5 — When Parts Become a Whole

**PASS.**

The literature on evolutionary transitions to multicellularity treats cooperation/conflict and emergence of collective-level Darwinian properties as central problems. ### Mandatory boundary
Do not claim a single universally agreed list of `major transitions`. Do not treat endosymbiosis, multicellularity and eusociality as mechanistically identical.

## Chapter 7 — The Body Electric

### Load-bearing claims
- cells maintain electrochemical gradients and membrane potentials;
- ion channels, pumps and transporters participate in those states;
- bioelectric phenomena occur in non-neural tissue;
- endogenous electrical phenomena can participate in development, regeneration, migration and wound responses.

### Fact-check result
**PASS WITH STRONG BOUNDARY.**

Peer-reviewed developmental-bioelectricity literature supports roles for ion flows, voltage gradients, ion channels, pumps and gap-junction-mediated electrical coupling in cell behavior and pattern formation. Reviews also discuss endogenous bioelectric mechanisms in regeneration.

### Mandatory boundary
Do not generalize from these results to a universal bioelectric code or civilization-scale electrical mechanism. `Bioelectric` means ordinary physical biology.

### Thermodynamics
Living cells are nonequilibrium biochemical systems requiring sustained energy transformation. Nonequilibrium thermodynamics is useful for cellular dynamics, fluxes, signaling, adaptation and energetic costs. It is not a definition of life.

## Chapter 16 — We Are the Microbe

### Scientific status
The final Macroscopic Life statement is a **research hypothesis**, not a conclusion supported directly by the cited biological phenomena.

Established science supports premises including:
- observer limitations;
- nested biological organization;
- historical transitions in individuality;
- distributed regulation;
- non-neural bioelectric mechanisms;
- regeneration and positional information;
- memory outside conscious recall;
- anticipatory biological regulation;
- collective decision making.

Those premises motivate but do not prove higher-scale living individuals.

### Controlling hypothesis

> Macroscopic Life is the hypothesis that living or life-like organization may occur in nested hierarchies at scales larger than familiar individual organisms. A candidate higher-order individual must not be inferred from resemblance, complexity, connectivity, or useful macro-description alone. It must earn individual-level treatment through converging causal evidence that the proposed higher-level organization maintains historically continuous, bounded or asymmetrically coupled, self-affecting organization in ways that improve explanation and prediction beyond independent components plus shared environment. Strong biological claims additionally require appropriate evidence concerning reproduction, heredity, and selection at the proposed level.

No citation should be attached in a way that makes this hypothesis appear already demonstrated.

# Endnote architecture

Final publication manuscript should use endnotes rather than dense parenthetical citations in narrative prose.

Each chapter gets:

1. numbered endnotes tied to factual/load-bearing claims;
2. a short `Further Reading` group only where it adds value;
3. primary sources for specific experiments where practical;
4. reviews for broad synthesis;
5. explicit notes identifying authorial frameworks such as Observer Window, Macroscopic Organization, whole-only information, Macroscopic Individuality Matrix and the Macroscopic Life hypothesis.

The main text should remain readable without notes.
