# 2. CLAIM SHOWN

Living cells can maintain measurable voltage differences across their plasma membranes because ions are distributed unequally and membrane proteins selectively permit or actively drive ion movement.

Changes in these electrical states can participate in cellular communication, sensing, contraction, development, regeneration, and coordination, depending on cell type and biological context.

The figure does **not** claim that bioelectricity is the sole controller of biological form or behavior.

# 7. CRITICAL SCIENTIFIC CORRECTION — CHARGE SEPARATION

A key production requirement is preventing the common misconception that the entire inside of a cell is negatively charged while the entire outside is positively charged.

The voltage across a biological membrane is associated with a very small charge imbalance concentrated near the membrane interfaces while the bulk intracellular and extracellular solutions remain approximately electroneutral.

Therefore final art must:

- keep charge-separation symbols close to the membrane;
- avoid filling entire compartments with positive or negative symbols;
- explain that the visual particle separation is exaggerated for comprehension;
- avoid representing voltage as a glowing substance.

# 14. EIGHTH-GRADE COMPREHENSION VERSION

Core explanation:

A cell is surrounded by a very thin membrane. Different kinds of charged atoms, called ions, are found in different amounts on the two sides of that membrane. Proteins in the membrane act like selective doors and pumps. Because the cell controls which ions can move and where they move, a tiny voltage difference can form across the membrane.

That voltage is real. Scientists can measure it.

Cells can also change it.

Those changes can help cells send signals, respond to their surroundings, contract, coordinate with nearby cells, and participate in development or repair.

But electricity is not a magical force controlling the body from outside ordinary biology. It is one part of ordinary biology.

# 17. AUDIO DESCRIPTION DRAFT

The figure begins with a cell and zooms into its thin outer membrane. Different ions are present in different concentrations inside and outside the cell. Proteins embedded in the membrane act as channels, transporters, and pumps, controlling how those ions move. This combination of ion gradients and selective movement produces a small voltage difference across the membrane. The charge imbalance is concentrated very close to the membrane; the fluids farther away remain nearly electrically neutral. Scientists can measure this voltage directly. Changes in membrane voltage can contribute to communication, contraction, sensing, development, repair, and coordination, depending on the biological system. The diagram ends by emphasizing that these are ordinary physical biological processes. Bioelectricity is important, but it is not a mystical energy field and it does not replace genetic, chemical, mechanical, or metabolic regulation.
