# 2. Chapter 15 — hostile prosecution review

## Attack A — the skeptic is a straw man

**FAILS.**

The skeptical model accepts almost every interesting phenomenon developed in the book: emergence, distributed intelligence, memory-like persistence, coordination, feedback, adaptation, energetic organization, forecasting, repair, and higher-scale organization.

It refuses only the additional claim that those phenomena require one new individual.

This is the strongest relevant null.

> **REAL ORGANIZATION DOES NOT REQUIRE A NEW ORGANISM.**

## Attack B — the prosecution only attacks simplistic organism definitions

**FAILS.**

The chapter explicitly acknowledges pluralism in biological individuality and does not force organism, biological individual, evolutionary individual, and higher-order individual into one universal definition.

The target is narrower and legitimate: whether Model D earns treatment of the candidate as a new higher-order individual rather than Model C macroscopic organization.

## Attack C — analogy stacking is still doing the persuasive work

**FAILS.**

The chapter attacks its own analogy architecture directly:

> **TEN CAREFUL ANALOGIES DO NOT ADD UP TO ONE IDENTITY.**

The book's biological vocabulary is explicitly denied cumulative evidentiary force unless mechanism and model competition support it.

## Attack D — the candidate boundary can always move

**FAILS.**

Boundary instability is treated as evidence against a well-formed Model D rather than an excuse to expand the candidate.

The chapter explicitly attacks post-result boundary movement.

## Attack E — higher-level explanatory variables are smuggled into organismhood

**FAILS.**

> **A USEFUL MACRO-VARIABLE IS NOT YET A NEW INDIVIDUAL.**

The skeptic is allowed to accept higher-level prediction, causal description, emergence, and relational information while retaining Model C.

## Attack F — civilization-scale intelligence is merely human intelligence renamed

**ACKNOWLEDGED AS A CENTRAL OBJECTION.**

> **DISTRIBUTED HUMAN INTELLIGENCE ≠ A NEW CIVILIZATION-SCALE MIND.**

The presence of already-intelligent components raises the attribution burden.

## Attack G — coordinated civilization behavior establishes agency

**FAILS.**

> **COORDINATED ACTION ≠ ONE AGENT**

> **A SHARED PROBLEM ≠ A SHARED GOAL**

> **A MAJORITY DECISION ≠ A CIVILIZATION-SCALE WILL**

## Attack H — conflict is hand-waved as normal imperfection

**FAILS.**

Conflict is treated as potentially serious negative evidence. The text does not require zero conflict, but asks whether civilization-scale mechanisms constrain conflict in ways that discriminate Model D from an ecology of cooperating and competing agents.

Model C is explicitly described as comfortable with the observed mixture.

## Attack I — repair is anthropomorphized

**FAILS.**

> **REBUILDING ≠ REGENERATION BY DEFAULT.**

Human and institutional reconstruction remains the lower-level default explanation unless candidate-level autonomy is demonstrated.

## Attack J — archives are called a global memory

**FAILS.**

> **GLOBAL MEMORY CAPACITY ≠ A GLOBAL REMEMBERER.**

## Attack K — many forecasters are called one predictor

**FAILS.**

> **MANY PREDICTORS ≠ ONE PREDICTOR**

## Attack L — thermodynamics is used as a life criterion

**FAILS.**

> **NONEQUILIBRIUM ORGANIZATION ≠ LIFE**

Energetic organization is explicitly separated from organism classification.

## Attack M — information becomes metaphysical glue

**FAILS.**

The manuscript requires physical carriers, measurable variables, and causal pathways and explicitly rejects information as magical explanatory vocabulary.

## Attack N — civilization reproduction is invented from cultural spread

**FAILS STRONGLY.**

> **SPREAD ≠ REPRODUCTION**

> **CULTURAL TRANSMISSION ≠ CIVILIZATION-LEVEL HEREDITY BY DEFAULT**

The lack of demonstrated reproduction/heredity remains a major weakness for an evolutionary-individuality claim.

## Attack O — sample-of-one problem is ignored

**FAILS.**

The chapter explicitly notes that one known planetary technical civilization sharply limits inference about transition frequency, lineage, heredity, and reproduction.

## Attack P — reversibility is either ignored or incorrectly treated as disproof

**FAILS.**

Reversibility is treated correctly as evidence whose meaning depends on model comparison, not automatic disproof.

## Attack Q — `emerging` makes the hypothesis immune to thresholds

**FAILS.**

> `Emerging` cannot mean `immune to failure.`

Gradual or multidimensional individuality still requires measurable dimensions and empirical commitments.

## Attack R — every failure can be renamed immaturity

**FAILS.**

The chapter directly attacks this rescue strategy and repeats:

> **A THEORY THAT CANNOT LOSE IS NOT A SCIENTIFIC THEORY.**

## Attack S — Model C is not itself falsifiable

**FAILS.**

This was an important possible weakness and PUB-7H closes it. The skeptic is explicitly required to move if prospectively specified observations show stable candidate boundaries, integration, repair, whole-level predictive advantage, higher-level causal leverage, or required reproduction/heredity that Model C cannot explain as well.

Model competition is therefore symmetric enough to remain scientifically meaningful.
