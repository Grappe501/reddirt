# Macroscopic Life — Research Queue

This queue is the build order for the intellectual project. Research passes should produce source notes, evidence-ledger updates, chapter implications, and unresolved questions.

## Phase 1 — Foundations for the Intro Book

### Pass 1 — Sensory Worlds
Research snakes, bats, worms, hermit crabs, electroreception, magnetoreception, polarization vision, chemoreception, echolocation, and the concept of Umwelt.

Deliverable: what each organism can detect that humans cannot, with strict sourcing.

### Pass 2 — Human Perceptual Limits
Map the electromagnetic spectrum, sound ranges, instrumentation, cognitive filtering, and examples of physically real phenomena outside unaided human perception.

### Pass 3 — Embedded Observers and Dimensions
Research Flatland-style dimensional reasoning, higher-dimensional mathematics, limits of observation from inside a system, and what can responsibly be said about additional physical dimensions.

### Pass 4 — Temporal Scale
Research biological clocks, lifespan variation, geological processes, evolutionary time, stellar time, slow adaptive systems, and methods for recognizing processes operating outside human timescales.

### Pass 5 — Bioelectricity
Deep research on membrane potentials, action potentials, cardiac electrophysiology, neural oscillations, developmental bioelectricity, wound healing, and bioelectric pattern regulation.

### Pass 6 — Resonance and Synchronization
Research physical resonance, coupled oscillators, entrainment, synchronization in biological systems, neural synchrony, cardiac rhythms, circadian entrainment, and where popular frequency claims exceed evidence.

### Pass 7 — Aura / Field Question
Inventory measurable emissions and fields around organisms, then separately map major aura traditions and modern claims. Identify any experimentally testable overlap claims.

### Pass 8 — Self-Similarity and Scaling
Research fractals, branching laws, vascular networks, river networks, roots, lungs, lightning, network topology, power laws, allometric scaling, and cosmic web comparisons.

### Pass 9 — Emergence
Research emergence in physics, biology, neuroscience, ant colonies, slime molds, flocking, markets, distributed computation, and collective intelligence.

### Pass 10 — What Is an Individual?
Research holobionts, microbiomes, symbiosis, colonial organisms, eusocial insects, extended phenotype, extended mind, and philosophical definitions of individuality.

## Phase 2 — Higher-Order Organization

### Pass 11 — Major Evolutionary Transitions
How independent units become integrated higher-order units: genes, chromosomes, cells, multicellularity, colonies, language, culture.

### Pass 12 — Superorganisms
What scientists mean by superorganism; where the analogy is accepted and where it breaks.

### Pass 13 — Homeostasis and Feedback
Control theory, cybernetics, biological regulation, negative and positive feedback, adaptive control, resilience, and systemic collapse.

### Pass 14 — Metabolism of Civilization
Quantify energy/material/information flows in cities and civilization. Research urban metabolism and industrial ecology.

### Pass 15 — Gaia and Planetary Regulation
Research James Lovelock, Lynn Margulis, Daisyworld, Earth-system science, strongest Gaia formulations, strongest criticisms, and modern planetary homeostasis work.

### Pass 16 — Multilevel Selection
Research group selection, multilevel selection, evolutionary transitions, cooperation, conflict, cheaters, and policing mechanisms.

## Phase 3 — Information and Intelligence

### Pass 17 — Information and Life
Research biological information, DNA, signaling, error correction, coding, memory, entropy, information theory, and competing definitions of life.

### Pass 18 — Distributed Intelligence
Research swarm intelligence, collective cognition, distributed decision-making, stigmergy, and decentralized control.

### Pass 19 — Consciousness
Map major scientific theories of consciousness, their evidence, disagreements, and whether any permit meaningful system-level consciousness beyond brains.

### Pass 20 — Extended Mind and Technological Cognition
External memory, language, tools, institutions, internet systems, human-machine cognition.

### Pass 21 — AI as Collective Interface
Trace AI from statistical learning to large models and agentic systems. Evaluate what it means for individuals to query representations of collective human information.

### Pass 22 — AI as Translation-Layer Hypothesis
Construct the strongest version and strongest critique of the hypothesis that AI could become an interface between humans and an emergent higher-order intelligence.

## Phase 4 — Physics, Reality, and Scale

### Pass 23 — Quantum Foundations
Superposition, entanglement, decoherence, measurement, fields, major interpretations. Explicitly catalogue common quantum pseudoscience errors.

### Pass 24 — Relativity and Observer Dependence
Frames of reference, spacetime, time dilation, simultaneity, and the difference between observer-dependence in physics and subjective perception.

### Pass 25 — Higher Dimensions
Mathematical dimensionality, Kaluza-Klein history, string-theory dimensions, branes, and what is speculative versus empirically constrained.

### Pass 26 — Cosmological Scale
Cosmic web, galaxies, clusters, dark matter, dark energy, horizon limits, large-scale structure, and observational constraints from living inside the universe.

### Pass 27 — Simulation Arguments
Bostrom and predecessors, computational universe ideas, objections, empirical-test proposals, and philosophical limits.

## Phase 5 — Mathematics of Recurrence

### Pass 28 — Scaling Laws
Power laws, allometry, universality, renormalization concepts, and scale invariance.

### Pass 29 — Networks
Small-world networks, scale-free networks, centrality, robustness, contagion, percolation, and biological/social/network comparisons.

### Pass 30 — Pattern Formation
Reaction-diffusion, self-organization, morphogenesis, cellular automata, criticality, and emergent order.

### Pass 31 — Resonance Across Mathematical Systems
Coupled oscillators, synchronization transitions, Kuramoto-type models, wave phenomena, and carefully defined cross-scale analogies.

## Phase 6 — Adversarial Testing

### Pass 32 — The Case Against Macroscopic Life
Build the strongest skeptical scientific argument that all observed correspondences are generic features of complex systems plus human pattern-seeking.

### Pass 33 — Anthropomorphism and Apophenia
Research human tendencies to infer agency, intention, faces, design, hierarchy, and purposeful organization.

### Pass 34 — Definition-of-Life Stress Test
Apply multiple scientific definitions of life to candidate macroscopic systems and document failures.

### Pass 35 — Falsifiability
Identify which Macroscopic Life claims are testable, which could become testable, and which must remain philosophy or metaphor.

### Pass 36 — Prediction Registry
For every surviving novel hypothesis, record unique predictions that differ from standard complex-systems explanations.

## Phase 7 — Intro Book Construction

### Pass 37 — Chapter Evidence Packets
Create a short, readable evidence packet for every chapter.

### Pass 38 — Narrative Bridges
Build the transitions that move the reader from sensory limits → scale → electricity/resonance → emergence → information → AI → macroscopic life.

### Pass 39 — Scientific Integrity Review
Review every sentence likely to be read as a scientific claim and assign its evidence class.

### Pass 40 — Manuscript Draft
Complete the short introductory manuscript.

## Phase 8 — Larger Work

After the introductory book is stable, build a separate master architecture for the full Macroscopic Life work including research monographs, models, interviews, simulations, visualizations, expert review, testable hypotheses, and publication strategy.
