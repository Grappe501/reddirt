# Macroscopic Life — Hostile Audit Phase 1 of 8

## Causal Ownership + Level Attribution

**Status: PHASE 1 BEGUN — FIRST FULL RED-TEAM PASS COMPLETE ON PUB-9C ADDITIONS**

This audit assumes the strongest skeptical position:

> Every apparent civilization-scale capability is actually performed by humans, institutions, machines, organisms, or local networks, and the word `civilization` adds no causal entity at all.

The burden is therefore not to show that large-scale functions exist. The burden is to show **who or what owns the function**, at what level, and what evidence could distinguish a genuinely useful macro-level causal variable from a convenient summary of lower-level events.

---

# Phase-1 ownership taxonomy

Every capability claim in Macroscopic Life should be classifiable into one of five levels.

## O1 — Component-owned

A familiar component performs the function.

Examples: a person remembers; a sensor measures; a server stores; a government issues an order.

**No macro-level inference allowed.**

## O2 — Subsystem-owned

An institution, organ, network, organization, or other identifiable subsystem performs the function through coordinated components.

Examples: a laboratory network detects an outbreak; a power grid balances load; an archive preserves records.

**Still no higher-order-individual inference allowed.**

## O3 — Distributed-relational

The capability depends on relations among multiple components/subsystems and is not naturally attributable to one component alone.

Examples can include distributed cognition, network synchronization, collective information aggregation, or multi-institutional coordination.

This is evidence for **macroscopic organization**, not automatically individuality.

## O4 — Macro-variable causally useful

A higher-level variable improves explanation, prediction, intervention, or control after serious lower-level baselines are included.

This is stronger evidence for **integration**.

It still does not by itself prove organismhood or higher-order individuality.

## O5 — Candidate whole owns the capability

The strongest attribution. The proposed whole has survived boundary, integration, intervention, model-competition, persistence, and other relevant tests strongly enough that attributing the capability to the whole does explanatory work that serious O1–O4 alternatives do not do as well.

**Book One currently does not establish O5 for civilization.**

That statement must remain explicit.

# Hostile finding register

## Finding 1 — The new four-stage hierarchy strengthens causal discipline

**Severity: PASS / MAJOR STRENGTH**

The revised hierarchy:

> **MACROSCOPIC PATTERN → MACROSCOPIC ORGANIZATION → INTEGRATION → HIGHER-ORDER INDIVIDUALITY**

is materially stronger than the earlier three-stage version because it prevents `organization` from jumping directly to `individuality`.

The new Integration barrier also asks for perturbation beyond a component-loss baseline. This is exactly the right direction for causal ownership.

### Hostile challenge

The phrase:

> `what the larger system can preserve, regulate, predict, repair, or do`

can grammatically assign ownership to the larger system **before ownership has been earned**.

### Recommended repair

Prefer:

> `whether perturbing those relationships changes system-level measures of preservation, regulation, prediction, repair, or performance beyond an appropriate component-loss baseline.`

This keeps the measurement at the macro level without prematurely saying the whole itself performs the function.

**Priority: P2.**

## Finding 2 — Chapter 13's null model is one of the strongest new additions

**Severity: PASS / MAJOR STRENGTH**

The new rival explanation explicitly says that humans and their machines/institutions may account for the observed organization without another individual existing above them.

This substantially reduces causal-ownership leakage.

The sentence:

> **A HIGHER-ORDER INDIVIDUAL MUST EXPLAIN OR PREDICT SOMETHING THE STRONGEST LOWER-LEVEL MODEL DOES NOT EXPLAIN AS WELL.**

is an excellent necessary condition.

### Hostile challenge

Prediction or explanation alone must never become a sufficient condition for individuality. A macro-variable can be predictively useful without representing a new biological individual.

### Required interpretation

This sentence means:

> Model D cannot win without predictive/explanatory advantage.

It does **not** mean:

> predictive/explanatory advantage makes Model D win.

The Eleven Tests and dependence review remain necessary.

**Priority: doctrinal lock, no prose expansion necessarily required.**

## Finding 3 — Chapter 16 contains the highest ownership-risk sentence in the new additions

**Severity: P1**

Current staged language:

> `Civilization is one candidate because it contains large-scale sensing, communication, persistent records, prediction, energetic organization, conflict-control mechanisms, repair, computation, and distributed problem solving.`

The word `contains` is cautious, but the noun list mixes phenomena at different ownership levels.

Examples:

- `persistent records` are often O1/O2 artifacts and archives;
- `computation` can be O1/O2 machines or O3 distributed systems;
- `distributed problem solving` can be O3;
- `large-scale sensing` may be networks of O1 sensors plus O2/O3 institutions;
- `prediction` often belongs to people, models, institutions, or machines;
- `repair` is especially risky because it sounds like organism-level autonomous repair;
- `conflict-control mechanisms` may describe legal/political institutions without showing organism-like internal conflict suppression at a higher selection level.

### Recommended replacement doctrine

Use ownership-neutral wording until a test earns stronger attribution:

> `Civilization is one candidate because civilization-scale systems contain sensor networks, communication networks, persistent external records, predictive institutions and models, large energy flows, mechanisms that constrain some internal conflicts, maintenance and recovery processes, computation, and distributed problem solving.`

Then add the brake:

> `The existence of those functions inside a civilization does not establish that civilization itself owns them.`

This would materially improve screenshot safety and causal precision.

**Priority: P1 — repair before freeze.**

## Finding 4 — `repair` is currently too ownership-loaded when moved upward

**Severity: P1**

In biology, repair can legitimately be attributed to an organism or tissue when mechanisms and system boundaries are already well established.

At civilization scale, `repair` can mean several different things:

- people repairing infrastructure;
- institutions restoring services;
- redundant networks rerouting around failure;
- markets reallocating resources;
- emergency systems responding to damage;
- actual macro-level autonomous restoration of a state variable.

These are not equivalent.

### Required rule

Before Test 7 Repair Autonomy is satisfied, prefer:

- `maintenance`;
- `recovery`;
- `restoration processes`;
- `repair-like system behavior`;

and identify the physical actors.

Reserve **the whole repairs itself** for evidence that survives Repair Autonomy plus Model Competition.

**Priority: P1.**

---

## Finding 5 — `prediction` requires an owner and a baseline

**Severity: P1**

A civilization can contain weather models, forecasters, markets, planning agencies, scientists, machine-learning systems, and other predictive subsystems.

That does not establish that `civilization predicts`.

### Required rule

Every upward prediction claim should specify:

1. predictor variables;
2. physical implementation;
3. target variable;
4. forecast horizon;
5. lower-level baseline;
6. whether the macro-state adds out-of-sample predictive power.

Only Test 6 Prediction Advantage can justify stronger macro-level language.

**Priority: P1 for research architecture; P2 for Book One prose if ownership-neutral wording is maintained.**

---

## Finding 6 — `memory` is well protected conceptually but needs ownership grammar

**Severity: PASS WITH P2 REFINEMENT**

The project already has an unusually strong brake:

> **THE PAST MATTERS LATER ONLY IF SOMETHING PHYSICAL PERSISTS AND CAN RE-ENTER CAUSAL PROCESSING.**

That is excellent.

But civilization-scale archives are not automatically `civilization's memory`.

An archive can persist while being causally irrelevant. A database can store information that no future process reads. Conversely, information can survive component turnover and alter later system behavior.

### Required distinction

- **storage:** physical persistence;
- **accessible record:** persistence that can be retrieved;
- **functional memory:** past state changes later behavior;
- **macro memory:** functional memory whose explanatory ownership survives lower-level alternatives and turnover testing.

This should guide Test 5 Memory Turnover.

**Priority: P2.**

## Finding 7 — Consciousness additions improve causal ownership rather than weaken it

**Severity: PASS / MAJOR STRENGTH**

The consciousness section explicitly separates:

> **SIGNAL ≠ INFORMATION ≠ INTEGRATION ≠ INTELLIGENCE ≠ AGENCY ≠ CONSCIOUSNESS**

and:

> **HIGHER-ORDER INDIVIDUALITY ≠ HIGHER-ORDER CONSCIOUSNESS.**

The strongest ownership brake is:

> **A COMPONENT'S INABILITY TO ACCESS THE WHOLE'S EXPERIENCE IS NOT EVIDENCE THAT A LARGER EXPERIENCE EXISTS.**

This successfully prevents the neuron/person analogy from becoming evidence for a civilization-scale subject.

### Remaining hostile requirement

Do not allow future language such as `civilization thinks`, `civilization wants`, `civilization knows`, or `civilization experiences` unless a separate consciousness/agency research layer earns those attributions.

**Priority: PASS; preserve exactly.**

---

## Finding 8 — Distributed cognition ancestry is safe only if ownership remains explicit

**Severity: P2**

Distributed cognition and collective intelligence can establish that cognitive work is sometimes distributed across people and artifacts.

They do not establish that the encompassing civilization is one cognitive subject.

### Required grammar

Preferred:

> `cognitive work is distributed across the system`

or

> `the coupled human-artifact network performs a task`

before:

> `civilization thinks`.

The latter is not currently earned.

---

## Finding 9 — Higher-Level Intervention is necessary but cannot alone establish the whole

**Severity: PASS WITH IMPORTANT LIMIT**

If an intervention on a well-defined macro variable changes downstream outcomes more predictively or efficiently than rival decompositions, that would be strong evidence that the macro variable is causally useful.

It would not, by itself, prove:

- organismhood;
- higher-order individuality;
- agency;
- consciousness;
- God;
- supernatural causation.

### Phase-1 conclusion

Higher-Level Intervention is best interpreted as a transition from O3 toward O4.

O5 requires the broader test architecture.

---

## Finding 10 — The research-program framing materially reduces motivated ownership

**Severity: PASS / MAJOR STRENGTH**

The statement that civilization may fail while the larger research program survives prevents the project from needing to force ambiguous capabilities upward.

That makes causal ownership more defensible because there is no structural requirement that civilization ultimately be declared the owner of sensing, memory, prediction, repair, or cognition.

Preserve this addition.

# Phase-1 standardized ownership grammar

Until stronger evidence exists, Book One and the research site should prefer this order:

1. **`contains`** — capability exists somewhere inside the candidate system;
2. **`supports`** — system relationships enable a capability;
3. **`exhibits at system scale`** — a measured macro pattern exists;
4. **`integrates`** — perturbation evidence supports relational dependence;
5. **`the whole performs/owns`** — reserve for a candidate that survives serious causal and model-comparison testing.

## Words requiring an owner check

Every use of these verbs/nouns should trigger review:

**senses / sensing / remembers / memory / predicts / prediction / repairs / repair / regulates / regulation / computes / computation / decides / decision / acts / agency / learns / learning / knows / knowledge / wants / goals / adapts / adaptation / communicates / communication / reproduces / heredity / intervenes / consciousness / experience.**
