# WHY A GENEALOGY IS NECESSARY

Without an explicit genealogy, a hostile academic reader can reasonably ask:

- Why eleven?
- Who says these are the right dimensions?
- Are they necessary?
- Are they sufficient?
- Are they independent?
- Are they biological criteria, causal tests, information criteria, or evolutionary criteria?
- Did the author invent a checklist that favors the desired conclusion?

The correct answer is not that the Eleven Tests descend intact from one authority.

They do not.

The correct answer is:

> The framework deliberately combines multiple literatures because the book is testing a cross-domain claim. It therefore requires evidence concerning boundary, integration, conflict, energetic organization, persistence, prospective performance, repair, relational information, causal intervention, reproduction/heredity, and comparative explanatory adequacy. The framework is provisional and itself testable and revisable.

# GENEALOGY SPINE — FOUNDATIONAL LITERATURE FAMILIES

## A. Major evolutionary transitions in individuality

Core intellectual problem:

How can entities that were previously more autonomous become parts of a new higher-level evolutionary individual?

Source families to resolve in final bibliography:

- John Maynard Smith & Eörs Szathmáry — major evolutionary transitions;
- Richard E. Michod — evolution and transitions in individuality, fitness transfer, cooperation/conflict;
- Stuart A. West, Roberta M. Fisher, Andy Gardner & E. Toby Kiers — comparative conditions associated with major transitions;
- David C. Queller & Joan E. Strassmann — cooperation, conflict, organismality.

Use for:

Tests 2, 3, 7, 10, 11 and the overall many-to-one transition logic.

Important limit:

This literature establishes that transitions in individuality occur in biological evolution and studies conditions associated with them. It does **not** establish that civilization is undergoing such a transition.

---

## B. Biological individuality / organismality pluralism

Core intellectual problem:

What counts as a biological individual, and do physiological, evolutionary, developmental, immunological, ecological, and lineage-based criteria always select the same entity?

Source families:

- Peter Godfrey-Smith — Darwinian populations and evolutionary individuality;
- Thomas Pradeu — immunology, biological identity, cohesion, delineation, persistence;
- Ellen Clarke and philosophy-of-biology work on biological individuality;
- Queller & Strassmann — organismality as cooperation/conflict structure;
- contemporary reviews distinguishing organismal from evolutionary-agential individuality.

Use for:

Tests 1, 2, 3, 5, 10 and terminology architecture.

Important limit:

The project must not imply that biology supplies one universally accepted definition of individuality.

---

## C. Perturbation / robustness / ablation / dependency

Core intellectual problem:

What happens when a candidate system is perturbed, disconnected, partially removed, or functionally degraded?

Relevant traditions:

- experimental perturbation in physiology and systems biology;
- lesion/ablation logic in neuroscience and developmental biology;
- ecological network perturbation and robustness;
- network-science node/edge removal and resilience;
- causal perturbation experiments.

Use for:

Tests 1 and 2, with support for Test 7.

Important limit:

Dependence on a network does not prove membership in one organism.

Ablation establishes functional dependency only relative to a specified intervention, outcome, timescale, and comparison model.

---

## D. Cooperation / conflict suppression / policing

Core intellectual problem:

How can a higher-level unit persist when lower-level components have partially divergent interests?

Source families:

- multilevel selection;
- kin selection and cooperation;
- policing/conflict mediation;
- germ-soma specialization;
- cancer/somatic conflict as an organism-level conflict problem;
- eusociality and major-transition literature.

Use for:

Test 3.

Important limit:

Governance, law, markets, norms, or conflict-management institutions are not biological conflict suppression by default.

The question is comparative: do candidate-level mechanisms create integration that discriminates a higher-order-individual model from organized multi-agent alternatives?

---

## E. Energetic organization / nonequilibrium thermodynamics

Core intellectual problem:

How is organized structure maintained through flows of energy and matter far from equilibrium?

Source families:

- biological thermodynamics;
- nonequilibrium statistical physics;
- metabolism and energetic constraints;
- ecological energetics;
- Earth-system/material-flow accounting where relevant.

Use for:

Test 4.

Important limit:

Nonequilibrium organization is widespread.

Energy throughput is therefore not a sufficient criterion for life or individuality.

The test asks whether energetic organization is meaningfully organized relative to the candidate boundary and whether that organization contributes discriminating evidence when combined with other tests.

---

## F. Persistence / turnover / memory

Core intellectual problem:

How can organization or information remain causally available despite replacement of the material components that previously carried it?

Source families:

- molecular/cellular memory;
- neural memory mechanisms;
- epigenetic persistence;
- developmental state persistence;
- immune memory;
- institutional/external record persistence;
- distributed cognition/external memory, used carefully;
- dynamical-systems state persistence.

Use for:

Test 5.

Important limit:

Persistent trace ≠ functional memory ≠ conscious recollection.

At civilization scale, archives and institutions can establish information persistence without establishing a global remembering subject.

---

## G. Anticipation / prediction / prospective performance

Core intellectual problem:

Can present information about recurring structure improve performance before an event is fully realized?

Source families:

- circadian biology;
- anticipatory regulation;
- predictive control;
- biological preparation/priming;
- forecasting and decision science;
- scoring rules and prospective prediction evaluation where appropriate.

Use for:

Test 6.

Important limit:

Preparation is not automatically prediction.

Prediction must be prospectively specified strongly enough to be wrong, and Prediction Advantage requires comparison with an appropriate reactive alternative.

Prediction does not imply consciousness.

---

## H. Repair / regeneration / autonomy

Core intellectual problem:

How does a system restore function or structure after perturbation, and at what organizational level is the repair process controlled?

Source families:

- regeneration biology;
- wound healing;
- developmental biology;
- tissue homeostasis;
- systems resilience;
- distributed infrastructure repair, as comparator rather than biological identity.

Use for:

Test 7.

Important limit:

Repair ≠ regeneration.

Rebuilding by intelligent lower-level agents ≠ autonomous repair by a higher-order body.

Restoration does not establish conscious target representation.

---

## I. Multivariate / relational / synergistic information

Core intellectual problem:

Can task-relevant information reside in relationships among components such that no single component alone contains the relevant predictive structure?

Source families:

- Shannon information theory as baseline;
- multivariate information theory;
- synergy/redundancy/unique-information research;
- partial information decomposition and related frameworks;
- network/relational representations;
- distributed computation.

Use for:

Test 8.

Important limit:

`Whole-only information` is not immaterial information floating above matter.

It must be physically instantiated in relations or joint states.

The project must not imply that partial information decomposition or any single multivariate-information formalism is settled enough to serve as the sole implementation of Test 8. The literature itself contains competing definitions and unresolved technical issues.

---

## J. Interventionist causation / higher-level causal variables

Core intellectual problem:

When is a macro-variable causally useful rather than merely a convenient redescription of coordinated lower-level changes?

Source families:

- James Woodward — interventionist causation;
- causal modeling traditions;
- philosophy of higher-level causation;
- debates over causal exclusion, proportionality, realization independence, and mixed-level models.

Use for:

Test 9.

Important limit:

The interventionist literature does **not** automatically vindicate every higher-level causal claim. There are live philosophical disputes about mixed-level causal models and causal exclusion.

Therefore Test 9 must be framed as a demanding model-comparison problem, not as `macro intervention succeeded, therefore macro entity exists.`

---

## K. Reproduction / heredity / evolutionary individuality

Core intellectual problem:

Does the candidate-level entity reproduce, transmit candidate-level organization, and participate in a lineage in a manner relevant to evolutionary individuality?

Source families:

- Lewontin-style conditions for evolution by natural selection;
- major evolutionary transitions;
- Godfrey-Smith on Darwinian populations;
- Michod on fitness transfer and evolutionary individuality;
- heredity/reproduction literature in philosophy of biology.

Use for:

Test 10.

Important limit:

Spread ≠ reproduction.

Cultural transmission ≠ candidate-level heredity by default.

Replication of institutions, cities, states, companies, or technical components does not establish reproduction of global civilization.

This remains one of the strongest current arguments against a strong civilization-level evolutionary-individual claim.

---

## L. Model competition / scientific inference

Core intellectual problem:

Does the stronger model predict, explain, or causally organize observations better than serious alternatives while paying an appropriate complexity cost?

Source families:

- statistical model selection;
- Akaike-style predictive model comparison;
- Bayesian model comparison;
- philosophy of scientific inference;
- explanatory competition;
- predictive accuracy, parsimony, and causal parameter recovery.

Use for:

Test 11 and the entire Book One burden-of-proof architecture.

Important limit:

Book One does not require every comparison to reduce to AIC, BIC, Bayes factors, or one universal numerical criterion.

`Model Competition` is broader: the candidate higher-order model must make some empirical difference—predictive, causal, compressive, intervention-guiding, or otherwise scientifically discriminating—that the strongest lower-level alternative does not already provide.

# TEST-BY-TEST GENEALOGY

# TEST 1 — BOUNDARY PERTURBATION

## Scientific ancestry

- biological individuality and organism boundaries;
- immunological delineation/cohesion;
- systems perturbation;
- ecological/network boundary dependence;
- intervention methodology.

## Core question

When flows, connections, or interactions across a preregistered candidate boundary are perturbed, does the candidate show reproducible boundary-relevant consequences that support treating that boundary as scientifically meaningful?

## What would count

- reproducible changes in candidate-level regulation or performance after controlled boundary perturbation;
- boundary behavior stable across multiple relevant classes of flow;
- predictions made before the perturbation;
- comparison against alternative boundary choices.

## What does not count

- drawing a boundary after observing the outcome;
- choosing a different boundary for each test to maximize support;
- mere geographical enclosure;
- simple connectivity.

## Primary source burden

High.

Final endnotes should include biological-individuality/boundary literature plus perturbation methodology.

---

# TEST 2 — INTEGRATION ABLATION

## Scientific ancestry

- lesion/ablation logic;
- systems biology;
- network robustness;
- major transitions / interdependence;
- functional dependency analysis.

## Core question

Does removal or disruption of a candidate subsystem produce a reproducible loss of candidate-level function beyond what the strongest distributed-network alternative predicts?

## What would count

- preregistered functional outcomes;
- graded ablations;
- recovery curves;
- subsystem controls;
- comparison with ordinary network dependency.

## What does not count

- `the internet went down and people were inconvenienced`;
- dependency alone;
- critical infrastructure importance without evidence of higher-order integration.

## Source burden

High.

---

# TEST 3 — CONFLICT SUPPRESSION

## Scientific ancestry

- cooperation theory;
- multilevel selection;
- major transitions;
- policing/conflict mediation;
- organismality.

## Core question

Do candidate-level mechanisms suppress or reorganize lower-level conflict strongly enough to produce integration that the organized-multi-agent model does not already explain?

## What would count

- measurable reduction of destructive lower-level competition tied to candidate persistence;
- conflict-control mechanisms whose failure predicts candidate-level dysfunction;
- comparison across systems with different degrees of conflict mediation.

## What does not count

- existence of laws;
- political authority;
- temporary coordination;
- majority rule;
- absence of visible conflict during one interval.

## Source burden

Very high because this test connects directly to established major-transition theory.

---

# TEST 4 — ENERGETIC ORGANIZATION

## Scientific ancestry

- nonequilibrium thermodynamics;
- metabolism/energetics;
- ecological material-energy flows;
- system boundary accounting.

## Core question

Does the candidate exhibit stable, measurable organization of energy/material throughput relative to a specified boundary in a way that contributes to its persistence and distinguishes it from arbitrary aggregation?

## What would count

- explicit boundary;
- measured fluxes;
- coupling between energetic organization and candidate-level persistence;
- perturbation response;
- comparison with alternative aggregations.

## What does not count

- `it consumes energy`;
- global energy use by itself;
- metaphorical metabolism.

## Source burden

High.

---

# TEST 5 — MEMORY TURNOVER

## Scientific ancestry

- biological memory;
- physical trace/persistence;
- turnover-resistant organization;
- distributed/external memory;
- information storage and causal reuse.

## Core question

Can information relevant to candidate organization, identity, or behavior remain causally available after substantial turnover of the components that originally carried it?

## What would count

- identifiable physical carrier(s);
- documented component turnover;
- later causal reuse;
- altered response attributable to retained state;
- controls distinguishing persistence from repeated re-creation.

## What does not count

- a surviving record never used again;
- archives existing somewhere;
- persistence without demonstrated causal availability;
- conscious-memory language.

## Source burden

High.

---

# TEST 6 — PREDICTION ADVANTAGE

## Scientific ancestry

- circadian anticipation;
- predictive control;
- prospective forecasting;
- anticipatory regulation;
- performance comparison.

## Core question

Does advance preparation based on present information about recurring structure improve performance compared with an otherwise appropriate system that reacts only after the event occurs?

## What would count

- prospective prediction;
- defined event/outcome;
- reactive comparator;
- scoring before outcome;
- error/cost when expected event fails to occur.

## What does not count

- retrospective explanation;
- generic readiness;
- adaptation with no prospective commitment;
- prophecy language.

## Source burden

High.

---

# TEST 7 — REPAIR AUTONOMY

## Scientific ancestry

- regeneration;
- wound repair;
- homeostasis;
- resilience;
- distributed repair systems.

## Core question

After damage, how much restoration is initiated, coordinated, and terminated by processes internal to the candidate-level organization rather than by independently directed lower-level agents or external repairers?

## What would count

- damage detection;
- coordinated restoration;
- candidate-level performance recovery;
- termination when appropriate;
- reduced reliance on external intelligent direction.

## What does not count

- humans deciding to rebuild a bridge;
- replacement of broken hardware by technicians;
- recovery produced entirely by outside intervention.

## Source burden

Very high because `autonomy` must be operationalized carefully.

---

# TEST 8 — WHOLE-ONLY INFORMATION

## Scientific ancestry

- multivariate information;
- relational information;
- synergy;
- distributed computation;
- network state representations.

## Core question

Does the state or relationship of the proposed whole provide reproducible task-relevant information that cannot be recovered from any single component alone and that improves prediction of system behavior beyond appropriate component-only models?

## What would count

- explicit target variable;
- joint-state or relational information;
- comparison with single-component and fair lower-level models;
- out-of-sample predictive improvement;
- physical implementation identified.

## What does not count

- saying `the whole is more than the sum of its parts`;
- any network statistic;
- a pattern visible only because the analyst aggregated the data;
- treating information as nonphysical.

## Source burden

Very high.

Final implementation should acknowledge that multivariate synergy measures remain an active technical research area.

---

# TEST 9 — HIGHER-LEVEL INTERVENTION

## Scientific ancestry

- interventionist causation;
- causal modeling;
- multilevel causation;
- causal exclusion/proportionality debates.

## Core question

Can a well-defined intervention on a candidate-level variable improve causal prediction or control in a way not reducible to merely renaming an arbitrary bundle of component interventions?

## What would count

- explicit macro-variable;
- intervention semantics;
- predicted downstream effect;
- lower-level comparison model;
- robustness across multiple realizations where appropriate.

## What does not count

- changing thousands of components and calling the bundle `the whole`;
- observational correlation;
- a useful summary variable with no causal leverage.

## Source burden

Extremely high.

This is philosophically contested territory and must be written accordingly.

---

# TEST 10 — REPRODUCTION/HEREDITY

## Scientific ancestry

- Darwinian/evolutionary individuality;
- major transitions;
- lineage formation;
- reproduction;
- heredity;
- heritable fitness variation.

## Core question

Does the proposed whole produce descendant wholes and transmit candidate-level organization in a manner that supports lineage-level evolutionary analysis?

## What would count

- operational candidate-level parent/offspring relation;
- candidate-level trait transmission;
- variation among descendant wholes;
- persistence across generations;
- possible selection at candidate level.

## What does not count

- colonization;
- copying institutions;
- cultural diffusion;
- reproduction of humans inside civilization;
- replication of machines;
- expansion into new territory.

## Source burden

Extremely high.

Current civilization-level state remains weak/not established.

---

# TEST 11 — MODEL COMPETITION

## Scientific ancestry

- statistical model comparison;
- scientific inference;
- prediction versus accommodation;
- parsimony/complexity penalties;
- causal model comparison;
- philosophy of explanation.

## Core question

Does Model D make a scientifically meaningful empirical contribution that Model C and other serious lower-level alternatives do not already provide?

## What would count

- preregistered novel prediction;
- better out-of-sample prediction;
- intervention guidance unavailable to C;
- stable compression/generalization across cases;
- explanatory unification tied to measurable success;
- evidence that cannot be recovered by a fair C-level model.

## What does not count

- emotional coherence;
- metaphorical elegance;
- renaming Model C phenomena;
- fitting observations after the fact;
- adding complexity without empirical gain.

## Source burden

Extremely high.

This is the final gate and the reason Model C currently wins.

# TEST DEPENDENCE MAP

The Eleven Tests must not be treated as eleven independent votes.

Likely dependence clusters include:

## Cluster A — boundary / integration / repair

Tests 1, 2, and 7 can share mechanisms.

A stable boundary can affect ablation response and repair organization.

## Cluster B — conflict / reproduction / evolutionary individuality

Tests 3 and 10 are deeply connected in major-transition theory.

Conflict mediation can enable candidate-level reproduction/heredity and fitness alignment.

## Cluster C — memory / prediction

Tests 5 and 6 can share retained physical state.

Prediction may depend on memory, but memory does not imply prediction.

## Cluster D — whole information / higher-level intervention / model competition

Tests 8, 9, and 11 are tightly related.

A useful macro-variable may improve prediction, intervention, and model comparison simultaneously.

These should not be counted as three independent confirmations if one underlying representation produces all three effects.

## Cluster E — energetic organization as enabling condition

Test 4 can support persistence, repair, and integration without independently identifying organismhood.

### Mandatory rule

> **DEPENDENT TESTS MAY STRENGTHEN A MECHANISTIC STORY WITHOUT MULTIPLYING THE EVIDENCE AS IF EACH WERE AN INDEPENDENT OBSERVATION.**

# NECESSITY / SUFFICIENCY RULE

Book One must not claim:

- every true biological individual must pass all Eleven Tests in exactly the same way;
- passing any fixed number proves organismhood;
- failing one test universally disproves every kind of biological individuality;
- the framework is taxonomically exhaustive.

Instead:

> The tests are discriminating dimensions selected to stress a proposed higher-order-individual model. Their relevance and operationalization can vary with the kind of individuality being claimed.

Test 10 carries special weight when the claim is specifically **evolutionary individuality**.

Tests 1–9 can remain relevant to organismal/functional individuality even where reproduction is not the immediate research question.

Test 11 is always required because the stronger model must compete with alternatives.

# CLAIM-TO-SOURCE RESOLUTION ARCHITECTURE

Every factual/scientific claim in the final manuscript should receive one of six source classes.

## S1 — PRIMARY EMPIRICAL

Original experimental, observational, or dataset paper directly supporting the claim.

Preferred for:

- specific biological mechanisms;
- regeneration examples;
- bioelectric findings;
- circadian/prediction examples;
- quantitative claims;
- specific system behavior.

## S2 — AUTHORITATIVE REVIEW / SYNTHESIS

Peer-reviewed review, National Academies synthesis, major scholarly review, or equivalent.

Preferred for:

- broad field summaries;
- accepted mechanism overview;
- historical synthesis;
- multiple-example statements.

## S3 — FOUNDATIONAL THEORY

Original theoretical work or canonical scholarly treatment.

Preferred for:

- major evolutionary transitions;
- evolutionary individuality;
- interventionist causation;
- model-selection theory;
- information-theoretic constructs.

## S4 — PHILOSOPHY / CONCEPTUAL ANALYSIS

Peer-reviewed philosophy of biology/science or scholarly monograph.

Preferred for:

- organism/individual distinctions;
- causal levels;
- identity/boundary questions;
- model competition;
- explanatory claims.

## S5 — AUTHORITATIVE INSTITUTIONAL / TECHNICAL

Government, intergovernmental, standards, scientific agency, or technical institutional documentation.

Preferred for:

- WMO observing architecture;
- infrastructure descriptions;
- official technical systems;
- standardized definitions where appropriate.

## S6 — AUTHOR SYNTHESIS / HYPOTHESIS

Claims that are explicitly the book's proposed framework, inference, metaphor, or research question.

These must not be disguised as sourced consensus.

Examples:

- the Eleven Tests as a set;
- Models A–D as this book's comparison architecture;
- the Macroscopic Life Hypothesis formulation;
- `We Are the Microbe` perspective device.

S6 claims may cite source families that motivate them, but the text must make clear that the synthesis itself is ours.

# SOURCE QUALITY HIERARCHY

For final publication sourcing, prefer in this order where appropriate:

1. primary peer-reviewed empirical paper;
2. authoritative peer-reviewed review/meta-analysis;
3. canonical theoretical paper/book from academic press;
4. National Academies / scientific society / major scientific institution;
5. high-quality scholarly secondary source.

Avoid using popular articles as the evidentiary foundation for central scientific claims.

Popular sources may be useful only for historical/public context when the underlying scientific claim is independently sourced.

---

# ADVERSARIAL SOURCE RULES

## Rule 1 — No citation laundering

A review citing an old claim does not automatically prove the claim is current consensus.

## Rule 2 — No source stretching

If a paper shows a mechanism in planaria, do not cite it for a universal claim about all regeneration.

## Rule 3 — No taxonomic transfer without support

Evidence in one species does not automatically transfer to another.

## Rule 4 — No scale transfer without argument

Evidence that a mechanism exists in cells does not establish that an analogous mechanism exists in civilization.

## Rule 5 — No terminology transfer

If two fields use `memory`, `prediction`, `information`, `network`, `boundary`, or `repair`, do not assume the same operational definition.

## Rule 6 — Negative evidence matters

Where the literature contains counterexamples, limitations, failed replications, or competing theories, the final source map should record them.

## Rule 7 — Prefer direct support over prestige

A famous paper that is only tangentially relevant is weaker than a less famous paper directly supporting the sentence.

## Rule 8 — Date matters when fields move

Use current reviews for active literatures while retaining foundational works for genealogy.

## Rule 9 — Philosophy claims require philosophy sources

Do not cite empirical biology as if it resolves conceptual questions about causation or individuality.

## Rule 10 — Our synthesis must remain ours

Do not make a citation appear to endorse the Eleven Tests as a package unless a source actually does—which is not currently claimed.
