# CLASSIFICATION SYSTEM

Every recurring brake is assigned one of four editorial classes.

## FOUNDATIONAL

The first decisive statement of a distinction or a later statement required because the evidentiary burden has materially changed.

**Rule: retain.**

## CALLBACK

A shortened reminder of an already established distinction needed because the reader has entered a new domain where the old mistake could recur.

**Rule: compress, but retain the concept.**

## REDUNDANT

A repetition that does not add a new mechanism, evidentiary distinction, boundary condition, or model-comparison consequence.

**Rule: candidate for deletion.**

## ESCALATING

A statement that resembles an earlier firewall but actually imposes a stronger requirement at a later stage.

**Rule: retain and make the escalation clear.**

# TERMINOLOGY MAP — CANONICAL DEFINITIONS

These definitions govern the production edit.

They are not intended to replace field-specific technical definitions in citations; they control how this book uses the terms.

## PATTERN

A reproducible or describable regularity at a chosen scale.

A pattern may arise without a bounded or integrated system.

**Pattern does not imply organization.**

## ORGANIZATION

Relationships among components that causally contribute to reproducible system-level states, functions, constraints, or dynamics.

Organization can be real without individuality.

## MACROSCOPIC ORGANIZATION

Organization scientifically useful at a scale above the focal components being studied.

In this book, the term is deliberately broader than organismhood.

**Macroscopic organization can be real without macroscopic individuality.**

## INTEGRATION

The degree to which relationships among components make system behavior dependent on their coordinated or constrained interaction.

Integration is dimensional, not automatically binary.

**Integration does not by itself establish individuality.**

## INDIVIDUAL

A context-dependent scientific unit treated as a coherent entity for a specified biological or explanatory purpose.

Because biology contains multiple individuality concepts, the word must not silently carry every possible criterion at once.

## BIOLOGICAL INDIVIDUAL

A living unit that satisfies a scientifically specified set of organismal, physiological, developmental, immunological, ecological, evolutionary, or other relevant individuality criteria.

The exact criteria depend on the research question.

Do not use as an undefined synonym for `organism` in contested cases.

## EVOLUTIONARY INDIVIDUAL

A unit for which reproduction, heritable variation in fitness, selection, conflict mediation, and related evolutionary criteria make higher-level evolutionary individuality scientifically meaningful.

This is especially relevant to Test 10.

Do not assume every physiologically integrated entity is an evolutionary individual.

## ORGANISM

A familiar biological category generally involving substantial physiological/developmental organization and functional integration, but not reducible in this book to one universal checklist.

Use confidently for ordinary uncontroversial organisms.

Use cautiously for contested collectives.

## ORGANISMALITY

The degree or set of properties by which a biological system exhibits organism-like integration.

If used, make clear whether the source literature treats organismality as graded and which dimensions are being measured.

## HIGHER-ORDER INDIVIDUAL

A proposed individual whose scientifically relevant individuality exists at an organizational scale above the focal lower-level components.

This term does **not** automatically mean conscious, intelligent, evolutionary, or animal-like.

## MACROSCOPIC LIFE

The project's umbrella research question concerning whether sufficiently integrated life-like or individuality-relevant organization can exist or emerge at scales above familiar organisms.

In Book One, `Macroscopic Life` is **not** a diagnosis that civilization is alive.

## SYSTEM-LEVEL FUNCTION

A reproducible capacity, state transition, or performance property attributable to the organized system and not necessarily possessed by any isolated component.

A system-level function does not establish individuality.

## EMERGENCE

A relationship in which system-level properties or descriptions become relevant because of component interactions and organization.

In this book:

> **EMERGENCE NAMES A LEVEL RELATION. IT DOES NOT BY ITSELF EXPLAIN THE MECHANISM.**

## INFORMATION

A physically instantiated distinction, state, relationship, or structure that is relevant to a defined inference, prediction, control, or causal process.

When information is claimed to do work in the argument, identify a physical carrier and causal pathway.

## MEMORY

Past-dependent physical organization that remains causally available and alters later response under a specified operational definition.

Do not equate automatically with conscious recollection.

## PREDICTION

A prospective commitment to an expected future state, event, timing, or probability distribution sufficiently specified that later outcomes can count for or against it.

Prediction does not require consciousness.

## INTELLIGENCE

The capacity of a system to use information to select, modify, or discover actions or internal states that improve performance across a defined class of problems or changing conditions.

Intelligence does not automatically imply agency or consciousness.

## AGENCY

The capacity attributed to a system to select or regulate actions in relation to states, goals, constraints, or policies under a specified operational framework.

Do not infer agency merely from complex output.

## CONSCIOUSNESS

Subjective experience or awareness in whatever scientifically specified sense is relevant to a claim.

Book One does not require consciousness for higher-order individuality and does not claim civilization-scale consciousness.

## BOUNDARY

A scientifically specified distinction between candidate system and environment for a particular class of material, energetic, informational, causal, developmental, ecological, or other interactions.

Boundaries may be multidimensional and permeable.

A candidate cannot change boundary opportunistically to rescue every failed test.

## HOMEOSTASIS / REGULATION

Processes that maintain, restore, or constrain variables relative to viable states or ranges.

Do not equate governance or coordination with biological homeostasis without mechanistic justification.

## REPAIR

Processes that restore damaged function, structure, or organization.

Repair is broader than regeneration.

## REGENERATION

Restoration of biological pattern or structure after loss or damage through regulated developmental/repair processes.

Rebuilding by external agents is not regeneration by default.

## REPRODUCTION

Production of a new candidate-level entity under an operationally specified process.

Spread, copying of information, institutional replication, and component reproduction do not automatically constitute reproduction of the proposed whole.

## HEREDITY

Transmission of candidate-level traits or organization across reproduction in a form relevant to variation and persistence across generations.

Cultural transmission alone does not automatically establish civilization-level heredity.

# TERMINOLOGY PROHIBITIONS

During line editing, do not silently substitute:

- `organism` for `organization`;
- `alive` for `integrated`;
- `mind` for `distributed cognition`;
- `memory` for any persistent trace;
- `prediction` for any preparation;
- `goal` for any stable outcome;
- `knows` for any regulatory response;
- `wants` for any optimization process;
- `nervous system` as literal identity where only functional comparison is intended;
- `metabolism` as literal identity where only material/energy throughput is established;
- `reproduction` for spread;
- `heredity` for cultural persistence;
- `consciousness` for intelligence;
- `emergence` as an explanation.

Anthropomorphic language may be used narratively only when immediately clear as metaphor and not doing scientific work.

# SAFE COMPRESSION RULES

A later editor may cut a firewall only if all five conditions are satisfied:

1. the same scientific distinction has already been stated clearly;
2. the deleted passage introduces no new mechanism;
3. the deleted passage introduces no new failure condition;
4. the deleted passage introduces no new domain-specific misuse that must be blocked;
5. the surrounding prose cannot reasonably be read as making the prohibited inference after the deletion.

If any condition fails, keep or rewrite rather than delete.
