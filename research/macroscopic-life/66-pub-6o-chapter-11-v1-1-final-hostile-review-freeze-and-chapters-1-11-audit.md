## P1-2 — Circadian free-running precision

**RESOLVED.**

**RESOLVED.**

Feedforward is now explicitly framed as comparative control language: a presently measured or estimated variable related to an upcoming demand/disturbance alters control action without waiting for resulting output error.

Locked brake:

> **FEEDFORWARD ≠ FUTURE CAUSATION**

## P1-4 — Test 6 performance metric

**RESOLVED.**

Performance must now be defined prospectively rather than selected after observing the result.

Locked methodological rule:

> **PREDICT THE OUTCOME AND DEFINE THE SCORE BEFORE YOU SEE THE RESULT.**

## P1-5 — Overbroad category inventory

**RESOLVED.**

# 4. TEST 6 FINAL LOCK

Canonical name:

**TEST 6 — PREDICTION ADVANTAGE**

Canonical operational question:

> **Does advance preparation based on present information about recurring structure improve performance compared with an otherwise appropriate system that reacts only after the event occurs?**

Minimum burden established by Chapter 11:

1. define event/recurring condition;
2. identify present cue/state;
3. identify physical carrier;
4. specify causal mechanism to preparation;
5. define appropriate reactive baseline;
6. define performance metric prospectively;
7. commit prediction before outcome;
8. permit measurable error/cost;
9. perturb proposed cue/clock/model/pathway;
10. retest;
11. compare against simpler models.

Positive Test 6 establishes a performance advantage in the tested context.

It does **not** by itself establish:

- consciousness;
- foresight;
- agency;
- organismhood;
- biological individuality;
- a higher-order individual.

# 9. THE MAJOR BURDEN SHIFT AFTER ACT III

At the beginning of the book, a skeptical reader could reasonably object:

> How could a larger organism exist if its components do not know they are part of it?

After Acts I–III, that objection is no longer strong.

Cells do not need to comprehend a human organism for a human organism to exist.

Another objection:

> How could an individual persist if its components are replaced?

That is no longer sufficient either.

Persistence depends on what organizational information survives and remains causally active, not merely on whether every component remains physically identical.

Another objection:

> How could a large system act coherently without a central controller?

Development, physiology, regeneration, neural systems, immune systems, and other distributed biological processes show that centralized homunculus-style control is not a prerequisite for coordination.

But eliminating weak objections does not establish the stronger hypothesis.

The burden now shifts.

From Act IV forward, the question becomes:

> **What positive evidence would distinguish a genuinely higher-order cognitive or biological individual from an extraordinarily complicated network of lower-level agents?**

That is the correct place for the book to become harder.
