# Macroscopic Life — Build Pass 23: The Agency Problem

## Governing question

**When are we justified in saying that the system acted, rather than merely saying that things happened inside the system?**

This pass separates causation, response, intelligence, agency, autonomy, consciousness, and moral agency.

- ML-878–879: Agency is graded and domain-specific; use a Macroscopic Agency Map.
- ML-880: Controlled action selection is the threshold from a system that processes the world to a system that can do something about it.

## Permanent Agency Doctrine

1. Cause ≠ agent.
2. Response ≠ agency.
3. Intelligence ≠ agency.
4. Agency ≠ consciousness.
5. Agency ≠ metaphysical free will.
6. Functional agency ≠ moral agency.
7. Planetary impact ≠ planetary agency.
8. Distributed control does not require a central executive, but distributed dynamics alone do not establish agency.
9. Attribute agency upward only when system-level organization materially improves explanation or prediction.
10. More integration is not inherently morally better.

## Pass synthesis

**When persistent system-level organization uses information to select among alternative actions, coordinates the components or effectors required to implement that selection, evaluates consequences through feedback, and produces behavioral regularities that are more intelligible at the system level than as an accidental aggregate of component actions, attribution of functional agency to the higher-order system becomes increasingly justified.**

Civilization may already possess enormous collective intelligence without possessing comparably unified collective agency. The problem is not simply whether humanity can think; it is whether distributed knowledge can become coherent action.
