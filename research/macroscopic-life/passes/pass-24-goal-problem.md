# Macroscopic Life — Build Pass 24: The Goal Problem

## Governing question

**When does a system merely move toward a stable state, and when is it genuinely organizing the present around a possible future?**

This pass separates attractors, regulation, function, goals, represented goals, intention, and purpose.

## Claims ML-881–ML-996

- ML-881–884: Goal language must be typed; attractor ≠ goal; set-point regulation is stronger than passive attraction but weaker than rich goal-directed agency.
- ML-885–891: Biological functional goals can exist without consciousness; viability regions, homeostasis, allostasis, and future-sensitive control are distinct.
- ML-892–897: Functional goals vs represented goals; representation must be earned; function ≠ intention; teleonomy is safer than teleology; evolution requires no forward plan.
- ML-898–906: Learned, assigned, self-generated goals; goal formation/revision/hierarchy; Goal Half-Life and Supra-Component Goal Persistence.
- ML-907–913: Institutional goals, revealed goals, Goal Identifiability Problem, perturbation-based testing, Defended-Variable Test.
- ML-914–923: Target plasticity, goal substitution, means–ends separation, instrumental convergence, self-preservation as possible instrumental goal, institutional survival, goal drift, and identity without goal constancy.
- ML-924–929: Cross-scale goal conflict; higher-level goals need not be component goals; component ignorance; distributed physical instantiation of system-level goals.
- ML-930–936: Avoid single-objective caricatures; biological systems are multi-objective and face tradeoffs; Viability-First Hypothesis; reproduction complicates organismal viability; always ask “goal of which system?”
- ML-937–943: Colony functional goal-directedness; civilization as Goal Ecology; goal alignment can be evolved or engineered but is never perfect.
- ML-944–950: Proxy failure, Goodhart-type problems, pathological goals, artificial objectives, reward ≠ desire, and desire as a separate phenomenological category.
- ML-951–958: Purpose is stronger than goal; distinguish designed purpose, biological function, personal purpose, and cosmic purpose; no cosmic teleology from complexity; evolution is not inevitable progress; higher ≠ better.
- ML-959–964: Future Depth Ladder: reactive → anticipatory → predictive → planning → counterfactual → normative. Civilization has high distributed future modeling but weak unified future selection.
- ML-965–978: Macroscopic Goal Integration, Goal Integration Index (GII), Goal Architecture Matrix, calibration across humans/colonies/corporations/internet/ecosystems/Earth/civilization, crisis-driven Goal Synchronization, episodic vs persistent collective agency.
- ML-979–986: Developmental targets, pattern regulation, morphological target states, regeneration as goal-test laboratory, local cells/global anatomy, morphogenesis as proof that local components can participate in global form without conscious comprehension.
- ML-987: Macroscopic Goal Hypothesis: higher-order functional agency strengthens when distributed components regulate toward persistent system-level target states and compensate for perturbations, substitute means, resolve conflict, and preserve targets through component turnover.
- ML-988–990: Goal falsifiers; goals must predict counterfactual behavior; purpose cannot become an escape hatch.
- ML-991: Permanent Goal Doctrine.
- ML-992–996: Attractor/regulation/function/goal/represented-goal/agency/intention/purpose are separated; Macroscopic Life becomes a multidimensional coordinate system; civilization/body profiles diverge; core research question becomes what combinations of integration, self-maintenance, goal regulation, agency, heredity, and scale suffice for a new individual.

## Permanent Goal Doctrine

1. Attractor ≠ goal.
2. Goal ≠ intention.
3. Function ≠ consciousness.
4. Evolved function ≠ foresight.
5. Assigned goals can still permit agency.
6. Self-generated goals represent richer autonomy.
7. Goal attribution must identify a defended variable or future state.
8. Perturbation should produce predicted compensatory behavior.
9. System-level goals must be physically instantiated in control architecture.
10. Cosmic purpose cannot be inferred from biological organization.

## Pass synthesis

**Goal-directedness becomes scientifically meaningful when a system persistently regulates toward identifiable states and produces predictable compensatory behavior when those states are disturbed. Richer agency appears when the system can represent possible futures, vary the means used to reach them, resolve competing objectives, and revise its own goals.**

Development and regeneration now become a critical bridge: local cells can participate in construction and restoration of global anatomy without any requirement that individual cells consciously comprehend the whole. This leads directly into developmental bioelectricity.
