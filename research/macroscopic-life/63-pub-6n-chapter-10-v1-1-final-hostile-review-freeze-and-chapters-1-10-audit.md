# CHAPTERS 1–10 — CUMULATIVE EARNED STAIRCASE

The book has now earned the following sequence without requiring acceptance of the Macroscopic Life hypothesis.

## Chapter 1

Embedded components can have incomplete direct access to the larger system in which they participate.

This is a possibility statement, not proof that humans occupy an analogous relation.

## Chapter 2

Unaided human perception samples only a subset of physically measurable reality.

Sensory limitation does not establish hidden organisms or unsupported phenomena.

## Chapter 3

Temporal mismatch can conceal structure and dynamics from ordinary observation.

Slow or fast change does not establish life, intelligence, or agency.

## Chapter 4

Known multicellular organisms demonstrate genuine nested biological organization: living cells can participate in a higher-order biological individual.

This does not mean every collection of organisms forms another organism.

## Chapter 5

Biological individuality itself can evolve under particular conditions.

Cooperation alone is insufficient.

## Chapter 6

Biological boundaries can be multidimensional, permeable, and process-dependent without becoming meaningless.

Boundary ambiguity alone is not evidence for a larger individual.

## Chapter 7

Local physical mechanisms, including bioelectric processes, can participate in organism-level regulation without any individual cell representing the whole organism.

Electrical coordination does not establish consciousness, hidden life force, or higher-order organismhood.

## Chapter 8

Distributed local interactions can generate reproducible large-scale anatomical form without any component containing a complete explicit representation of the finished body.

Self-organization does not establish life, intelligence, agency, consciousness, or a universal morphogenetic field.

## Chapter 9

After perturbation, biological systems can in some cases restore characteristic organization through distributed, physically instantiated regulatory processes.

Perturbation can reveal what is actively maintained, but restoration alone does not establish explicit target representation, consciousness, or higher-order individuality.

## Chapter 10

Past events can leave physically instantiated states that remain causally relevant to later behavior or organization, including in systems without conscious recollection.

Functionally relevant information can in some cases remain available through component turnover, but persistence through turnover does not determine which level owns the memory and does not establish higher-order organismhood.
