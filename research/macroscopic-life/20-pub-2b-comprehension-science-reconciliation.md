## Audit rules

Every explanation was tested for six failure modes:

1. Does the simplification become false?
2. Does an analogy accidentally become a mechanism?
3. Does functional language imply consciousness or intention?
4. Does a higher-level capability get assigned to a larger system merely because its components possess it?
5. Does an example overgeneralize beyond the organisms or mechanisms for which evidence exists?
6. Does the explanation hide scientific disagreement that matters to the argument?

# Chapter-by-chapter reconciliation

## Chapter 1 — The Microbe

### PASS

The intelligent-cell thought experiment remains properly bounded as an epistemic device rather than evidence.

### Correction 1A — Evolution definition

Current accessibility definition is broadly sound but should distinguish evolution from natural selection more explicitly at first use.

Preferred replacement:

> **Evolution** is change in inherited characteristics of populations across generations. Natural selection is one process that can cause evolutionary change, alongside mutation, genetic drift, gene flow, recombination, and other processes.

Reason: avoids making `selection, chance, and other processes` sound like equivalent informal forces without naming the population-level definition.

### Correction 1B — Individuality language

`Evolutionary history gives us strong examples that individuality has changed scale before` survives, but should be immediately followed by:

> Biologists disagree about exactly how individuality should be defined across all cases, so this book treats individuality as a set of testable properties rather than a single magical switch.

This prepares the reader for pluralistic individuality without specialist vocabulary.

## Chapter 5 — When Parts Become a Whole

### PASS WITH IMPORTANT REPAIRS

The chapter's conflict-centered treatment is supported by evolutionary-transition literature. Conflict mediation is central in many transitions, but the manuscript should not imply one universal transition recipe.

### Correction 5A — Major transitions

Replace any implication of one canonical mechanism with:

> Evolutionary history includes multiple transitions in which entities that once reproduced or persisted with greater independence became incorporated into more integrated higher-level systems. The mechanisms, life cycles, and degree of individuality differ among cases.

### Correction 5B — Reproductive bottleneck

Current explanation is good but `align much of the organism's ancestry` can be sharpened:

> Development from a single cell or small propagule can increase genetic similarity among the cells of the resulting organism and reduce opportunities for competing cell lineages to be transmitted independently across generations.

### Correction 5C — Levels of selection

Keep the locked sentence:

> Selection at a level is evidence that the level matters evolutionarily. It is not, by itself, proof that the level is one organism.

Add an eighth-grade bridge:

> Something can matter to evolution as a group without automatically becoming one biological body.

### Ant-colony audit

PASS if `superorganism` remains limited to sufficiently integrated eusocial colonies rather than all social insects.

## Chapter 6 — Where Does the Individual End?

### PASS WITH EXTENDED-PHENOTYPE REPAIR

### Correction 6A — Extended phenotype

The beaver-dam explanation risks making the extended phenotype sound like any environmental modification.

Preferred:

> The term **extended phenotype** is used for effects of an organism's genes that extend beyond its body through behavior or constructed environmental features. A beaver dam is a familiar illustration of how genetically influenced behavior can produce structures outside the animal that affect survival and reproduction.

The dam is not literally beaver tissue and does not automatically belong inside the organismal boundary.

### Boundary framework

PASS as an analytical framework. The five boundaries are a book-level decomposition, not a claim that biology recognizes exactly five official boundary types.

Add once:

> These five categories are an analytical tool used in this book, not an official universal biological classification.

## Chapter 7 — The Body Electric

### PASS WITH THE MOST IMPORTANT PHYSICS CORRECTION

The chapter is accessible and broadly accurate, and current reviews support non-neural bioelectric roles in morphogenesis while emphasizing that the field is comparatively less understood than neural electrophysiology.

### Correction 7A — Ions

Current: `An atom or molecule that has gained or lost electrons...`

Better:

> An atom or molecule with a net electric charge is called an **ion**. This often happens when electrons are gained or lost, though ions can also be formed or rearranged through chemical reactions involving charged groups.

Reason: avoids teaching an overly narrow ion-formation story.

### Correction 7B — Membrane potential

Current wording can imply bulk charge separation across the entire cell.

Preferred:

> A very small separation of electric charge across the thin cell membrane creates an electrical potential difference called the **membrane potential**. The fluids inside and outside the cell remain nearly electrically neutral overall.

This is an important scientific correction.

### Correction 7C — Dissipation

Replace `Some energy becomes spread out, often as heat, and is no longer available to perform the same work` with:

> During real physical processes, useful free energy is transformed and some energy is dispersed, often as heat. Energy is conserved, but not all of it remains available to perform the same kind of work.

Reason: prevents an eighth-grade reader from hearing `energy disappears.`

### Correction 7D — Bioelectricity scope

Add:

> Bioelectric state is one part of developmental and physiological regulation. It interacts with biochemical signals, genes, mechanical forces, tissue geometry, and environmental conditions; it is not a master code replacing those mechanisms.

This preserves the strongest HARDEN-5 boundary.

### Wound-electric-field audit

PASS. Endogenous wound electric fields and electrotaxis are established research phenomena, while their importance varies by tissue and context.

## Chapter 10 — Memory Without a Brain

### PASS WITH TERMINOLOGY GUARDRAIL

### Correction 10A — Memory definition

Current functional definition is good, but add:

> This is a deliberately broad functional definition used for comparison. Different scientific fields use the word `memory` more narrowly in different contexts.

### Correction 10B — Epigenetics

Avoid defining epigenetics so broadly that every gene-regulatory state qualifies.

Preferred:

> **Epigenetic regulation** refers to changes in gene activity or cellular state associated with mechanisms such as chromatin organization and chemical modification of DNA or DNA-associated proteins, without requiring a change in the underlying DNA sequence. Scientists use the term in several related ways.

### Correction 10C — Immune memory

Replace `some immune cells and molecules can persist or change` with:

> Following some infections or vaccinations, long-lived immune cells and altered populations of immune cells can support faster or stronger responses during later exposure. Antibodies may also persist for varying periods.

Reason: distinguishes persistent molecules from memory-cell mechanisms.

### Supra-component memory

Keep clearly labeled as book terminology.

## Chapter 14 — Is Civilization Becoming an Individual?

### PASS — CENTRAL CONCLUSION SURVIVES

The property/evidence split is one of v0.4's strongest scientific safeguards.

### Correction 14A — `Matter coordination strong`

The existence of global material flows is strong. Calling them coordinated should be carefully bounded because many are emergent consequences of markets and local decisions.

Preferred label:

> **Large-scale matter flow:** strong. **Unified system-level coordination:** mixed.

### Correction 14B — Energy

Same distinction:

> **Large-scale energy transformation and distribution:** strong. **Evidence for civilization-level metabolism:** weak.

### Correction 14C — Information

Change `Whole-only information can genuinely emerge` to:

> Distributed systems can construct integrated information states that no individual contributor possessed beforehand.

Then retain ambiguity over whether that capability belongs to civilization as one agent.

### Correction 14D — Persistence

Civilization's persistence through human turnover is strong as historical organization. It is only moderate evidence for individuality because institutions, languages, and cultures can persist without being organisms.

### Final rating

Keep:

> Civilization currently fits **macroscopic organization / cognitive ecology** substantially better than **higher-order biological individual**.
