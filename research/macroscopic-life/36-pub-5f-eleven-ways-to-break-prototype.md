# 4. TEST 1 — BOUNDARY PERTURBATION

### Question

Does the proposed higher-order system possess a meaningful boundary expressed through measurable asymmetry rather than a circle chosen by the observer?

### Change / measure

Perturb interactions across candidate inside/outside boundaries and compare them with interactions within the candidate system.

### Strengthens

Consistent, causally important differences in coupling, regulation, information exchange, or constraint across a historically persistent candidate boundary.

### Weakens

No meaningful inside/outside asymmetry, or boundary placement changes arbitrarily without affecting explanation/prediction.

Brake:

> **A boundary earns meaning through measurable asymmetry, not because we drew a circle.**

---

# 5. TEST 2 — INTEGRATION ABLATION

### Question

Are the proposed integrative connections causally necessary for the claimed system-level capability?

### Change / measure

Disrupt selected communication, coordination, regulatory, transport, or integration pathways while holding other conditions as constant as practical.

### Strengthens

Removing specific integrative relationships selectively degrades predicted higher-level functions and restoring them restores function.

### Weakens

Claimed higher-level behavior persists unchanged when supposed integration pathways are removed, or components plus shared environment explain the outcome equally well.

---

# 6. TEST 3 — CONFLICT SUPPRESSION

### Question

Does the candidate system possess mechanisms that limit destructive lower-level competition sufficiently to preserve higher-level viability?

### Change / measure

Measure component conflicts, enforcement, policing, bottlenecks, shared incentives, sanctions, resource constraints, and failure modes.

### Strengthens

Persistent mechanisms suppress component-level behavior that would otherwise destroy higher-level organization.

### Weakens

Lower-level competition routinely overwhelms candidate-whole viability, with no coherent conflict-management mechanism attributable to the proposed level.

---

# 7. TEST 4 — ENERGETIC ORGANIZATION

### Question

Does the candidate level organize acquisition, transformation, allocation, and dissipation of energy in a way that contributes explanatory power beyond independently acting components?

### Change / measure

Track energy flows, constraints, dependencies, allocation, buffering, and failure after energetic disruption.

### Strengthens

Higher-level organization predicts energetic allocation/dependence patterns that component-only models miss.

### Weakens

Energy behavior is fully explained by component processes and shared infrastructure/environment without a meaningful candidate-whole energetic organization.

Brake:

> **Nonequilibrium thermodynamics explains energetic cost and dynamics. It does not by itself define life or individuality.**

---

# 8. TEST 5 — MEMORY TURNOVER

### Question

Can the candidate system preserve usable information through substantial turnover of its components?

### Change / measure

Track whether a past event leaves a physical trace that persists, can later be retrieved/reactivated, and changes future behavior after relevant component replacement.

### Strengthens

System-level memory remains causally available despite turnover and cannot be reduced to one continuously present component.

### Weakens

The apparent memory disappears with particular components or is better explained as separate component memories with no system-level persistence.

Locked chain:

**PAST EVENT → PHYSICAL TRACE → PERSISTENCE → RETRIEVAL / REACTIVATION → ALTERED FUTURE RESPONSE**

---

# 9. TEST 6 — PREDICTION ADVANTAGE

### Question

Does the proposed higher-level organization improve preparation for future conditions in ways that can be prospectively right or wrong?

### Change / measure

Define predictions before outcomes occur and compare candidate-whole models against component/environment alternatives.

### Strengthens

The higher-level model makes prospectively successful predictions unavailable or materially weaker in adequate lower-level models.

### Weakens

The candidate model adds no predictive advantage, or apparent anticipation is reconstructed only after outcomes are known.

Brake:

> **A prediction earns the name partly because it can be wrong.**

---

# 10. TEST 7 — REPAIR AUTONOMY

### Question

Does damage trigger restoration through organization attributable to the candidate system rather than merely independent local repair or external intervention?

### Change / measure

Damage defined components/relationships and observe reconstruction, functional restoration, coordination, and dependence on external controllers.

### Strengthens

The system restores organization through distributed internally coupled processes that preserve higher-level function across varying local damage.

### Weakens

Restoration is entirely local, externally directed, or does not reconstruct the proposed higher-level organization.

For civilization, prefer **coordinated reconstruction** until higher-level autonomy is demonstrated.

---

# 11. TEST 8 — WHOLE-ONLY INFORMATION

### Question

Does integration produce relational/system-level information that improves explanation or action and is not available to isolated components?

### Change / measure

Compare local observations with physically implemented integrated measurements/models and test whether the relational information changes prediction or action.

### Strengthens

System-level relationships produce reproducible informational variables that matter causally and cannot be recovered from an isolated component's local state alone.

### Weakens

The supposed whole-only information is merely a convenient observer summary with no causal or predictive role.

Brake:

> **Relational information can belong to an organized system description without belonging to a conscious owner.**

---

# 12. TEST 9 — HIGHER-LEVEL INTERVENTION

### Question

Can an intervention defined at the proposed higher level change outcomes in a way better predicted by the higher-level model?

### Change / measure

Manipulate candidate-level constraints, topology, rules, resource distribution, boundary conditions, or integration while tracking component and whole outcomes.

### Strengthens

Higher-level interventions generate reliable effects predicted by the higher-level model beyond adequate component-level alternatives.

### Weakens

All effects reduce cleanly to independently specified component interventions with no additional explanatory gain.

No ghostly downward arrow is permitted; the intervention must be physically implemented.

---

# 13. TEST 10 — REPRODUCTION / HEREDITY

### Question

For a strong biological-individual claim, does the candidate level reproduce and transmit organization across generations of comparable candidate individuals?

### Change / measure

Identify candidate offspring/lineages, transmission mechanisms, variation, inheritance, and continuity at the proposed level.

### Strengthens

Clear candidate-level reproduction and heritable organization exist, allowing meaningful comparison among lineages.

### Weakens

Only components reproduce, institutions/cultural patterns replicate without candidate-whole reproduction, or no population of comparable reproducing candidate individuals can be identified.

For civilization, reproduction and civilization-level heredity remain **unestablished**.

---

# 14. TEST 11 — MODEL COMPETITION

### Question

Does the higher-order-individual model actually outperform a simpler adequate alternative?

### Competing models

**MODEL A:** components + interactions + institutions/infrastructure + shared environment.

**MODEL B:** a higher-order individual adds causal organization not adequately captured by Model A.

### Strengthens

Model B generates reproducibly better explanations, interventions, or prospective predictions despite complexity penalties.

### Weakens

Model A remains adequate, or Model B merely renames patterns already explained without producing additional testable value.

Locked rule:

> **Prefer the simpler adequate model unless the higher-order model earns its extra ontology through evidence.**

# 15. WHY THERE IS NO SINGLE PASS SCORE

Biological individuality is multidimensional and debated.

The tests are not eleven boxes where eight checks produce an organism.

Some criteria matter differently depending on the strength of claim.

A system may show:

- strong organization;
- useful system-level variables;
- distributed intelligence;
- memory;
- prediction;

without satisfying stronger requirements for a biological individual.

The figure must therefore show a **convergence pattern**, not a score total.

# 18. EIGHTH-GRADE COMPREHENSION VERSION

If we want Macroscopic Life to be a scientific idea, we have to give it a way to fail.

So we ask eleven different kinds of questions.

Does the possible larger system have a real boundary? Does it depend on connections between its parts? Can it control conflict? Does it organize energy? Can it remember after some parts are replaced? Can it prepare for the future? Can it repair itself? Does combining information create something useful that no single part has? Can changing the larger organization change what the parts do? Can the larger thing reproduce and pass its organization forward? And, most importantly, does this whole idea explain anything better than simply studying all the parts and their environment?

If the answer keeps being no, the theory should get weaker.

That is the point.

---

# 19. FINAL CAPTION DRAFT

> **MODEL — FALSIFICATION FRAMEWORK.** These eleven tests ask what evidence would strengthen or weaken the hypothesis that a candidate macroscopic system deserves treatment as a higher-order individual. The tests ask whether a candidate system has a measurable boundary, whether disrupting integration destroys higher-level function, whether component conflicts are controlled, whether energy is organized at the candidate level, whether memory survives turnover, whether the higher-level model improves prediction, whether repair is autonomous, whether integration produces causally useful whole-level information, whether higher-level interventions have distinctive effects, whether reproduction and heredity exist at the proposed level, and whether the entire higher-order model actually outperforms a simpler components-plus-environment explanation. The point is converging evidence and the willingness to reject the theory if the simpler model keeps winning.
