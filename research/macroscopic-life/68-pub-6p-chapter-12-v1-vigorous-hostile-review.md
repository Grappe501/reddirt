The chapter succeeds at its central job: it makes system-level capability scientifically discussable without granting system-level individuality, agency, consciousness, or organismhood. It is one of the book's most important burden-shifting chapters because it removes the naive objection that a whole cannot possess a capability unless one component contains that capability in full.

# WHAT THE CHAPTER EARNS

By the end of v1.0, the reader may legitimately accept that:

1. explaining cognition with an internal unexplained observer creates a regress rather than a mechanism;
2. cognitive function can depend on distributed physical processes without every component containing the whole computation;
3. distributed does not mean unstructured, homogeneous, or nonhierarchical;
4. system-level capability can be a meaningful explanatory level when organization makes a reproducible causal contribution;
5. relational variables can contain task-relevant physical information absent from any single component considered alone;
6. collective task performance can arise through architectures radically different from a brain;
7. artifacts and institutions can participate causally in human cognitive performance;
8. supra-individual capability does not establish supra-individual consciousness;
9. intelligence, agency, consciousness, and individuality must remain separate claims;
10. Model C — distributed system capability — cannot be promoted to Model D — higher-order cognitive individual — by metaphor.

This is exactly the conceptual platform Chapter 13 requires.

# P1-1 — THE OPENING `YET YOU DO` COMPRESSES TOO MANY LEVELS

Opening sequence:

> No neuron knows your name ... Yet you do.

Rhetorically excellent, but scientifically it slides between component-level knowledge and organism-level subjective knowledge before the chapter has separated representation, behavior, reportability, and conscious experience.

Do not remove the hook.

Harden immediately after it with one sentence:

> **Whatever physical processes make recognition, memory, report, and conscious experience possible, no single neuron is known to contain and interpret the complete process.**

This preserves the force without pretending the chapter has solved how `you know` anything.

---

# P1-2 — OPERATIONAL INTELLIGENCE DEFINITION RISKS MAKING EVOLUTIONARY FIT LOOK INTELLIGENT

Current definition:

> intelligence — the capacity of a system to use information to select, modify, or discover actions or internal states that improve performance across a defined class of problems or changing conditions.

This is the locked book definition and should remain, but the surrounding prose needs an explicit anti-teleology brake. Otherwise natural selection across generations can be casually redescribed as the system itself `discovering` actions.

Add directly after the seven requirements:

> **A process is not intelligent merely because selection, filtering, or evolution eventually produces an effective outcome. The proposed system must itself implement the task-relevant information-processing and state-changing mechanism at the level and timescale being claimed.**

This inherits the teleology discipline earned in Chapter 11.

---

# P1-3 — MODEL C LANGUAGE `COMPONENT-ONLY DESCRIPTIONS FAIL TO CAPTURE ADEQUATELY` IS TOO ELASTIC

`Fail to capture adequately` can be satisfied rhetorically rather than empirically.

Replace Model C definition with:

> **The organized relationships among components make a reproducible causal contribution to task performance or prediction, and models containing those organization-dependent variables outperform appropriate component-only baselines on prospectively defined tests.**

This aligns Model C with Test 8 and the book's model-competition doctrine.

---

# P1-4 — TEST 8 CONCLUSION OVERSTATES WHAT A POSITIVE RESULT ESTABLISHES

Current conclusion says a positive result would establish that:

> the organization of the proposed whole contains task-relevant information that a component-only description misses.

Close, but `the proposed whole` can imply that the chosen boundary has already been validated.

Replace with:

> **A positive result would show that organization-dependent variables at the tested boundary contain reproducible task-relevant information not captured by the tested component-only baseline. Whether that boundary identifies the best explanatory whole remains a further model-comparison question.**

This matters enormously for later civilization-scale arguments, where boundaries will be contestable.

---

# P1-5 — `SCIENCE CAN PRODUCE KNOWLEDGE NO INDIVIDUAL SCIENTIST POSSESSES` NEEDS AN ONTOLOGICAL BRAKE EARLIER

The section eventually supplies the brake, but the opening phrase can be quoted independently as an attribution of literal knowledge to Science.

Use:

> **Scientific communities can preserve, combine, test, and make available bodies of knowledge that no individual scientist possesses in full.**

Then later retain:

> **SUPRA-INDIVIDUAL CAPABILITY ≠ SUPRA-INDIVIDUAL CONSCIOUSNESS.**

This is stronger and screenshot-safe.

---

# P1-6 — CHAPTER 13 HANDOFF MUST NOT SMUGGLE IN A SINGLE PLANETARY COGNITIVE SUBJECT

The architecture requires the handoff to the technological network, but the final paragraphs must remain conditional.

The final transition must preserve these exact boundaries:

> **None of that proves a planetary mind.**

and

> **We did not grow a second brain. We built something outside ourselves that increasingly performs functions brains once had to perform alone.**

Before the final line, add or preserve:

> **Whether those functions belong only to people using tools, to coordinated institutions, to distributed networks, or eventually to some stronger higher-order system is precisely the question that remains open.**

This prevents Chapter 12 from deciding Chapter 13 in advance.

# P2-1 — THERMOSTAT / CALCULATOR / RIVER EXAMPLE MIXES DIFFERENT SENSES OF `COMPUTATION`

The thermostat and calculator are engineered information-processing systems; sediment sorting is a physical transformation sometimes described computationally only under broader theoretical framings.

The sentence is rhetorically useful but invites a philosophy-of-computation detour.

Prefer:

> **Physical and engineered systems can transform inputs and states in highly regular ways. Calling such a transformation computation does not by itself establish intelligence.**

Then retain thermostat/calculator as clear examples and remove the river unless the manuscript explicitly marks it as analogy.

---

# P2-2 — `CORRELATIONS ... CAN AFFECT HOW INFORMATION IS ENCODED AND DECODED` NEEDS CARE

Noise correlations and population relationships can improve or impair coding depending on structure and task. Avoid sounding as if correlation automatically adds information.

Use:

> **Relationships among neuronal responses, including correlation structure, can alter what information is available to a population-level decoder; their effects depend on the structure of the correlations, signals, and task.**

---

# P2-3 — FIVE MATHEMATICIANS EXAMPLE SHOULD DISTINGUISH CAPABILITY FROM INDIVIDUALITY

The example is good but the transition could be even cleaner.

After coordinated mathematicians, add:

> **The group may now possess a task capability that depends on organization even though the evidence still supports a team of people rather than a new person.**

This concretizes Model C without anthropomorphism.

---

# P2-4 — `WHOLE` SHOULD BE TREATED AS A HYPOTHESIS THROUGHOUT TEST 8

The chapter correctly says the word whole must be earned. Carry that grammar into Test 8 by preferring:

- `proposed whole`
- `tested boundary`
- `candidate system-level variable`

rather than unqualified `whole` wherever possible.

The canonical test name remains **Whole-Only Information**.

---

# P2-5 — ANT COLONY SECTION SHOULD EXPLICITLY MARK SPECIES/TASK DEPENDENCE ONCE MORE

The prose already says `depending on species and task`, which is good. Strengthen the later sentence:

> An ant trail can efficiently exploit food sources.

to:

> **In particular species and environmental conditions, recruitment and trail dynamics can improve exploitation of food sources relative to specified alternatives.**

Avoid generic colony optimization language.

---

# P2-6 — EXTENDED COGNITION SECTION SHOULD DISTINGUISH PHILOSOPHICAL CONSTITUTION FROM EMPIRICAL DEPENDENCE

The chapter mostly does this correctly.

Make the split explicit:

> **Empirical causal dependence on an external resource is one claim. Whether that resource should count as a constitutive part of the cognitive system is a stronger boundary claim.**

This will inoculate the next chapter against treating smartphones or databases as automatically part of one macroscopic mind.

---

# P2-7 — COLLECTIVE-INTELLIGENCE LITERATURE SHOULD NOT REST ON ONE FAMOUS `c FACTOR` RESULT

The manuscript appropriately notes contestation. In the final notes/research layer, cite both the original empirical literature and critical/replication/alternative-factor work. Do not let one paper carry the claim that groups possess a general intelligence factor.

The prose itself can remain restrained:

> **Some research has modeled cross-task group performance using a collective-intelligence factor, while later work has debated its interpretation, measurement, and robustness.**

---

# P2-8 — AI SECTION MUST PRESERVE THE SYSTEM-BOUNDARY PROBLEM

The AI discussion is useful only if it avoids shifting ambiguously among model, deployed service, datacenter, training pipeline, human organization, and internet-scale infrastructure.

Require a sentence equivalent to:

> **`The AI system` is not a scientifically adequate boundary until the claim specifies whether it refers to a trained model, an inference process, a deployed service, a human-machine workflow, or a larger technical network.**

Then preserve:

> **TASK PERFORMANCE ≠ CONSCIOUSNESS.**

This is especially important because readers will otherwise treat current AI as the easiest analogy for the book's macroscopic-life hypothesis.

# ELEVEN-TEST AUDIT

Primary test:

**TEST 8 — WHOLE-ONLY INFORMATION — STRONG PASS, REQUIRES P1-4 BOUNDARY HARDENING.**

Secondary test connection:

**TEST 3 — CONFLICT SUPPRESSION — UNDERUSED.**

The architecture specifically reconnects intelligence to individuality through conflict suppression. v1.1 should add a short paragraph after system-level attribution or model competition:

> **A system that performs a task collectively may still fragment when component interests conflict. That is why system-level problem solving and higher-order individuality remain different claims. Test 3 asks whether the organization can detect, constrain, redirect, arbitrate, or suppress destabilizing conflict when component-level policies diverge from system-level performance.**

Then brake:

> **CONFLICT SUPPRESSION CAN SUPPORT COHERENT SYSTEM PERFORMANCE WITHOUT PROVING THAT THE SYSTEM IS ONE BIOLOGICAL INDIVIDUAL.**

This is not a new P1 because the chapter works without it, but adding it materially improves architecture fidelity.
