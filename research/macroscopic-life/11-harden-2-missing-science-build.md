# 1. LIFE / ORGANISM / INDIVIDUAL / INTELLIGENCE / AGENCY / CONSCIOUSNESS

## Finding

The manuscript must stop allowing these categories to blur.

Modern philosophy of biology and evolutionary biology do not support one universally accepted, context-free criterion for biological individuality. Different research domains use physiological, evolutionary, developmental, immunological, metabolic, reproductive, ecological, and other criteria. The important implication for Book One is not that individuality is meaningless, but that **individuality is plural and operational**.

## Lock

Book One will distinguish at least six categories:

- **Life** — organized, self-maintaining, far-from-equilibrium biological process; no single criterion is sufficient at all edge cases.
- **Organism** — a strongly integrated living organization conventionally treated as one physiological individual.
- **Biological individual** — an entity individuated for a specific biological question; may be evolutionary, physiological, developmental, immunological, etc.
- **Intelligence** — information-guided problem solving/adaptive behavior; does not imply consciousness.
- **Agency** — functionally organized action selection relative to goals/regulated states; does not imply human-like intention.
- **Consciousness** — subjective experience; scientifically distinct from all of the above and not required by the Macroscopic Life argument.

## Manuscript consequence

Add a compact taxonomy early in Act II. This will prevent later readers from treating `alive`, `organism`, `individual`, `intelligent`, `agentic`, and `conscious` as synonyms.

## Core sources

- Alison K. McConwell, *Biological Individuality*, Cambridge University Press, 2023. DOI: 10.1017/9781108942775.
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, `Biological Individuals`, substantive revision 2024.
- NASA Astrobiology discussion of the difficulty of defining life and the history of the common working definition.

# 2. MAJOR EVOLUTIONARY TRANSITIONS — THE STRONGEST PRECEDENT

## Finding

This is one of the strongest foundations of Book One and deserves more narrative weight.

Evolutionary history includes transitions in which previously more autonomous units formed new higher-level individuals. Canonical examples include the origin of the eukaryotic cell through symbiotic integration, transitions to multicellularity, and highly integrated eusocial insect societies.

The modern literature emphasizes that a transition is not just `many things cooperating`. Important ingredients include:

1. **group formation**;
2. **alignment of fitness interests / conflict reduction**;
3. **group integration**;
4. often **division of labor**;
5. often increased **component dependence**;
6. in strong transitions, transfer of important reproductive functions from lower-level units to the higher-level whole.

## Stronger Book One principle

> **Higher-order individuality is not created by size or cooperation alone. It emerges when the evolutionary fate and functioning of lower-level units become sufficiently integrated that selection, regulation, and reproduction increasingly make sense at the level of the whole.**

## Conflict suppression lock

Cancer is not merely a colorful analogy. Multicellular individuality depends on mechanisms that constrain cell-level cheating and destructive proliferation. Development from a single cell or narrow bottleneck can reduce within-organism genetic conflict; apoptosis, immune surveillance, tissue architecture, and regulatory constraints also contribute to organismal integrity.

For social insect colonies, reproductive division of labor can move important reproductive function upward toward colony-level organization.

## Manuscript consequence

Expand Chapter 5 substantially. Make the reader understand that **conflict suppression is one of the defining problems of becoming a new individual**.

## Core sources

- Michod-style transition literature summarized in: `The Evolution of Multicellularity: A Minor Major Transition?`, *Annual Review of Ecology, Evolution, and Systematics*.
- `Feedback loops in the major evolutionary transition to eusociality`, *Current Opinion in Insect Science*, 2019.
- `Patterns of reproductive differentiation and reproductive plasticity in the major evolutionary transition to superorganismality`, *Current Opinion in Insect Science*, 2019.
- `Inclusive fitness and the major transitions in evolution`, *Current Opinion in Insect Science*, 2019.
- Kocher & Kingwell, `The Molecular Substrates of Insect Eusociality`, *Annual Review of Genetics*, 2024.

# 3. SYMBIOSIS / ENDOSYMBIOSIS — YESTERDAY'S ORGANISM CAN BECOME TODAY'S COMPONENT

## Finding

The eukaryotic cell is a decisive precedent for the book because mitochondria retain bacterial ancestry. Contemporary work on eukaryogenesis still debates many details of the transition, but bacterial endosymbiosis at the origin of mitochondria is central to current models.

## Locked conceptual line

> **A component we now regard as part of one organism can carry the evolutionary history of a lineage that was once more independent.**

This is not evidence that humans are becoming organelles. It demonstrates that the boundaries of biological individuality have changed radically in evolutionary history.

## Manuscript consequence

Expand the mitochondria/endosymbiosis sequence in Chapter 4 or 5. Use it as a historical shock: what appears to us today as one cell contains the legacy of a merger between once-distinct lineages.

## Core sources

- `The emerging view on the origin and early evolution of eukaryotic cells`, *Nature*, 2024.
- Murphy & O'Neill, `A break in mitochondrial endosymbiosis as a basis for inflammatory diseases`, *Nature*, 2024.
- `Transitions in individuality through symbiosis`, *Current Opinion in Microbiology*, 2016.

# 4. MICROBIOME / HOLOBIONT — USE THE CONTROVERSY, NOT THE SLOGAN

## Finding

The microbiome complicates organismal boundaries, but Book One should avoid declaring the human-plus-microbiota automatically to be one evolutionary individual.

Microbiota participate in digestion, immune development, colonization resistance, signaling, and other host functions. At the same time, microbial membership is variable, spatially heterogeneous, replaceable, and subject to its own ecological/evolutionary dynamics.

## Lock

The microbiome is a **boundary problem**, not proof of superorganismality.

Use the question:

> **How much ecologically distinct participation can a biological individual contain before `self` becomes a regulatory rather than purely genetic concept?**

## Manuscript consequence

Use the microbiome briefly in Chapter 6 to destabilize naive skin/genome boundaries, then move immediately to operational membership tests.

## Core sources

- McCallum & Tropini, `The gut microbiota and its biogeography`, *Nature Reviews Microbiology*, 2024.

# 5. BOUNDARY ARCHITECTURE

## Finding

Book One needs a more rigorous theory of boundaries.

A membrane is only one kind of boundary. Biological individuation can also depend on regulation, causal coupling, development, immune discrimination, metabolism, reproduction, and evolutionary fate.

## Locked boundary framework

For Macroscopic Life candidates, test at least six boundary dimensions:

1. **Geometric boundary** — spatial edge, membrane, shell, territorial extent.
2. **Exchange boundary** — regulated entry/exit of matter and energy.
3. **Informational boundary** — preferential internal communication and state sharing.
4. **Regulatory boundary** — variables actively stabilized at the system level.
5. **Causal boundary** — perturbations propagate more strongly within the proposed system than across its environment.
6. **Evolutionary boundary** — lower-level fitness interests become aligned enough for selection to act meaningfully on the higher-level unit.

## New test

**Boundary Perturbation Test:** disrupt a proposed boundary mechanism and measure whether the candidate system loses coherent higher-level regulation while components remain locally functional.

## Manuscript consequence

Rebuild Chapter 6 around boundary pluralism rather than `skin is not the only kind of boundary`.

# 6. THERMODYNAMICS — ORGANIZATION HAS AN ENERGY COST

## Finding

Living systems operate far from thermodynamic equilibrium and require continuing energy and material throughput. Modern nonequilibrium thermodynamics emphasizes that energy dissipation is not decorative—it constrains sensing, adaptation, accuracy, biochemical regulation, synchronization, and other functions.

## Important correction

Do **not** say life `violates` or `escapes` the Second Law. Local biological order is maintained through energy and matter flows while total entropy production remains consistent with thermodynamics.

## Locked Book One formulation

> **Life does not defeat entropy. It maintains organized states by continuously consuming free-energy gradients and exporting entropy to its surroundings.**

## Macroscopic implication

A candidate higher-order individual cannot be defined only by information. It must possess meaningful organization of energy and material throughput that supports persistence, regulation, and work.

## New test

**Energetic Closure Test:** identify the energy transformations required to maintain the proposed higher-level organization, then determine whether those flows are regulated at the proposed scale or merely coincidentally shared by components.

## Manuscript consequence

Add a concise thermodynamic grounding section in Chapter 5 or 6 and return to it in Chapter 14.

## Core sources

- Fang & Wang, `Nonequilibrium Thermodynamics in Cell Biology`, *Annual Review of Biophysics*, 2020.
- `Nonequilibrium Thermodynamics for Biochemical Networks: Energetic Principles of Cellular Function`, *Annual Review of Biophysics*, 2026.
- Singh et al., `Non-equilibrium self-assembly for living matter-like properties`, *Nature Reviews Chemistry*, 2024.
- Muenker et al., `Accessing activity and viscoelastic properties of artificial and living systems from passive measurement`, *Nature Materials*, 2024.

# 7. MORPHOGENESIS — GLOBAL FORM FROM DISTRIBUTED MECHANISM

## Finding

The manuscript's `no ghost blueprint` doctrine is strongly supported, but we need to harden the mechanisms.

Morphogenesis emerges from interacting biochemical signaling, gene regulation, cell behaviors, geometry, mechanics, and bioelectric processes. Mechanical forces are not merely outputs; they can feed back on fate and tissue organization.

## Locked conceptual line

> **Global anatomical form can be produced and stabilized by distributed local interactions without any individual cell containing a complete symbolic representation of the finished organism.**

## Important boundary

This does not establish that morphology is stored in one hidden field or one `code`. Multiple interacting mechanisms contribute.

## Manuscript consequence

Chapter 8 should explicitly present morphogenesis as a **closed-loop multiscale process** rather than a one-way genetic program.

## Core sources

# 8. BIOELECTRICITY — KEEP IT, BUT DEFINE THE MECHANISM

## Finding

Recent reviews support non-neural bioelectricity as a serious part of morphogenesis, tissue repair, and cell behavior, while also emphasizing that the field remains less understood than neural electrophysiology.

## Locked distinctions

Book One must distinguish:

- ion concentration gradients;
- membrane potential;
- ion-channel activity;
- ionic currents;
- endogenous electric fields;
- gap-junction coupling;
- electrochemical signaling;
- neural electrical activity;
- non-neural developmental bioelectric phenomena.

Do not collapse all of these into `energy`.

## Strongest safe claim

> **Electrical state is one of several physical variables through which cells coordinate behavior during development and repair.**

## Boundary

No inference to aura, telepathy, emotion-frequency broadcasting, or unknown long-range communication follows.

## Manuscript consequence

Chapter 7 should become more mechanistic at the beginning, then more provocative after the reader understands the physical substrate.

## Core sources

# 9. REGENERATION / POSITIONAL MEMORY — A MAJOR HARDENING WIN

## Finding

Recent work gives us stronger mechanisms for the `missing limb` argument.

Planarian regeneration depends on pluripotent neoblasts plus positional-control systems; Wnt/β-catenin signaling plays a major role in anterior/posterior specification. Axolotl/salamander limb regeneration involves connective-tissue cells retaining spatially organized positional information, including gene-expression and chromatin-state differences.

A 2025 Nature study specifically reported molecular mechanisms underlying positional memory in salamander connective tissue and showed that manipulating positional memory alters signaling output during regeneration.

## Locked conceptual correction

Do **not** say the organism compares itself with a consciously represented template.

Say:

> **The remaining tissue retains distributed physical information about positional identity and regulatory relationships. Injury changes those relationships, enabling different regenerative responses according to location and missing structure.**

## Strong line retained

> **A macroscopic absence can be converted into microscopic actionable signals.**

This remains defensible when immediately explained mechanistically.

## Manuscript consequence

Chapter 9 should be one of the book's scientific centerpieces. Add positional memory as the bridge from regeneration to memory.

## Core sources

- Otsuki et al., `Molecular basis of positional memory in limb regeneration`, *Nature*, 2025.
- `A histone code controls positional identity in limb regeneration`, *Nature Reviews Molecular Cell Biology*, 2024.
- `Evolutionary dynamics of whole-body regeneration across planarian flatworms`, *Nature Ecology & Evolution*, 2023/2024.
- `Wnt/β-catenin signalling is required for pole-specific chromatin remodeling during planarian regeneration`, *Nature Communications*, 2023.

# 10. MEMORY — BUILD A HIERARCHY AND REQUIRE FUTURE CONSEQUENCE

## Finding

Book One's definition is sound but should be hardened:

> **Memory is past information made causally available to future behavior.**

Adaptive immune memory provides a clear established case. Epigenetic states can persist and influence future gene regulation, but not every persistent molecular state should be called memory. External memory technologies also participate in human cognitive processes, but `library = memory` is too simple.

## Locked hierarchy

Distinguish:

1. **Persistent state** — history leaves a physical trace.
2. **Encoded state** — the trace carries discriminable information about prior conditions.
3. **Retrievable state** — system processes can access/use the trace.
4. **Functional memory** — retrieval changes future system behavior.
5. **Adaptive memory** — memory improves future response under relevant conditions.

## Supra-component memory criterion

A higher-order memory claim becomes interesting when:

- information survives turnover of original components;
- retrieval depends on system architecture;
- the retrieved information changes future system-level behavior.

## Manuscript consequence

Rebuild Chapter 10 around this hierarchy. Civilizational archives become stronger examples only after retrieval and causal use are demonstrated.

## Core sources

- Lam et al., `A guide to adaptive immune memory`, *Nature Reviews Immunology*, 2024.
- Smith et al., `DNA methylation in mammalian development and disease`, *Nature Reviews Genetics*, 2024.
- Briffa et al., `Dissecting Mechanisms of Epigenetic Memory Through Computational Modeling`, *Annual Review of Plant Biology*, 2024.
- `An overview of the Externalization of memory`, *Memory, Mind & Media*, 2026.

# 11. ANTICIPATION / PREDICTION — TEMPORAL ORGANIZATION

## Finding

Circadian biology provides an exceptionally strong example of non-conscious anticipation. Contemporary reviews explicitly describe circadian clocks as allowing physiology to anticipate recurring environmental challenges.

## Locked distinction

### Reaction
`event → detection → response`

### Anticipation
`present cue/internal clock + retained temporal structure → preparatory state → expected event`

### Forecast/model
`current state + model → estimated future state → action`

## Critical doctrine

> **Prediction does not mean the future causes the present. Present physical states encode regularities or models that alter current action before the predicted event occurs.**

## New test

**Prediction Advantage Test:** an anticipatory system must outperform an appropriate purely reactive baseline under repeatable conditions, and its predictions must be capable of error.

## Manuscript consequence

Strengthen Chapter 11 with circadian communication across tissues and make the difference between homeostatic feedback and anticipatory/feedforward regulation visually explicit.

## Core sources

- Mortimer et al., `Circadian clock communication during homeostasis and ageing`, *Nature Reviews Molecular Cell Biology*, 2025.
- Franken & Dijk, `Sleep and circadian rhythmicity as entangled processes serving homeostasis`, *Nature Reviews Neuroscience*, 2024.

# 12. COLLECTIVE INTELLIGENCE — MULTISCALE BUT NOT AUTOMATICALLY ONE MIND

## Finding

Recent biological work explicitly develops collective intelligence as a multiscale concept spanning cells, tissues, organisms, and swarms. This is intellectually compatible with Book One, but some of this literature is perspective-driven and must not be narrated as universal consensus.

Social insects and other distributed systems offer established cases of collective problem solving without a central executive.

## Lock

Collective intelligence is demonstrated when system architecture enables problem-solving performance that cannot be adequately described as isolated components operating independently.

It does **not** imply:

- one consciousness;
- one evolutionary individual;
- one persistent agent;
- organismhood.

## New baseline rule

Every higher-order intelligence claim should specify a comparison baseline:

> **Does the integrated system outperform the same components when communication/coordination is removed or randomized?**

## Manuscript consequence

Chapter 12 should foreground this baseline and reduce rhetorical use of `thinking` unless it is functionally defined.

## Core sources

- Kocher & Kingwell, *Annual Review of Genetics*, 2024, for eusocial collective traits.

# 13. CIVILIZATION / WHOLE-ONLY INFORMATION

## Finding

The most promising strengthening of the civilization argument is not `civilization looks like a brain`.

It is **distributed epistemic capability**.

Modern civilization produces knowledge that no single human directly possesses from personal observation: global weather states, genomic databases, astronomical catalogs, epidemiological surveillance, economic aggregates, climate reconstructions, and integrated scientific models.

This does not mean one civilizational subject knows them.

It does establish a functional distinction between:

- **information contained somewhere in the network**;
- **information integrated by the network**;
- **information converted into system-level action**.

## Locked three-level framework

### Distributed information
Data exists across components.

### Integrated knowledge
System architecture combines distributed observations into higher-level representations unavailable to isolated components.

### Operationalized knowledge
Those representations alter coordinated behavior of the larger system.

This is a much stronger framework than simply saying `civilization knows`.

## Manuscript consequence

Make this the conceptual heart of Chapter 13.

# 15. BIRTH / DEATH / CONTINUITY — A MISSING TEST, BUT HANDLE CAREFULLY

## Finding

There is no simple, cross-scale scientific definition of the birth or death of every biological individual. Cell death itself comprises multiple regulated mechanisms, while organismal identity and continuity are distinct conceptual problems.

Therefore Book One should not invent a universal death criterion for macroscopic systems.

## Useful operational questions

### Birth test
When does a proposed higher-order organization first acquire:

- persistent integration;
- system-level regulation;
- self-maintenance;
- continuity across component turnover?

### Death test
What perturbation produces **irreversible loss of the organizational processes that made the higher-level individual scientifically useful as one system**, even if many components remain alive?

## Important distinction

Component death ≠ higher-level death.

Higher-level death ≠ immediate death of all components.

A human can die while many cells remain viable temporarily. A colony can collapse while many workers remain alive. An institution can cease to exist while its members survive.

These comparisons are conceptual calibration, not proof that institutions are biological organisms.

## Manuscript consequence

Add birth/death as a short but memorable subsection in Chapter 5 or 14 rather than a standalone chapter.

# 16. NESTED INDIVIDUALITY — HIGHER LEVEL DOES NOT ERASE LOWER LEVEL

## Finding

Biological organization is hierarchically nested. Major transitions do not always erase every lower-level competence; they change autonomy, reproductive control, conflict, dependence, and selection.

## Lock

> **Higher-order individuality is not necessarily zero-sum. A component can remain a meaningful individual for some biological questions while participating in a more integrated individual at another level.**

This solves a philosophical problem in the manuscript: humans would not have to become mindless cells for a higher-order organization to become scientifically meaningful.

## Proposed continuum

`independent → cooperative → interdependent → integrated → obligately integrated`

Use as a heuristic, not a universal evolutionary law.

# 17. INFORMATION — STOP USING ONE WORD FOR SIX DIFFERENT THINGS

## Finding

`Information` is currently overloaded in Book One.

## Locked taxonomy

1. **Physical state** — a measurable configuration.
2. **Correlation** — state A covaries with state B.
3. **Signal** — a state that causally affects a receiver.
4. **Encoded information** — distinctions reliably mapped to other states/actions.
5. **Functional meaning** — receiver-specific consequence within a control system.
6. **Memory/model** — persistent encoded structure used across time.

## Rule

Whenever `information` carries an important argument, specify which of these is intended.

## Scientific rule

No criterion receives automatic supremacy across all contexts, but **reproduction/heredity/selection are especially important if the claim is specifically Darwinian evolutionary individuality**.

# 21. WHAT HARDEN-2 ADDS TO THE BOOK'S CENTRAL ARGUMENT

The strengthened argument now becomes:

1. **Perception is selective.** Observers never sample all physically available information.
2. **Scale matters.** Processes can disappear when observed at the wrong spatial or temporal resolution.
3. **Life is hierarchical.** Biological organization is nested across levels.
4. **Individuality has evolved.** Previously more autonomous units have become components of higher-level individuals.
5. **Transitions require more than cooperation.** Conflict suppression, dependence, integration, and reproduction often matter.
6. **Boundaries are multidimensional.** A skin is one solution, not the definition of individuality.
7. **Organization costs energy.** Persistent living order requires regulated material and energetic throughput.
8. **Global form can emerge from local mechanism.** Morphogenesis and regeneration demonstrate multiscale coordination without a miniature executive.
9. **History can be physically retained.** Functional memory exists in multiple biological substrates.
10. **Systems can anticipate.** Circadian and other control architectures use temporal regularities to prepare before events occur.
11. **Intelligence can be distributed.** Problem solving need not sit in one component.
12. **Civilization has acquired higher-scale capabilities.** Its sensing, memory, prediction, and information integration exceed individual humans.
13. **But civilization fails important individuality tests.** Its global boundary, conflict suppression, goal unity, reproduction, heredity, and higher-level selection remain weak or unresolved.
14. **Therefore no macroscopic organism has been demonstrated.**
15. **But biology gives no principled reason to assume individuality must terminate at the human scale.**
16. **The question can now be made empirical through comparative criteria and perturbation tests.**

# 22. NEW SCIENTIFIC TEST SUITE

HARDEN-2 adds a more rigorous research toolkit for Chapter 16:

### 1. Boundary Perturbation Test
Disrupt proposed boundary mechanisms; measure whether higher-level coherence selectively fails.

### 2. Integration Ablation Test
Remove or randomize communication among components; compare system-level capability.

### 3. Conflict Suppression Test
Identify component-level incentives/replication and determine whether higher-level organization constrains destructive optimization.

### 4. Energetic Closure Test
Trace whether energy transformations are regulated in service of higher-level persistence.

### 5. Memory Turnover Test
Determine whether functional information survives replacement of the components that originally encoded it.

### 6. Prediction Advantage Test
Compare anticipatory performance with reactive baselines and require falsifiable error.

### 7. Repair Autonomy Test
Damage the system and distinguish endogenous restoration from external rebuilding.

### 8. Whole-Only Information Test
Ask whether integration creates scientifically usable representations unavailable to isolated components.

### 9. Higher-Level Intervention Test
Manipulate a proposed system-level variable and test predicted downstream effects.

### 10. Reproduction/Heredity Test
Identify descendants, transmitted organization, variation, and differential persistence at the proposed level.

### 11. Model Competition Test
Compare the higher-order model against independent-agent / shared-forcing / network-only alternatives.

The higher-order description earns status only if it improves explanation or prediction.
