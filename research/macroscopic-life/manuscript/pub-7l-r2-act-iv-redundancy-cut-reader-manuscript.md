# ACT IV — THE INTELLIGENCE QUESTION

## Chapter 12 — Intelligence Without a Little Man

No neuron in your brain knows your name.

No neuron understands this page, contains your childhood, plans your afternoon, or worries about tomorrow.

Yet you do.

Whatever physical processes make recognition, memory, report, and conscious experience possible, no single neuron is known to contain and interpret the complete process.

A brain is not a bag of identical switches. It contains specialized cells, circuits, regions, recurrent pathways, hubs, bottlenecks, modulators, and structures with different roles. Local specialization and distributed activity coexist.

There is no known neuron sitting above the network, reading everything else and experiencing the finished result for the organism.

> **THE WHOLE CAN SOLVE A PROBLEM THAT NO SINGLE COMPONENT SOLVES ALONE.**

That raises the question Act IV must answer:

**When does a capability legitimately belong to a system rather than merely to its parts?**

### The Little Man Who Explains Nothing

A crude theory of vision says the brain builds an internal picture and someone inside looks at it.

But then who interprets the picture for that observer?

Another observer?

And another?

That is the homunculus regress. An explanation of intelligence cannot finish by inserting another unexplained intelligence inside the system.

Representations may be useful scientific concepts, but their causal role must be implemented by physical processes.

### A Working Definition

For this book:

> **INTELLIGENCE — the capacity of a system to use information to select, modify, or discover actions or internal states that improve performance across a defined class of problems or changing conditions.**

That requires a defined system, available information, task, possible actions, performance measure, mechanism, and comparison baseline.

It does not require speech, feeling, personhood, or consciousness.

The semantic ladder established earlier still applies:

> **SIGNAL ≠ INFORMATION ≠ INTEGRATION ≠ INTELLIGENCE ≠ AGENCY ≠ CONSCIOUSNESS**

We do not need to repeat that firewall after every example. We need to respect it.

### When Does the Whole Own the Capability?

Imagine five mathematicians working separately. Each solves a problem independently. We have capable components.

Now allow them to exchange intermediate results, divide tasks, preserve partial calculations, criticize one another, and combine specialties. Organization changes what the group can do.

That gives us four models:

**MODEL A — CAPABLE COMPONENTS**  
Individual components adequately explain performance.

**MODEL B — COORDINATED COMPONENTS**  
Coordination improves what capable components accomplish together.

**MODEL C — DISTRIBUTED SYSTEM CAPABILITY**  
Organization-dependent variables make a reproducible causal or predictive contribution beyond appropriate component-only baselines.

**MODEL D — HIGHER-ORDER COGNITIVE INDIVIDUAL**  
Additional evidence justifies treating the larger system as a cognitive individual rather than merely a distributed capability.

> **DO NOT PROMOTE MODEL C TO MODEL D BY METAPHOR.**

A room containing a chess grandmaster contains chess-playing intelligence, but the room is not thereby a chess player. Presence of capable parts does not transfer their abilities to an arbitrary boundary.

The reverse mistake is equally serious: if a task genuinely depends on organized interactions and no isolated component can perform it, refusing to recognize a system-level capability can also misdescribe the mechanism.

So define the boundary and task. Perturb the organization. Remove connections. Delay communication. Shuffle relationships. Disable specialized components. Compare predictions.

Capability attribution must do causal work.

### Test 8 — Whole-Only Information

> **Does the state or relationship of the proposed whole provide reproducible task-relevant information that cannot be recovered from any single component alone and that improves prediction of system behavior beyond appropriate component-only models?**

Nothing mystical is required. Relationships among physical states can contain task-relevant information: topology, timing, spatial configuration, correlation, interaction history, or distributed state.

> **SAME PARTS. DIFFERENT RELATIONSHIPS. DIFFERENT SYSTEM STATE.**

A rigorous test compares an appropriate component baseline with models containing physically measurable relational variables, then asks whether the added organization improves out-of-sample prediction and survives perturbation.

> **WHOLE-ONLY INFORMATION, IF DEMONSTRATED, IS INFORMATION IN ORGANIZATION — NOT INFORMATION FLOATING ABOVE MATTER.**

A positive Test 8 result establishes explanatory relevance of organization at the tested boundary. It does not by itself establish Model D.

### Swarms, Institutions, and External Cognition

Ant colonies, brains, and scientific institutions can all exhibit distributed task performance while using radically different mechanisms.

Local interaction can produce coherent global behavior without a central commander. But coherence alone is not our operational definition of intelligence.

Likewise, humans routinely move cognitive work into the environment. We count on fingers, write notes, use maps, calculators, databases, search systems, and software. External-cognition and extended-cognition traditions disagree over when such resources should count as constituents of cognition rather than powerful tools.

This book does not need to settle that dispute.

The weaker fact is enough:

> **Human cognitive performance can depend causally on structures outside the biological brain.**

Science provides a striking example. No scientist contains all of modern science, yet communities, instruments, archives, methods, software, education, and institutions preserve and combine knowledge across people and generations.

That is real supra-individual capability without requiring a person called Science who wakes up and knows it.

### AI Makes the Boundary Harder

When we say “the AI did it,” what is the system?

The trained model? The running inference process? Servers? Retrieval? Databases? Software? Human operators? The deployed service? A larger workflow?

The scientific boundary has to be specified before performance is attributed.

Task performance can then be measured. Whether that performance implies agency or consciousness is a separate question.

### Who Is Solving the Problem?

Person. Person plus tool. Team. Institution. Network.

Each attribution proposes a boundary.

Which boundary best captures causal dependencies? Which predicts failure when communication is severed? Which contains information unavailable to isolated components? Which persists through turnover? Which explains conflict and control? Which outperforms simpler models?

That is how a metaphysical-sounding question becomes empirical.

> **A CAPABILITY CAN LEGITIMATELY BELONG TO AN ORGANIZED SYSTEM EVEN WHEN NO COMPONENT POSSESSES THAT CAPABILITY ALONE.**

That still does not tell us that the system is one individual.

Now look at the system humans have built outside themselves.

## Chapter 13 — The Nervous System We Built Outside Ourselves

A weather satellite measures an ocean no human observer can see all at once. Buoys, stations, aircraft, radar, and other instruments measure other parts of the same changing atmosphere.

The observations enter communication systems, cross institutional boundaries, combine with other measurements, enter models, produce forecasts, and influence decisions far away.

No human body spans that loop.

> **NO HUMAN BODY SPANS THE PLANET. HUMAN-BUILT SENSING SYSTEMS INCREASINGLY DO.**

The title of this chapter is intentionally dangerous.

A biological nervous system is living tissue with specialized cells, development, metabolism, electrophysiology, synapses, recurrent signaling, sensorimotor coupling, repair, and homeostatic regulation.

A fiber-optic cable is not an axon. A router is not a synapse. A satellite is not a sensory neuron.

> **FUNCTIONAL ANALOGY ≠ BIOLOGICAL IDENTITY.**

With that brake attached, the comparison becomes useful.

### Functions Keep Moving Outside Us

Humans externalize memory into writing and archives, sensing into instruments, calculation into machines, communication into networks, and prediction into models and institutions.

Over time many of these aids became standardized, networked, automated, and linked across enormous distances.

There was no single plan and no inevitable march toward a planetary organism. What emerged is a heterogeneous ecology of human-built infrastructure.

Yet the scale of possible action changed.

### Planet-Scale Sensing

Weather systems make the point cleanly. Measurements distributed across land, ocean, atmosphere, and orbit can become available for shared forecasting tasks.

No unaided person can occupy those observing locations simultaneously. The organized system can therefore perform a sensing function beyond any one human's physical reach.

That is a legitimate system-level capability in the limited sense of Chapter 12.

It does not identify one sensory subject.

### Signals That Outrun Bodies

Modern telecommunications let encoded signals travel through fiber, radio, cellular networks, and satellites. The engineering differs radically from neural signaling.

The important fact is functional: information generated in one location can become available for action at enormous distance before a human messenger could travel there.

This changes the geographic scale of coordination.

### Memory That Outlives the Rememberer

Records can survive the people who created them. They can be copied, migrated to new hardware, interpreted by new institutions, and used decades later.

The carrier changes while information remains causally available.

That is why **TEST 5 — MEMORY TURNOVER** matters at larger scales.

Archival persistence alone is not enough. The retained information must remain available to a defined system and alter later behavior.

The Chapter 10 rule remains sufficient:

> **THE PAST MATTERS LATER ONLY IF SOMETHING PHYSICAL PERSISTS AND CAN RE-ENTER CAUSAL PROCESSING.**

### Integration, Prediction, and Control

Storage alone is not integration. A warehouse can hold a million documents without combining them.

Weather forecasting combines observations with model state. Power grids measure conditions and alter generation or load. Industrial controllers sense, calculate, act, and measure again. Emergency systems turn observations and forecasts into warnings that change behavior.

These are bounded information loops extending through technical systems, institutions, and physical action.

The fact that such loops exist proves something important: sensing, memory, computation, prediction, and control can be distributed across organizations larger than one human body.

It does not prove that all such loops belong to one global agent.

**[FIGURE 13 — THE NERVOUS SYSTEM WE BUILT OUTSIDE OURSELVES — INSERT HERE]**

> **THE INTERNET IS NOT INTERESTING BECAUSE IT LOOKS LIKE A BRAIN. IT IS INTERESTING BECAUSE IT CHANGES WHICH FUNCTIONS CAN BE PERFORMED AT WHICH SCALE.**

> **WE HAVE SHOWN THAT THE FUNCTIONS CAN SCALE. WE HAVE NOT YET SHOWN THAT THE INDIVIDUAL DOES.**

*Scientific brake:* **FUNCTIONS CAN SCALE WITHOUT PROVING THAT INDIVIDUALITY SCALES WITH THEM.**

### Look Closely at the Brain Analogy

From far away, the internet offers tempting similarities: many nodes, long-distance signaling, routing, redundancy, storage, feedback, specialization, local failure, and changing connectivity.

Move closer and the differences dominate. Endpoints contain independent people and institutions with conflicting goals. Networks fragment. Data are duplicated, hidden, corrupted, attacked, ignored, and deliberately falsified. Infrastructure depends on power, fabrication, cooling, repair, governance, and supply chains crossing any simple internet boundary.

There is no established single global action policy or agreed organismal boundary.

The analogy survives only as a functional question: what can this architecture do at this scale?

### Conflict and Higher-Level Variables

Civilization contains mechanisms for coordination and conflict management: standards, laws, authentication, error correction, markets, courts, security systems, and emergency institutions.

It also contains war, deception, incompatible goals, institutional capture, fraud, sabotage, and collective-action failure.

Conflict therefore becomes evidence to measure, not a rhetorical point for or against individuality.

Likewise, network-level variables such as topology, latency, information access, routing architecture, or coordination structure can matter predictively and causally.

**TEST 8 — WHOLE-ONLY INFORMATION** asks whether relational variables improve prediction beyond appropriate component baselines.

**TEST 9 — HIGHER-LEVEL INTERVENTION** asks whether operationally defined higher-level interventions improve causal prediction or control beyond appropriate lower-level descriptions.

A useful macro-variable is scientifically valuable. It is not automatically a new individual.

### AI Accelerates the Loop

AI can shorten portions of sensing, classification, retrieval, prediction, recommendation, and control loops. In some systems, intermediate states can move from sensor to database to model to software service to actuator without continuous human interpretation at every step.

That changes latency and capability.

It still does not choose the correct system boundary for us.

The relevant system might be a model, a human-machine workflow, an institution using multiple models, or a larger sensor-model-actuator network.

### Four Explanations

For the macroscopic system we are approaching, keep four explanations alive:

**Model A — Individual Humans + Tools**

**Model B — Coordinated Institutions**

**Model C — Distributed Technical-Social Network**

**Model D — Higher-Order Cognitive Individual**

Models A through C already explain a great deal. Model D therefore carries the extra burden.

### Function Has Moved Outside the Body

Human civilization contains systems extending sensing, memory, communication, computation, prediction, and control beyond the capacity and lifetime of any individual human.

Some capabilities genuinely depend on organized relationships among people, institutions, and infrastructure.

Cognitive function can therefore become dependent on organizations extending beyond any individual body.

That is the earned result.

Not a planetary mind.

Not a second brain.

A change in the scale at which functions can be performed.

> **WE HAVE SHOWN THAT THE FUNCTIONS CAN SCALE. WE HAVE NOT YET SHOWN THAT THE INDIVIDUAL DOES.**

Now civilization itself goes on trial.

## Chapter 14 — Is Civilization Becoming an Individual?

At night, another geography rises through the continents.

Cities burn against the dark. Roads stitch settlements together. Aircraft cross oceans. Ships move through narrow corridors. Electrical grids redistribute energy. Radio and fiber carry information. Satellites measure weather, crops, oceans, fire, ice, position, and communications.

Food, fuel, minerals, medicine, money, waste, pathogens, people, and instructions move through the network.

Seen from far enough away, the temptation is almost irresistible.

*It looks alive.*

But a photograph is not a biological test.

> **A PLANETARY PATTERN IS NOT A PLANETARY INDIVIDUAL.**

Civilization gets no credit for resemblance. It must earn every stronger claim.

### Put Civilization on Trial

The question is not whether civilization is complicated, connected, energetic, intelligent in parts, or increasingly automated.

It is:

> **Does contemporary human civilization satisfy enough measurable criteria of higher-order individuality to justify treating it as one emerging individual rather than as an ecology, network, or collection of interacting agents?**

> **THE HIGHER-ORDER INDIVIDUAL MODEL BEARS THE BURDEN OF PROOF.**

### What Are We Testing?

Civilization has no obvious single boundary.

Humans alone exclude much of the infrastructure responsible for its distributed capabilities. Humans plus infrastructure raises new questions about agriculture, livestock, soils, mines, reservoirs, managed ecosystems, energy sources, and waste sinks. A still wider Earth-system boundary includes processes that do not inherit human agency merely because humans interact with them.

For this chapter, the primary candidate is **global technical-social civilization**: humans, institutions, information systems, infrastructure, and major organized material and energy networks connecting them.

The boundary remains provisional.

> **THE RESULT OF A TEST IS CONDITIONAL ON THE BOUNDARY BEING TESTED.**

### Pattern, Organization, Individual

The hierarchy is now explicit:

**MACROSCOPIC PATTERN** — a large-scale regularity exists.

**MACROSCOPIC ORGANIZATION** — relationships among components causally sustain reproducible system-level functions or states.

**HIGHER-ORDER INDIVIDUALITY** — the proposed whole earns substantially stronger boundary, integration, persistence, conflict-control, causal, and other properties justifying treatment as an individual.

Evolutionary history shows that individuality can move upward. It does not show that every increasingly integrated network is undergoing such a transition.

Individuality is also plural: evolutionary, physiological, developmental, immunological, and functional criteria need not coincide.

The Eleven Tests are therefore a **proposed synthesis for model comparison in this project**, not a consensus definition of life or individuality. They are not assumed to be independent and must not be added into an organism score.

### Four Models Enter

**Model A — Independent Agents + Shared Environment**  
Large-scale patterns arise from interacting agents responding to shared conditions.

**Model B — Coordinated Institutions**  
Organizations create stable supra-individual capabilities without requiring civilization to be one individual.

**Model C — Distributed Technical-Social Macroscopic Organization**  
Relationships among humans, institutions, infrastructure, information systems, and material networks create reproducible system-level organization.

**Model D — Emerging Higher-Order Individual**  
Civilization has developed enough integrated higher-level properties to justify treating it as one emerging individual.

> **DO NOT PROMOTE MODEL C TO MODEL D BY ANALOGY.**

Now test them.

### 1 — Boundary Perturbation

Civilization crosses political, communication, energy, ecological, and supply-chain boundaries. Particular boundaries can be perturbed, but no single line currently separates inside from outside across every relevant flow.

A fuzzy boundary can be real. A boundary silently redrawn for every desired result cannot support a stable individuality claim.

**Result: BOUNDARY-DEPENDENT / MIXED.**

### 2 — Integration Ablation

Remove communication, electricity, transport, ports, finance, supply chains, or technical standards and modern societies lose capabilities.

Organization plainly matters.

But networks, ecosystems, federations, and institutions also lose function when relationships are removed.

**Result: STRONG FOR MANY BOUNDED SUBSYSTEMS; MODERATE AT CIVILIZATION SCALE; INSUFFICIENT FOR INDIVIDUALITY.**

### 3 — Conflict Suppression

Civilization has laws, contracts, courts, diplomacy, standards, regulation, norms, and security systems that constrain some destabilizing behavior.

It also contains war, corruption, sabotage, misinformation, arms races, exploitation, and collective-action failures.

Conflict does not disprove individuality—organisms contain internal conflict too—but the strength and scale of conflict control matter.

**Result: MODERATE / MIXED.**

### 4 — Energetic Organization

Civilization moves enormous measurable quantities of energy and matter through agriculture, industry, cities, grids, transport, extraction, manufacturing, and waste systems.

The flows are real. Their organization is real.

Calling them the metabolism of one civilization-scale organism is a stronger claim not established by flow accounting alone.

**Result: STRONG FOR BOUNDED SUBSYSTEMS / MODERATE AT CIVILIZATION SCALE / ORGANISMAL IDENTITY NOT ESTABLISHED.**

### 5 — Memory Turnover

Laws, languages, archives, mathematics, institutions, databases, technical standards, software, roads, and buildings can persist beyond the people who created them.

Many bounded systems therefore display functional memory across component turnover.

Civilization-scale ownership of that memory remains boundary-dependent.

**Result: STRONG INFORMATION PERSISTENCE / STRONG FUNCTIONAL MEMORY IN MANY BOUNDED SYSTEMS / CIVILIZATION-SCALE ATTRIBUTION UNRESOLVED.**

### 6 — Prediction Advantage

Weather, epidemiology, logistics, utilities, agriculture, engineering, and other systems make prospective predictions that can be scored and can improve preparation.

Those are genuine anticipatory capabilities of bounded systems.

They do not establish one civilization-scale predictor.

**Result: STRONG FOR MANY BOUNDED SUBSYSTEMS / NOT ESTABLISHED AS ONE CIVILIZATION-SCALE PREDICTOR.**

### 7 — Repair Autonomy

Power lines are rebuilt. Networks reroute. Databases restore. Cities rebuild. Supply chains find alternatives. Institutions appoint successors.

Much of this repair is performed by people and bounded organizations acting intentionally; some engineered systems also recover automatically.

Distributed repair capacity is real. Autonomous repair by civilization as one whole is much weaker.

**Result: MODERATE DISTRIBUTED REPAIR CAPACITY / WEAK WHOLE-LEVEL REPAIR.**

### 8 — Whole-Only Information

Network topology, flows, bottlenecks, distributions, and correlations can improve prediction in financial, epidemiological, infrastructure, and supply systems.

The test is not won by finding a large-scale correlation. The relational variable must improve prediction or intervention against a fair baseline.

**Result: STRONG EVIDENCE THAT RELATIONAL INFORMATION CAN MATTER / CIVILIZATION-AS-ONE-WHOLE CLAIM BOUNDARY-DEPENDENT.**

### 9 — Higher-Level Intervention

Macro variables such as topology, standards, institutional rules, routing architectures, or market rules can sometimes be manipulated in scientifically useful ways.

But changing many components and giving the bundle a macro name does not establish independent top-down force.

The higher-level variable must improve causal prediction or control relative to an appropriate lower-level model.

**Result: MODERATE / BOUNDARY-DEPENDENT.**

### 10 — Reproduction/Heredity

Civilizations spread institutions, technologies, languages, political forms, standards, and organizational templates.

Spread is not reproduction of the proposed whole.

Contemporary global civilization has no demonstrated civilization-level parent-offspring cycle, reproductive bottleneck, or population of descendant global civilizations undergoing heritable variation in civilization-level fitness.

Imagined off-world descendants are not current evidence.

**Result: WEAK / NOT ESTABLISHED.**

For evolutionary individuality, this is one of Model D's largest deficits.

### 11 — Model Competition

Now ask what Model D predicts that A, B, and especially C do not.

At present: not enough.

A stronger future result might include a stable candidate boundary predicting perturbation responses; whole-level repair not reducible to local organizations; reproducible conflict suppression organized around persistence of the proposed whole; civilization-level heredity; or macro variables that repeatedly outperform rich lower-level alternatives prospectively.

Until then:

> **CURRENT EVIDENCE STRONGLY SUPPORTS MACROSCOPIC ORGANIZATION. IT DOES NOT YET REQUIRE HIGHER-ORDER INDIVIDUALITY.**

**CURRENT BEST MODEL: C.**

### Model C Is Not a Consolation Prize

The alternatives are not “one organism” or “nothing but isolated parts.”

An ecology can be highly organized without being one individual. A scientific community can preserve knowledge without becoming a person. A market can have system dynamics without possessing one mind. A technical network can route information without becoming a nervous system.

Civilization can therefore be scientifically real as a macroscopic organization and cognitive ecology without satisfying Model D.

That is a positive result.

### Is Civilization Becoming One?

Communication has accelerated. Information storage has expanded. Technical standards, supply chains, finance, transport, sensing, computation, scientific exchange, and digital networks have increased some dimensions of interdependence.

But increasing integration does not determine what the integrated system is becoming.

> **MORE CONNECTED OVER TIME ≠ MORE INDIVIDUAL OVER TIME UNLESS THE VARIABLES THAT DEFINE INDIVIDUALITY ALSO CHANGE.**

AI may accelerate integration, automate portions of prediction and control, or increase fragmentation, concentration, correlated failure, and conflict. Its effect on the trajectory is empirical.

### The Verdict

Civilization is not merely a pattern.

It contains genuine macroscopic organization: persistent institutions, distributed sensing, long-lived information, infrastructure dependence, relational information, prediction, material and energy networks, conflict-management mechanisms, and repair capacity.

But the strongest evidence is uneven and often strongest in subsystems. Civilization lacks a clearly demonstrated unified boundary, coherent civilization-scale action policy, strong whole-level conflict suppression, autonomous whole-level repair architecture, reproductive bottleneck, and civilization-level heredity.

Most importantly, Model D does not yet explain enough beyond Model C.

> **Civilization is an unusually integrated macroscopic organization and cognitive ecology. Whether it is moving toward a new level of individuality remains an open question.**

The affirmative case has now gone as far as this book can responsibly take it.

Good.

A theory that survives only because its author refuses to let it lose is not a theory.

So Act V stops helping Macroscopic Life.

> **If Macroscopic Life is real, it must survive the case against it.**
