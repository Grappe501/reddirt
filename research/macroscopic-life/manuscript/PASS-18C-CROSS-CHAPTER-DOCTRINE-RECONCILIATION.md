## Doctrine 1 — Observer limitation

Canonical qualification:

`observer-relative detectability ≠ evidence for the hidden interpretation`

Spatial, temporal, sensory, instrumental, and boundary mismatch can hide a phenomenon. They cannot establish what kind of phenomenon has been hidden.

This doctrine is consistent from Chapters 1–2 through Chapter 16.

**Reconciliation status: PASS.**

`interesting property inside circle → circle declared individual → property used to prove circle`

That is circular.

# 3. HIGHER-LEVEL CAUSATION

The book does not require a mysterious downward force.

Canonical formulation:

> **Higher-level causation requires no new physical force.**

Every higher-level state is physically implemented through components, interactions, fields, flows, histories, constraints, and environment.

The scientific question is whether a higher-level variable captures stable causal organization that provides prospective prediction, explanatory compression, intervention guidance, or generalization across implementations that a fair rival does not recover equally well.

Therefore:

`higher-level causal relevance ≠ violation of lower-level physics`

and

`lower-level implementation ≠ automatic elimination of higher-level explanation`

The book must reject both extremes:

1. **mystical emergence:** the whole acquires unexplained forces;
2. **automatic reduction:** because components physically implement the phenomenon, every higher-level description is scientifically dispensable.

Chapter 5 establishes the core logic. Chapters 7–16 consistently inherit it.

# 4. REPLACEMENT TEST / REMOVE-THE-WHOLE DOCTRINE

The same test appears under several property-specific forms: remove the higher-level goal, remove the chooser, remove the individual, compare the higher-level reproduction model with reconstruction, and so on.

Canonical general form:

> **If the higher-level variable can be removed and the strongest fair lower-level/environmental account preserves the relevant prediction, explanation, and intervention performance, the stronger higher-level interpretation has not been demonstrated.**

This is the canonical **Replacement Test**.

Property-specific versions remain legitimate and should not be renamed away.

# 5. PREDICTION ADVANTAGE — FINAL CANONICAL STANDARD

The book begins with the generic form:

`PA = Performance(H) - Performance(B)`

Later chapters correctly specialize it:

- `PA_memory`
- `PA_anticipation`
- `PA_goal`
- `PA_choice`
- `PA_individual`
- `PA_reproduction`
- `PA_inheritance`
- `PA_selection`
- `PA_transition`

These are not separate doctrines. They are applications of one comparison standard.

## Four-part Prediction Advantage requirement

Every final use of Prediction Advantage inherits all four conditions:

1. **Prospective target** — prediction target, boundary, and key property variable are specified before the confirmation outcome, with discovery and confirmation separated.
2. **Fair Rival Resources** — serious rivals receive comparable relevant information, history, model capacity, tuning opportunity, and access to justified nonlinear interactions/latent structure.
3. **Out-of-sample evaluation** — performance is evaluated on held-out or genuinely new times, sites, disturbances, implementations, lineages, or regimes as appropriate.
4. **Visible uncertainty** — uncertainty, calibration, and relevant error structure remain visible rather than reducing the comparison to one favorable point estimate.

Canonical interpretation:

`PA > 0` = demonstrated predictive advantage **under that comparison**.

It does **not** by itself establish:
- causation;
- ownership;
- individuality;
- agency;
- consciousness;
- ontological independence;
- historical origin.

`PA ≤ 0` = no demonstrated prediction advantage for H over B under that test.

This does not mean H is impossible. It means the claimed predictive advantage was not earned.

## Prediction vs intervention

Canonical:

> **Prediction Advantage and Intervention Advantage are related but not interchangeable.**

A variable may predict because it is a proxy. Intervention or converging causal evidence can distinguish predictive association from causal organization.

Ancient or ethically inaccessible systems may not permit direct intervention; comparative, historical, phylogenetic, natural-experiment, and mechanistic triangulation can then carry more burden.

**Reconciliation status: PASS WITH FOUR-PART STANDARD LOCKED GLOBALLY.**

# 8. INFORMATION DOCTRINE

The opening and Chapters 7–9 distinguish several uses of information.

Canonical categories for Book One:

1. **Statistical information** — measurable dependence that reduces uncertainty under a defined statistical relation.
2. **Functional information** — a physical difference is selectively used within a causal/regulatory architecture to affect a specified function.
3. **Semantic interpretation** — a stronger claim about what a signal or state is *about* or means for a system; requires additional justification and must not be imported from the scientist's labels.

Canonical warnings:

`correlation ≠ communication`

`frequency ≠ signal`

`resonance ≠ communication`

`synchronization ≠ coordination automatically`

`prediction by observer ≠ predictive control by system`

`human label ≠ system meaning`

Chapter 7 shorthand:

> **Information can exist for the scientist before it exists for the system.**

must be interpreted carefully. Statistical dependence can be physically real independent of a scientist. The intended contrast is that a scientist can **discover/use predictive information** before evidence shows that the system itself functionally uses that variable as a signal or control input.

Chapter 9's stronger wording correctly sharpens this:

> **PREDICTIVE INFORMATION CAN EXIST FOR THE OBSERVER BEFORE PREDICTIVE CONTROL EXISTS FOR THE SYSTEM.**

Final assembly should prefer the Chapter 9 precision where the distinction matters.

**Reconciliation status: PASS WITH SHORTHAND QUALIFICATION.**
