# P1 — Opening signature conflicts slightly with agency pluralism

Current:

> **SELECTION IS REAL. AGENCY HAS NOT YET BEEN EARNED.**

Problem:
The next paragraph correctly explains that serious frameworks attribute agency to minimal autonomous systems using criteria other than deliberative choice. Therefore “agency has not yet been earned” can sound as though this book is imposing its own universal agency threshold.

Repair options:

Preferred:

> **SELECTION IS REAL. HIGHER-LEVEL CHOICE HAS NOT YET BEEN EARNED.**

Alternative:

> **SELECTION IS REAL. OWNERSHIP HAS NOT YET BEEN EARNED.**

The preferred version is more explicit and aligns with Pass 12B's correction.

Severity: P1 HIGH.

# P2 — Define “choice” as project property earlier and once

The draft says the chapter asks when selection among physically realizable courses belongs causally to the proposed whole. Good, but the operational target should be stated in one clean sentence immediately after agency pluralism.

Repair:

> In this chapter, *higher-level choice* means a narrower testable claim: that a proposed whole contributes causally to selection among physically realizable response trajectories in a way that survives serious lower-level, environmental, stochastic, and alternative-boundary rivals.

Label this explicitly as project operationalization, not universal terminology.

Severity: P1 HIGH.

# P3 — Selector “alternatives are physically real” needs precision

A two-route circuit can have two physically possible outputs, but only one may be reachable under a given exact state/input. The chapter later handles nearby physically realizable conditions correctly.

Repair opening wording:

> The circuit contains more than one physically realizable route across its operating conditions.

Avoid implying metaphysical alternative possibilities at one identical complete microstate.

Severity: P1 MODERATE.

# P8 — “Eventually the swarm commits” is acceptable but commitment/departure mechanisms should be separated

Do not imply quorum alone mechanically flies the swarm to the site.
