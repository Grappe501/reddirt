# ENDNOTE [1] — CELLULAR MEMORY / FEEDBACK / HYSTERESIS

Verified source:

Yanfei Jiang & Nan Hao (2021). “Memorizing environmental signals through feedback and feedforward loops.” *Current Opinion in Cell Biology* 69: 96–102. DOI: 10.1016/j.ceb.2020.11.008. PMID 33549848. PMCID PMC8058236.

Verification status: VERIFIED.

What the source supports strongly:
- cells can retain information about previous environmental conditions;
- retained cellular states can change future responses;
- network motifs including negative feedback, positive feedback, and feedforward loops can underlie cellular memory;
- positive feedback can support bistability/hysteresis and priming;
- cellular memory can be implemented by physical regulatory dynamics rather than neurons.

What it does NOT by itself support:
- the Macroscopic Life five-stage memory ladder;
- the higher-level Memory Ownership Test;
- a universal definition of memory;
- ecosystem memory ownership;
- the general claim that any state surviving arbitrary material turnover counts as memory.

Sentence-level disposition:

“Biochemical feedback can preserve a state after the original trigger has disappeared.” — SUPPORTED.

“In biological literature, some of these persistent bistable or hysteretic states are themselves legitimately described as forms of cellular memory.” — SUPPORTED.

“Cellular and epigenetic memory research gives us concrete examples of organized states maintained despite material turnover.” — PARTIALLY SUPPORTED BY [1]. The cellular-state portion is supported; the explicit epigenetic/turnover claim deserves a separate verified epigenetic-memory source if retained in that form.

Repair instruction: do not make [1] carry the entire turnover paragraph. Add a dedicated source or narrow the sentence.

# ENDNOTE [2] — ADAPTIVE IMMUNE MEMORY

Verified source:

Nora Lam, YoonSeung Lee & Donna L. Farber (2024). “A guide to adaptive immune memory.” *Nature Reviews Immunology* 24(11): 810–829. DOI: 10.1038/s41577-024-01040-6. PMID 38831162. Published online 3 June 2024; issue November 2024.

Verification status: VERIFIED; working endnote metadata can now be completed.

What the source supports strongly:
- immune memory includes T cells, B cells, plasma cells, and secreted antibodies;
- antigen-experienced immune states differ from antigen-inexperienced states;
- memory T-cell responses can activate/proliferate and produce effector molecules faster and at greater magnitude than naive cells;
- re-exposure can produce rapid/effective pathogen clearance.

Manuscript claim:
“When the adaptive immune system encounters an antigen, the response can alter populations and states of B cells, T cells, plasma cells, antibodies, and related immune machinery. After the immediate episode ends, some of those consequences persist. A later encounter can therefore produce a response different from the response of an antigen-inexperienced system.”

Disposition: SUPPORTED, with one wording refinement. Antibodies are secreted products rather than a “population” analogous to cells. During repair use “populations of B cells, T cells and plasma cells, together with antibodies and other retained immune states/effects” or equivalent.

Project inference “Memory does not require consciousness.” — defensible conceptual inference from established nonconscious immune memory, but do not present Lam et al. as a philosophical authority for the general proposition.

# ENDNOTE [3] — CRISPR-CAS ADAPTIVE IMMUNE MEMORY

Verified source:

Peter C. Fineran & Emmanuelle Charpentier (2012). “Memory of viral infections by CRISPR-Cas adaptive immune systems: acquisition of new information.” *Virology* 434(2): 202–209. DOI: 10.1016/j.virol.2012.10.003. PMID 23123013.

Verification status: VERIFIED.

What the source supports strongly:
- CRISPR-Cas adaptive immune systems can acquire new spacer information from invasive genetic elements;
- acquired spacer sequence provides a physical record relevant to later defense;
- the system is appropriately discussed in memory terminology;
- retained sequence information contributes to sequence-specific subsequent immunity.

Manuscript claim:
“In CRISPR-Cas adaptive immune systems, sequence derived from invading genetic material can be incorporated into CRISPR arrays. That retained sequence can later contribute to sequence-specific defense when matching genetic material appears again.”

Disposition: SUPPORTED.

Required scientific wording repair from 9E-1 still applies: “some bacteria and archaea with CRISPR-Cas adaptive immune systems” is safer than wording that could imply universality.

Do not infer:
- autobiographical recollection;
- cognition;
- agency;
- semantic representation in the strong psychological sense.

# ENDNOTE [4] — PLANT STRESS PRIMING / EPIGENETIC CAUTION

Verified source:

C. Jake Harris, Anna Amtmann & Jurriaan Ton (2023). “Epigenetic processes in plant stress priming: Open questions and new approaches.” *Current Opinion in Plant Biology* 75: 102432. DOI: 10.1016/j.pbi.2023.102432. PMID 37523900.

Verification status: VERIFIED. Working draft currently shortens first author to “Harris, C. J.”; final bibliography should preserve published author form consistently according to chosen style.

What the source supports strongly:
- priming can retain effects of prior environmental stress and improve response to recurring stress;
- epigenetic modifications are candidates for short- and long-term stress memory;
- transgenerational effects are discussed;
- evidence that particular epigenetic modifications causally carry memory remains largely circumstantial in important cases;
- targeted reverse-epigenetic manipulation is a route to stronger causal testing.

Manuscript claim:
“Prior environmental stress can alter later plant responses, and researchers use terms such as stress priming and stress memory for some of these effects. But the phenomenon and the proposed storage mechanism must be separated.”

Disposition: STRONGLY SUPPORTED.

Manuscript claim:
“Evidence that prior stress changes later response can be strong while evidence that a particular chromatin state, methylation pattern, or other epigenetic mechanism carries the relevant memory remains case-dependent.”

Disposition: SUPPORTED and appropriately restrained.

Required 9E-1 repair: add explicit firewall that within-generation priming does not establish transgenerational memory.

# ENDNOTE [5] — ANEURAL HABITUATION

Verified source:

Romain P. Boisseau, David Vogel & Audrey Dussutour (2016). “Habituation in non-neural organisms: evidence from slime moulds.” *Proceedings of the Royal Society B: Biological Sciences* 283(1829): 20160446. DOI: 10.1098/rspb.2016.0446. PMID 27122563. PMCID PMC4855389.

Verification status: VERIFIED. The draft's “DOI to verify” flag can be removed.

What the source supports:
- experiments in *Physarum polycephalum* interpreted by the authors as habituation;
- investigation of simple learning/habituation-like phenomena in a non-neural organism;
- a legitimate empirical basis for discussing whether learning-like behavior requires neurons.

What the source does NOT settle:
- that all accepted criteria for learning are satisfied beyond dispute;
- that every response decrement is habituation;
- that single-cell/aneural cognition is established generally;
- mechanism of the retained state;
- higher-level agency or consciousness.

Manuscript wording:
“has been interpreted as evidence that some hallmarks of simple learning can occur without neurons.”

Disposition: APPROPRIATELY CAUTIOUS and supported.

The following paragraph listing fatigue, adaptation, depletion, damage and drift is a conceptual rival list and should not be attributed solely to Boisseau et al. A general habituation/learning-criteria source would strengthen that paragraph if it survives final prose.

# ENDNOTE [6] — ECOLOGICAL MEMORY / FOREST RESILIENCE

Verified source:

Jill F. Johnstone, Craig D. Allen, Jerry F. Franklin, Lee E. Frelich, Brian J. Harvey, Philip E. Higuera, Michelle C. Mack, Ross K. Meentemeyer, Margaret R. Metz, George L. W. Perry, Tania Schoennagel & Monica G. Turner (2016). “Changing disturbance regimes, ecological memory, and forest resilience.” *Frontiers in Ecology and the Environment* 14(7): 369–378. DOI: 10.1002/fee.1311.

Verification status: VERIFIED; volume, issue and page range can now be completed.

What the source supports strongly:
- ecological memory is established ecological terminology;
- ecological memory is central to ecosystem disturbance response in the review's framework;
- the authors distinguish information legacies and material legacies;
- seeds, nutrients, biotic/abiotic structures and species life-history traits can contribute to disturbance legacies;
- ecological memory can influence resilience and post-disturbance dynamics.

Manuscript claim:
“Ecology already uses the term ecological memory for ways antecedent conditions, disturbances, material legacies, biological traits, community composition, and other historical factors influence present or future ecological responses.”

Disposition: SUPPORTED IN SUBSTANCE. “Community composition” is consistent with ecological-memory literature broadly, but Johnstone et al. should not be made to carry every listed category unless the final sentence is slightly generalized.

Recommended repair:
“Ecology already uses ecological memory for ways antecedent conditions and biological or material legacies influence later ecological responses.” Then examples can follow with appropriate sourcing.

Project claim:
“Ecological memory is real terminology. Ecological legacy effects are real scientific targets. Neither fact establishes forest-level individuality.”

Disposition: first two clauses supported; final clause is project reasoning and should remain visibly separate from the citation rather than appear sourced from Johnstone et al.
