# Executive hostile finding

The chapter's strongest achievement is that it refuses two opposite errors at once:

1. memory must require a brain;
2. every persistent consequence of history is memory.

The chapter also survives the most important Macroscopic Life attack:

A forest can contain organisms, soils, populations, and structures with history-dependent states without thereby becoming one remembering individual.

The strongest unresolved vulnerability is not the forest. It is the definition of **functional memory**. As currently written, “re-enter an identified process in a way that changes function” can still sweep ordinary state dependence into the memory category unless the project is explicit that this is a deliberately stronger operational usage and that the retained state must earn explanatory/experimental work rather than merely participate causally.

That repair is surgical, not architectural.

# P1 — required scientific repairs

## P1-1 — Scar physiology claim is unnecessarily specific

Current opening says scar tissue may “stretch differently, feel different, or respond differently to another injury.” The first two are intuitively safe, but the third invites a biomedical support burden that the chapter does not need.

Repair: reduce to structural/mechanical difference or cite an appropriate wound-healing source. Prefer reduction because the scar is a conceptual control, not a medical case study.

## P1-2 — Trace/retention language must avoid universal definition

The chapter presents trace and retention cleanly, but the transition can sound like a universal definition of memory research.

Repair: reinforce once, early, that the ladder is the **Macroscopic Life operational ladder for stronger system-level claims**, not a replacement for discipline-specific usages.

Do not repeat this caveat throughout the chapter.

## P1-3 — Hysteresis paragraph is scientifically good but rhetorically ambiguous

The draft correctly says some bistable/hysteretic states are legitimately called cellular memory. Good.

Vulnerability: “does not by itself establish the stronger memory claim” needs to name what stronger means: evidence relevant to functional/selective use and higher-level ownership, not a declaration that accepted cellular-memory usage is weak or wrong.

Repair: specify the evidentiary target.

## P1-4 — “Story about its history” risks representation smuggling

Line:

> A system can carry its history forward without carrying a story about its history.

This is elegant but could imply that stronger memory requires representation or a “story.” That contradicts the chapter's own non-neural/nonconscious architecture.

Repair: either remove or immediately clarify that functional memory does not require explicit representation of the past. Do not let this become a signature line.

## P1-5 — Adaptive-memory definition must not equate function with evolutionary adaptation

“Adaptive memory” is defined as history-dependent response improving an independently specified functional outcome. The term *adaptive* can be read as evolutionary fitness/adaptation.

Repair: explicitly state that project usage means performance/regulatory advantage under a specified test, not necessarily an evolved adaptation and not necessarily increased Darwinian fitness.

## P1-6 — Immune-memory wording needs one precision repair

The phrase “B cells, T cells, plasma cells, antibodies, and related immune machinery” is broadly reasonable but mixes cells, products, and mechanisms under “populations and states.”

Repair: separate persistent memory-cell populations/states and circulating/secreted products where relevant. Keep prose accessible.

## P1-7 — CRISPR must avoid implying all bacteria/archaea possess adaptive CRISPR memory

Current “Bacteria and archaea can make the point” is acceptable, but ensure the next sentence stays explicitly limited to organisms with CRISPR-Cas adaptive immune systems.

Repair: tiny wording check only.

## P1-8 — “Memory does require…” is too universal

Line:

> It does require a way for the past to remain causally available.

This is philosophically strong and likely defensible for physical memory, but because “memory” spans many disciplines, frame as the project's physical requirement:

For the kinds of memory claims examined here, the past must remain causally available through a physical state or reconstructible process.

This avoids pretending to settle every philosophical definition of memory.

## P1-9 — Physical-substrate list includes “bioelectric state” without Chapter 8-specific source

Bioelectric state is plausible and consistent with the larger project, but in this chapter it appears in a specialist substrate list without direct grounding in the current source register.

Repair options:
1. source it during 9F; or
2. remove it from this particular list.

Short-bow recommendation: remove unless it becomes narratively important later.

## P1-10 — State-only comparison needs stronger philosophical precision

Current:

> Does it make prospective predictions that a fair rival description misses?

Good, but the chapter must not imply that a “memory model” should outperform a literally complete physical microstate. Pass 9B already repaired this.

Repair: explicitly compare against a **fair practical rival representation/model at the relevant explanatory scale**, with equivalent access to measured current variables. The advantage sought is compression, intervention leverage, cross-realization stability, or prospective prediction—not metaphysical information absent from the present.

## P1-11 — Functional memory remains too permissive

Current ladder:

> A retained difference may later re-enter an identified process in a way that changes function; call that functional memory...

Problem: a scar can re-enter biomechanics and change function. Soil compaction can re-enter hydrology and change function. This threatens to re-admit the false positives the opening excluded.

Required repair: functional memory in the **strong project usage** should require a retained state to participate in an identified regulatory/response architecture in a way for which history-sensitive organization adds explanatory or intervention value beyond merely describing damage or altered material properties.

Do not demand consciousness, symbolic representation, or adaptation.

This is the most important repair in Pass 9E.

## P1-12 — Selectivity must not be mandatory for all legitimate memory

The draft mostly handles this by presenting a ladder. Preserve that. Avoid sentences that imply generic memory is invalid unless different pasts are discriminable.

Repair: say selectivity strengthens the case and is required for the project's **Selective Memory** level, not for every scientific usage of memory.

## P1-13 — “Wounded rather than informed” overstates information distinction

Line:

> If every past event leaves the same kind of weakness, the system may be wounded rather than informed.

Good rhetoric, but “informed” can imply semantic information.

Repair: “the system may be carrying nonspecific damage rather than a selective retained difference.” Less lyrical, more exact. If the original line survives, surround it with precision.

## P1-14 — Plant priming section needs explicit mechanism restraint retained

This is already one of the best scientific passages. Do not accidentally strengthen it during revision.

Permanent requirement: prior stress → later altered response can be supported while a proposed epigenetic storage mechanism remains unproven/case-dependent.

## P1-15 — Slime-mold passage must remain explicitly contested

The draft does this well. Source verification still required. Do not upgrade “interpreted as evidence” into “demonstrates learning.”

Also distinguish habituation-like behavior from general cognition.

## P1-16 — Microbial selection example needs inherited resistance caveat

The selection example says perhaps no individual learned anything. Correct.

Add that resistance may pre-exist, arise genetically, or reflect other heritable/physiological variation depending on the experimental system. The example is conceptual and should not imply one universal microbial mechanism.

## P1-17 — Population-level variable passage risks scale reification

Line:

> If a stable population-level variable survives turnover...

A variable does not literally survive; a property/pattern can persist or be reconstructed.

Repair: say the measured population-level property remains stable/reconstructs through turnover and predicts/manipulates collective response.

## P1-18 — Turnover paragraph overuses citation [1]

Jiang & Hao supports feedback/cellular memory but may not be the best source for broad epigenetic inheritance across cell division/material turnover.

Repair: verify and insert a dedicated turnover/cellular-memory source during 9F. Pass 9B already flagged a 2025 epigenetic feedback review; verify exact metadata before use. Do not rely on a provisional citation.

## P1-19 — Signature candidate must be interpreted carefully

> MEMORY NEED NOT PRESERVE THE MATERIAL. IT MUST PRESERVE THE CAUSAL DIFFERENCE.

Strong enough to keep.

But “causal difference” cannot mean any persistent consequence or the scar qualifies again.

Repair immediately after signature: for the stronger project claim, the preserved/reconstructed difference must remain available to the relevant response/regulatory architecture and do measurable work at the claimed level.

## P1-20 — Forest drought paragraph contains unsupported mechanistic specifics

“Fungal relationships changed” and some other items are plausible but hypothetical. Because the forest is explicitly hypothetical, they are allowed as candidate changes, but the prose should not sound like every severe drought necessarily causes each listed effect.

Repair: use “could/might” for candidate legacies.

## P1-21 — Ecological memory terminology needs exact source definition

The draft is appropriately humble, but final prose should track Johnstone et al. closely enough not to broaden the term beyond the source. Avoid implying ecology has one universal definition.

Repair: “Ecologists use ecological memory in several related ways…” then give antecedent/legacy example.

## P1-22 — Project novelty wording needs one additional brake

“What Macroscopic Life adds…” risks priority claim.

The stricter ownership test may be project synthesis, but similar level/causal questions exist elsewhere in philosophy of biology and complex-systems science.

Repair: “What Macroscopic Life asks here is a narrower question…” rather than asserting historical novelty.

## P1-23 — Ownership test must remain noncircular

Current wording is good: boundary independently earned before memory ownership.

Strengthen once: memory evidence may test an already proposed boundary, but cannot be the sole evidence used to define that boundary and then confirm it.

## P1-24 — “regulation attributable to a higher-level organization” needs operational consequence

This phrase can become hand-waving.

Repair: tie attribution to selective perturbation, alternative-boundary comparison, cross-realization/turnover stability, and Prediction Advantage. The later paragraph already does this; tighten the first use so the definition points forward.

## P1-25 — Experimental erasure language must acknowledge feasibility

“Can it be erased, transplanted, reconstructed…” is appropriately interrogative, but not every memory substrate permits literal erasure/transplantation.

Repair: “where feasible” and use independent perturbation/disruption as general requirement.

## P1-26 — Natural disturbance design needs causal caution

The chapter allows natural disturbances “strong enough to compare exposed and control systems.” Observational/natural experiments can have confounding.

Repair: mention matched controls, pre-disturbance data where available, and explicit confound modeling; do not imply natural exposure equals randomized intervention.

## P1-27 — “same conditions later” is excellent; preserve

No repair. This is one of the chapter's strongest methodological lines:

> “Same conditions later” is something the experiment must earn.

Keep, likely prose-level rather than signature typography.

## P1-28 — Current-state rival outcome wording needs repair

Line:

> perhaps the historical variable ... disappears when present state is measured properly.

This can imply historical information literally vanishes.

Repair: say its **additional explanatory/predictive value** disappears once relevant present-state variables are included.

## P1-29 — “Model A/B/C” outcome paragraph must stay property-specific

Current wording is safe. Preserve exactly the rule that model classification follows the property actually established.

Do not make “higher-level memory” synonymous with Model C in revision.

## P1-30 — Anticipation bridge needs circadian precision

“Plants can alter physiology around predictable day-night rhythms” is broadly true but will require a source if retained as an empirical claim.

More importantly, entrainment can produce phase-appropriate behavior without a system predicting a future event in a cognitive sense.

The draft says this. Preserve and sharpen it.
