# Chapter 7 — The Body Electric

Take a grain of table salt and dissolve it in water.

The crystal disappears, but its charged components do not.

Table salt is sodium chloride. In water, it separates into sodium ions and chloride ions. Sodium ions carry positive charge. Chloride ions carry negative charge.

Your cells live in water containing charged particles too.

Sodium. Potassium. Chloride. Calcium. And others.

This is where the body's electricity begins—not with a mysterious force, but with ordinary matter.

An **ion** is an atom or molecule with a net electric charge. Ions move through watery environments. They are attracted or repelled by electrical forces. Their movement is also affected by differences in concentration.

Put a thin biological membrane between two solutions, control which ions can cross it, and something remarkable becomes possible.

A living cell can maintain an electrical difference across its boundary.

**Electricity in the body is ordinary physics organized by living systems.**

## A Voltage Across a Living Boundary

A cell membrane is extraordinarily thin, but it separates two chemically different environments.

The membrane is not equally permeable to everything.

Proteins embedded within it create selective routes across the boundary.

**Ion channels** are protein pathways through which particular ions can move when the channels are open. Which direction an ion tends to move depends on its **electrochemical gradient**—the combined effects of concentration differences and electrical forces.

Other membrane proteins act as pumps and transporters. Some directly or indirectly use cellular energy to move ions or other solutes and to establish or maintain gradients.

Those gradients matter.

Suppose potassium is more concentrated on one side of a membrane than the other. Chemical forces tend to favor movement down that concentration difference. But potassium carries positive charge, so electrical forces matter too. As charged particles move, the electrical conditions affecting further movement can change.

The balance is not simply chemical.

It is electrochemical.

The resulting difference in electric potential across a cell membrane is called the **membrane potential**.

This phrase invites an easy misconception.

A cell with a membrane potential does not contain a giant reservoir of negative charge surrounded by a giant reservoir of positive charge. The bulk fluids inside and outside remain close to electrically neutral overall. A comparatively small separation of charge near the membrane can produce a measurable voltage difference across that extremely thin boundary.

A battery offers a loose first comparison because separated charge can create electrical potential. But a living membrane is not literally a battery. It is a dynamic biological interface containing channels, pumps, transporters, receptors, lipids, structural attachments, and other machinery operating in a changing chemical environment.

The important point is simpler.

A living boundary can maintain an electrical state.

And maintaining it takes work.

## Life Maintains Gradients

Leave a cup of hot coffee on a table and its temperature moves toward that of the room.

Create a concentration difference and, when a pathway exists, random molecular motion tends to spread particles in ways that reduce that difference.

Living cells continually resist the passive loss of many biologically important gradients.

They do not violate physics to do this.

They use physics.

Chemical reactions release usable free energy. Molecular machinery couples that energy to transport, synthesis, motion, signaling, and other cellular work. Gradients are established, used, altered, and rebuilt.

A living organism is therefore an **open system**. Matter and energy cross its boundaries. Molecules enter and leave. Chemical bonds are made and broken. Heat is released. Structures are repaired. Concentration differences are maintained.

This is part of what scientists mean when they describe living systems as operating **far from thermodynamic equilibrium** or in **nonequilibrium states**.

The word *entropy* is sometimes translated casually as *disorder*. That shortcut can create more confusion than understanding. For our purposes, we need a narrower lesson: organized biological gradients and structures persist only because physical processes continually maintain them while energy is transformed and dissipated.

Stop maintaining the gradients and they tend to decay.

Stop maintaining the organization permanently and the living state does not remain indefinitely.

But this gives us another place to be careful.

A flame requires continual energy flow.

A hurricane does too.

Neither fact makes a flame or hurricane a biological organism.

> **Nonequilibrium thermodynamics helps explain the energetic cost of biological organization. It does not define life by itself.**

Energy flow is necessary to understand known life.

It is not a shortcut for identifying it.

## Neurons Are Not the Only Electrical Cells

Ask someone where electricity exists in the body and they will probably point to the brain, the nerves, or the heart.

They would not be wrong.

They would be incomplete.

Neurons use rapid changes in membrane voltage as part of signaling. Muscle cells use electrical activity as part of contraction. The coordinated electrical activity of cardiac tissue is essential to normal heartbeat.

But a neuron did not invent membrane potential.

Non-neural cells maintain electrical states too.

Changes in ion-channel activity and membrane voltage can interact with intracellular calcium, secretion, contraction, migration, metabolism, signaling pathways, and gene regulation. Which effects occur depends on the cell, tissue, developmental state, and physiological context.

This broader territory is often described with the word **bioelectricity**.

Bioelectricity is a broad term for electrical properties and processes in living systems, including membrane voltages, ionic currents, electrical coupling, and endogenous electric fields.

It is important precisely because it is not separate from the rest of biology.

Electrical state interacts with biochemical signaling.

Biochemical signaling changes ion channels and transporters.

Gene regulation changes which channels, receptors, pumps, and structural proteins are produced.

Mechanical forces can alter cell behavior and ion-channel activity.

Metabolism supplies energy required to maintain gradients.

Tissue geometry changes the physical context in which signals and forces operate.

The arrows run both ways.

Calling bioelectricity important does not make it a master code that independently controls anatomy.

And it does not make it a life force.

## Cells Can Couple

A cell does not always operate electrically in isolation.

In many animal tissues, neighboring cells can be connected by **gap junctions**—intercellular channels formed by proteins spanning the membranes of adjacent cells.

Gap junctions can permit ions and some small molecules to pass directly between neighboring cells.

That provides one route for direct electrical and chemical coupling.

The qualification matters.

Not every cell is coupled to every other cell. Coupling varies by tissue, developmental state, channel composition, and physiological context. Gap junctions can open, close, change in abundance, and participate differently in different tissues.

So we should not picture the body as one uniform electrical wire.

Picture something more interesting.

Local cells possess electrical states. Some neighboring cells can influence one another through defined physical connections. Those interactions occur alongside chemical signaling, mechanical forces, metabolism, gene regulation, extracellular structure, circulation, and other processes.

Coordination can therefore propagate without requiring one cell to know what the organism is doing as a whole.

That principle matters enormously for this book.

## A Wound Changes the Local Environment

Imagine a continuous sheet of epithelial cells.

Then cut it.

At our scale we describe the event in one sentence:

*The tissue is wounded.*

But there is no single object called *wound* floating down to the cellular scale to issue instructions.

The damage changes local conditions.

Cells are broken or displaced. Barriers are interrupted. Ions move differently. Chemical contents can be released. Mechanical tension changes. Surfaces that were covered become exposed. Local electrical conditions can change as well.

In some epithelial systems, measurable endogenous electric fields occur around wounds. Under experimental and physiological conditions, some cell types can migrate directionally in response to electric fields. This directional response is often called **electrotaxis** or **galvanotaxis**.

An electric field is not the same thing as an electric current.

An **electric field** describes how electric force would act on charge at different locations. An **electric current** is a net movement of electric charge. In biological tissue the two can be related, but they are not interchangeable terms.

More importantly, the electrical cue is not alone.

A wounded tissue presents cells with a changing combination of electrical, chemical, mechanical, structural, and metabolic conditions.

Responsive cells integrate those local conditions through the molecular machinery they possess.

Some move.

Some alter adhesion.

Some change secretion or signaling.

Some proliferate or change state, depending on the biological context.

Together, those local responses can contribute to tissue repair.

Notice what just happened conceptually.

At the organismal level, the function is **repair the wound**.

At the cellular level, there need not be a command that says those words.

There are altered local conditions and cells capable of responding to them.

> **A mechanism can be entirely local while the function it implements belongs to a larger organizational level.**

This is not metaphor.

It is ordinary multiscale biology.

## Electrical State Is Not Information by Default

This is where language can quietly outrun mechanism.

Suppose a cell's membrane voltage changes.

We have established a change in **electrical state**.

Suppose that voltage change alters the state of a voltage-sensitive channel, changes intracellular ion concentration, and causes the cell to secrete a molecule.

Now we have a defined causal chain.

Whether it is useful to call some part of that chain a **signal** or **information** depends on the biological system and the question being asked.

The distinctions matter:

**ELECTRICAL STATE ≠ SIGNAL ≠ INFORMATION ≠ INTEGRATION ≠ INTELLIGENCE ≠ AGENCY ≠ CONSCIOUSNESS**

A measurable voltage is evidence for a measurable voltage.

A reproducible cellular response is evidence for that response.

Coordinated activity is evidence for coordination.

Each additional claim requires additional evidence.

This rule will become increasingly important as we move upward in scale.

## Rhythms Without Magic

Electrical systems make another family of words unavoidable.

*Frequency.*

*Oscillation.*

*Synchronization.*

*Entrainment.*

*Resonance.*

These are real scientific concepts.

They are also words that become meaningless very quickly when detached from mechanisms.

An **oscillation** is a variable changing repeatedly over time.

**Frequency** is the rate at which a repeating cycle occurs.

**Phase** describes position within that cycle.

**Coupling** means that one system physically affects another.

**Synchronization** describes coordinated timing between oscillating systems.

**Entrainment** occurs when a rhythm becomes aligned with another rhythm or periodic forcing.

**Resonance** refers to conditions in which a system responds strongly to forcing because of the relationship among the forcing, the system's characteristic dynamics, coupling, and damping.

Biology is full of rhythms.

Heartbeats repeat. Circadian systems oscillate. Neural activity can exhibit oscillatory patterns. Biochemical networks can oscillate.

But the existence of biological rhythms does not establish a universal hidden communication field.

> **Frequency is a measurement, not an explanation.**

If someone proposes a biological effect based on frequency or resonance, ask ordinary questions.

What oscillates?

At what rate?

What are the system's characteristic dynamics?

What physically couples the systems?

How strong is the coupling?

What response should occur?

How will it be measured?

Does it reproduce?

Without those answers, *resonance* is a word looking for a mechanism.

> **A shared word is not a shared mechanism.**

## The Aura Boundary

Human bodies produce measurable physical effects beyond the skin.

They radiate heat.

They emit chemicals.

They generate sound and mechanical vibration.

Electrical currents associated with physiology can have measurable electromagnetic consequences.

Those phenomena are real.

Their existence does not automatically establish a personality-bearing aura, telepathy, emotional information transmitted through an unspecified frequency, or a hidden energetic body.

Chapter 2 gave us the rule already.

**Existence is not interpretation.**

Chapter 7 adds another.

**A shared word is not a shared mechanism.**

A measurable biological field establishes that field.

It does not transfer evidence to another proposed phenomenon merely because both are described with words such as *energy*, *field*, *vibration*, or *frequency*.

That distinction does not make biology less extraordinary.

It lets us see what is actually extraordinary.

## The Whole Without a Little Commander

We can now return to the question that ended Chapter 6.

If the parts of an organism cannot see the whole, what physical processes allow the whole to coordinate them?

We have found part of the answer.

Cells maintain gradients.

Membranes create regulated interfaces.

Electrical states change.

Ion channels respond.

Cells exchange molecules.

Some cells couple directly through gap junctions.

Chemical signals move.

Mechanical forces propagate.

Genes change their activity.

Metabolism changes what is possible.

Geometry constrains what happens next.

None of these is the organism's tiny commander.

Together they form interacting regulatory processes distributed across cells and tissues.

Organism-level function can emerge from those interactions.

This is not evidence that every connected network is an organism.

It is not evidence that civilization possesses a bioelectric field analogous to a body.

It is not evidence that electrical coordination produces consciousness.

It establishes something narrower and more useful.

Known biology already contains mechanisms through which components responding to local physical conditions participate in functions that belong to a larger biological individual.

The cell does not need to understand the body.

It needs mechanisms that connect its local state to the states and actions of other parts of the system.

And now the problem becomes stranger.

Because coordination is only the beginning.

Cells do not merely maintain a body that already exists.

They build one.

They do it without seeing the finished form.

They divide, migrate, adhere, signal, push, pull, change electrical state, change gene expression, and alter the environment encountered by neighboring cells.

From those local interactions, anatomy appears.

**Cells do not need to see the body they are building.**

They respond to local conditions—chemical, mechanical, electrical, spatial, and historical.

Yet from those local interactions, anatomy appears.
