## Purpose

Pass 18H defines the final reader-level compression rules without mutating the frozen chapter sources. The goal is not to make the manuscript shorter merely for the sake of word count. The goal is to remove the sensation that the book repeatedly stops to reteach scientific rules the reader has already learned.

The frozen science remains authoritative. This overlay governs only the publication reader layer.

## Governing rule

EARLY CHAPTERS TEACH THE METHOD. LATER CHAPTERS USE IT.

Once a doctrine has been fully established, later chapters should normally invoke it through a concise callback rather than restating the entire lesson. Repetition remains when it changes the scientific burden of the new property, performs narrative work, prevents a genuine category error, or is required for the chapter to stand independently enough for comprehension.

## Protected material

The following are not compression targets merely because they recur:

- the property-independence spine;
- fair-rival discipline;
- observer independence where it is directly relevant;
- the scientific value of Model B;
- Earth having to earn each property separately;
- explicit distinctions necessary to prevent one property from being substituted for another;
- chapter-specific Ownership Tests;
- chapter-specific failure conditions;
- the eight locked signature lines from the opening movement;
- frozen signature statements in Chapters 7–16;
- the final Chapter 16 aperture.

No compression may turn Model B into failure, collapse Model C across properties, imply new physics, turn temporal mismatch into evidence, or allow analogy to function as evidence.

## Compression targets

### 1. Prediction Advantage

Chapter 5 owns the full explanation of Prediction Advantage and its four requirements:

1. prospective target;
2. fair rival information/resources;
3. held-out or genuinely new conditions;
4. visible calibration/uncertainty.

Later property chapters may state the property-specific PA equation and use a short callback to the Chapter 5 discipline. They should not repeatedly re-explain all four requirements unless the property introduces a genuinely new complication.

### 2. Model A / B / C

The first full explanation remains intact. Later chapters retain their property-specific Model A/B/C outcomes, because failure architecture is scientifically important, but boilerplate explanations should be compressed. The line `MODEL B IS A DISCOVERY, NOT A CONSOLATION PRIZE.` remains available as a whole-book signature but should not feel mechanically inserted in every chapter.

### 3. Property independence

Retain the principle strongly, but vary its expression. Later chapters should not repeatedly list every unearned property. Prefer the smallest scientifically sufficient sentence: earning property X earns property X and nothing stronger.

### 4. Forest recurrence

The forest is the book's recurring experimental organism/candidate, not a chapter-reset device. From Chapters 7 onward, do not rebuild the entire forest premise. Begin each return at the new scientific problem. Preserve continuity by allowing previous experimental history to accumulate.

The forest should increasingly feel like one investigation becoming harder rather than sixteen separate hypothetical forests.

### 5. Earth recurrence

Earth remains a candidate and stress test. Its recurrence should escalate:

- early: perceptual and boundary temptation;
- middle: higher-level organization and property ownership;
- late: lineage, heredity, selection, and transition limitations.

Do not repeatedly restate the generic warning that Earth is not automatically alive. By the later chapters, state the specific reason the current property has or has not been earned.

### 6. No-new-physics disclaimer

Retain the full causal doctrine where introduced. Later chapters should use concise callbacks unless a passage could otherwise be reasonably misread as invoking a new force or nonphysical cause.

### 7. Anti-teleology

Do not repeatedly defend against intentional design after the goal/choice chapters have established the distinction. Chapter 16 still needs a direct evolutionary anti-progress/anti-purpose safeguard because `construction` and `transition` language creates a fresh risk.

### 8. Negative-definition lists

Long `X is not Y` sequences are useful when a property is introduced. Afterward, replace repeated catalogs with prose that identifies only the live rival in the current argument.

## Copyedit rules

- Paragraphs remain the primary reading unit.
- Avoid one-line paragraphs except for genuine signature or hinge moments.
- Preserve the book's conversational scientific voice.
- Prefer 4–8 sentence paragraphs where the argument naturally supports them.
- Do not create textbook-like bullet density in the reader manuscript.
- Remove production vocabulary from reader prose: `pass`, `prefreeze`, `gate count`, `artifact`, `build`, `canonical blob`, and similar terms belong outside the book unless the ordinary English word is required by the argument.
- Keep mathematical notation only where it improves precision rather than displaying rigor.
- Do not flatten memorable concrete examples into abstract summaries.
- Do not compress the opening movement so aggressively that wonder disappears before the scientific discipline is established.
- Do not compress Chapter 16's closing cadence.

### Chapters 13–16

Compression: strongest, but surgical.

By this point the reader knows how Macroscopic Life tests a claim. These chapters should feel like increasingly difficult applications of a mature method, not repeated introductions to it. Preserve the new scientific rivals unique to reproduction, inheritance, selection, and evolutionary transition. Compress generic PA instructions, generic A/B/C explanations, generic fair-rival language, generic Earth disclaimers, and repeated property-independence catalogs.

## Specific late-book seam instruction

The sequence

`individuality → reproduction → inheritance → selection → transition`

must read as one cumulative argument.

Each chapter should begin from what the previous chapter actually earned, then introduce exactly one new burden. It should not imply that the previous chapter proved the stronger property now under examination.

Chapter 13 asks whether a whole reproduces.
Chapter 14 asks whether higher-level differences survive lineage.
Chapter 15 asks whether those differences alter higher-level reproductive success.
Chapter 16 asks whether evolutionary history reorganizes individuality itself.

That cumulative staircase is more important than preserving repeated methodological boilerplate.

## Signature scarcity

Signature lines should function as memory anchors, not decoration. Bold standalone sentences should be retained when they mark a conceptual threshold, but ordinary explanatory sentences should be returned to paragraphs where possible. The reader should feel a signature because the argument earned it.

## Expected compression

Target: approximately 3–5% of reader-body prose, concentrated primarily in Chapters 7–16.

This is not a quota. If a deletion weakens scientific precision, causal continuity, narrative comprehension, or the emotional movement of the book, keep the language.
