# Overall verdict

Pass 11A survives, but several formulations must be tightened before narrative architecture.

The most important finding is that **goal-directedness and teleology are already legitimate technical terms in multiple scientific and philosophical traditions**, including organizational biology, cybernetics, dynamical-systems accounts, and developmental/regenerative biology. Macroscopic Life must not define those usages away merely to reserve “goal” for its strongest ownership claim.

Therefore the chapter's primary distinction should not be:

`attractor/regulation = not goal`

as a universal scientific definition.

Instead:

> **STABILITY, ATTRACTORS, HOMEOSTASIS, AND REGULATION CAN LEGITIMATELY SUPPORT GOAL-DIRECTED OR TELEONOMIC DESCRIPTIONS IN EXISTING LITERATURE. THEY DO NOT BY THEMSELVES ESTABLISH THE STRONGER SYSTEM-OWNED GOAL CLAIM MACROSCOPIC LIFE IS TESTING.**

This is analogous to Chapter 9's correction that circadian anticipation is real anticipation even when it does not establish flexible forecast ownership.

The architecture should therefore distinguish **legitimate goal-directed description** from **evidence for higher-level goal ownership**.

# 1. HOMEOSTASIS — STRONG POSITIVE CONTROL, NOT A FALSE CATEGORY

Walter Cannon's classic homeostasis framework and modern regulatory biology treat living systems as actively maintaining physiological variables. More recent reviews also distinguish homeostasis from rheostasis and allostasis, where regulated values themselves may shift across conditions.

Useful anchors:

- Cannon, W. B. (1929). “Organization for physiological homeostasis.” *Physiological Reviews* 9(3):399–431. DOI 10.1152/physrev.1929.9.3.399.
- Sterling, P. (2012). “Allostasis: a model of predictive regulation.” *Physiology & Behavior* 106(1):5–15. DOI 10.1016/j.physbeh.2011.06.004.
- Schulkin, J., & Sterling, P. (2019). “Allostasis: A Brain-Centered, Predictive Mode of Physiological Regulation.” *Trends in Neurosciences* 42(10):740–752. DOI 10.1016/j.tins.2019.07.010.
- “Defining the brain control of physiological stability.” *Hormones and Behavior* 164 (2024):105607. DOI 10.1016/j.yhbeh.2024.105607.

Scientific consequence:

Pass 11A is correct that exact scalar setpoints are too narrow. Modern physiology explicitly recognizes changing setpoints and hierarchical regulation.

Permanent restraint:

> **A GOAL CLAIM CANNOT REQUIRE RETURN TO ONE FIXED NUMBER.**

But the reverse restraint is equally important:

> **REGULATION CAN BE REAL BEFORE HIGHER-LEVEL GOAL OWNERSHIP IS ESTABLISHED.**

# 2. ATTRACTORS — DO NOT DEFINE THEM OUT OF GOAL-DIRECTEDNESS

Dynamical-systems and teleonomy literature sometimes interprets goals in terms of attractors or far-from-equilibrium attractor-like organization. Recent reviews of teleonomy explicitly connect equifinality, negative feedback, persistence, and attractors to forms of goal-directedness.

Relevant anchor:

- “Teleonomy in living systems: an overview.” *Biological Journal of the Linnean Society* 139(4):341–369 (2023).

That literature discusses equifinality and treats some goal-directed systems as interpretable through attractor dynamics.

Therefore Pass 11A's line:

`An attractor is a dynamical property, not automatically a target represented, detected, or controlled by the system.`

survives strongly.

But Macroscopic Life should **not** say attractor dynamics are categorically non-goal-directed. Some scientific/philosophical frameworks use goal language precisely for attractor-like organization.

Safer rule:

> **AN ATTRACTOR CAN SUPPORT A GOAL-DIRECTED DESCRIPTION WITHOUT BY ITSELF ESTABLISHING A SYSTEM-OWNED TARGET.**

The marble remains a strong minimum adversary because it shows convergence without any need to infer detection, comparison, adaptive compensation, or ownership.

# 3. RETURN IS NOT YET A GOAL — SURVIVES

Pass 11A's central brake survives because it is epistemic rather than definitional:

> **RETURN IS NOT YET A GOAL.**

The word “yet” matters.

The marble-in-a-bowl example remains scientifically useful because passive dynamics can produce convergence toward a familiar state. This defeats the inference:

`return/convergence → therefore system-owned goal`

without denying that some literatures legitimately call attractor-based behavior goal-directed.

# 4. BIOLOGICAL FUNCTION — PLURALISM IS REAL

Philosophy of biology does not offer one uncontested definition of “function.” Selected-effect and causal-role accounts remain major traditions, with organizational accounts adding another alternative.

Useful anchors:

- Keeling, D. M., Garza, P., Nartey, C. M., & Carvunis, A.-R. (2019). “The meanings of ‘function’ in biology and the problematic case of de novo gene emergence.” *eLife* 8:e47014.
- Mossio, M., Saborido, C., & Moreno, A. (2009). “An Organizational Account of Biological Functions.” *British Journal for the Philosophy of Science* 60(4):813–841. DOI 10.1093/bjps/axp036.
- Christie, J. R., Brusse, C., Bourrat, P., Takacs, P., & Griffiths, P. E. (2022/2024 publication record). “Are Biological Traits Explained by Their ‘Selected Effect’ Functions?” *Australasian Philosophical Review* 6(4):335–359. DOI 10.1080/24740500.2024.2370630.

Chapter consequence:

`Function ≠ Goal ≠ Purpose` remains a valuable project firewall, but “function” must not be written as if philosophy of biology agrees on one ontology.

Macroscopic Life should define its operational use when necessary and explicitly avoid claiming consensus.

# 5. ORGANIZATIONAL TELEOLOGY — A SERIOUS COMPETING FRAMEWORK

The organizational approach to biology argues that living systems can exhibit intrinsic teleology through self-maintaining closure of constraints. In this framework, mutually dependent constraints contribute to generating and maintaining one another and the organization they instantiate.

Strong anchors:

- Montévil, M., & Mossio, M. (2015). “Biological organisation as closure of constraints.” *Journal of Theoretical Biology* 372:179–191. DOI 10.1016/j.jtbi.2015.02.029.
- Mossio, M., Montévil, M., & Longo, G. (2016). “Theoretical principles for biology: Organization.” *Progress in Biophysics and Molecular Biology* 122(1):24–35. DOI 10.1016/j.pbiomolbio.2016.07.005.
- Mossio, M., & Bich, L. (2017). “What makes biological organisation teleological?” *Synthese* 194:1089–1114. DOI 10.1007/s11229-014-0594-z.
- Bich, L. (2024). “Organisational teleology 2.0: Grounding biological purposiveness in regulatory control.” *Ratio*. DOI 10.1111/rati.12405.

This literature creates an important hostile challenge to Pass 11A.

Macroscopic Life cannot simply assume that a goal must be separately represented as a target variable. Organizational accounts explicitly ground teleology in self-maintaining organization and regulatory control without requiring a symbolic goal representation.

Therefore the architecture should preserve:

> **A GOAL NEED NOT BE SYMBOLICALLY REPRESENTED TO BE SCIENTIFICALLY TESTABLE.**

But Macroscopic Life can remain stricter about **level ownership**: if organizational closure or regulation exists at one level, it does not automatically transfer goal ownership to a larger containing level.

# 6. ECOLOGICAL REGULATION — THE LEVEL PROBLEM IS ALREADY LIVE

A 2025 review of ecological regulation found the term “regulation” used across multiple levels and distinguished narrower organismal regulation from broader ecosystem-level dynamic stability/feedback usage.

Anchor:

- Pinto Leite, C. M., de Carvalho, Í. N., Coutinho, J. G. E., & El-Hani, C. N. (2025). “Regulation in ecological systems: an overview.” *History and Philosophy of the Life Sciences* 47:58. DOI 10.1007/s40656-025-00707-0.

The review notes varied ecological uses of regulation and explicitly distinguishes organismal regulatory control from broader ecosystem dynamic stability/feedback descriptions.

This is highly favorable to Macroscopic Life's level doctrine.

Permanent brake:

> **ECOLOGICAL REGULATION AS A DESCRIPTION DOES NOT BY ITSELF ESTABLISH ECOSYSTEM-LEVEL CONTROL OWNERSHIP.**

The forest experiment remains legitimate and timely.

# 7. ROBUSTNESS, REDUNDANCY, AND DEGENERACY — IMPLEMENTATION FLEXIBILITY IS REAL BUT NONUNIQUE

Biological robustness can arise through redundancy, degeneracy, modularity, response diversity, homeostasis, plasticity, environmental tracking, and other architectures.

Anchors:

- Kitano, H. (2004). “Biological robustness.” *Nature Reviews Genetics* 5:826–837. DOI 10.1038/nrg1471.
- Whitacre, J. M. (2010). “Degeneracy: a link between evolvability, robustness and complexity in biological systems.” *Theoretical Biology and Medical Modelling* 7:6.
- “Biological Robustness: Paradigms, Mechanisms, and Systems Principles.” review literature.

Consequence:

Pass 11A's implementation-flexibility test survives, but the evidence must be interpreted carefully.

Different mechanisms reaching the same outcome can arise from network architecture without a separately represented target.

Permanent brake survives:

> **MULTIPLE WAYS TO REACH THE SAME STATE DO NOT BY THEMSELVES SHOW THAT THE STATE IS A SYSTEM-OWNED GOAL.**

# 8. EQUIFINALITY — VALID CLUE, NOT SUFFICIENT DISCRIMINATOR

Equifinality has a long history in systems thinking and appears in teleonomy discussions as convergence toward characteristic outcomes from different starting conditions.

Pass 11A correctly treats equifinality as insufficient because passive attractors can also create convergence.

However, the proposed term **Compensatory Equifinality** does not appear to be an established standardized term in the searched literature.

Recommendation:

Do not present it as a scientific field term.

Either:

A. label it explicitly `Macroscopic Life project synthesis`; or
B. use the plainer phrase **compensatory convergence across distinct perturbations** in reader-facing prose.

The concept remains useful: distinct disturbances recruiting different routes that preserve the same prospectively specified constraint is stronger evidence than simple relaxation.

# 9. TRADEOFFS AND SACRIFICE — ARCHITECTURE SURVIVES AND STRENGTHENS

The strongest novel discriminator in 11A remains:

> **WHAT WILL THE SYSTEM ALLOW TO FAIL FIRST?**

This is scientifically valuable because conflict among constraints can expose a regulatory hierarchy.

However, a hostile interpretation must remain available: observed sacrifice can emerge from fixed network architecture, energetic scarcity, differential fragility, or selection-shaped component responses without a system-owned goal.

Therefore tradeoff evidence becomes strong only when a prospectively specified target hierarchy predicts responses to new conflicts better than those mechanistic rivals.

Safer project principle:

> **A GOAL HYPOTHESIS EARNS MORE WHEN IT PREDICTS TRADEOFFS BEFORE THE SYSTEM REVEALS THEM.**

This is stronger than post hoc declaration that whatever survived “must have been the goal.”

# 10. OBSERVER OBJECTIVE FUNCTION — SURVIVES AS A MAJOR BRAKE

Pass 11A's line remains exceptionally strong:

> **AN OBJECTIVE FUNCTION CAN EXIST IN OUR MODEL BEFORE A GOAL EXISTS IN THE SYSTEM.**

This distinction is supported by the broader literature on mathematical optimization, dynamical models, active inference, and philosophy of modeling.

A system can often be represented as minimizing or maximizing a quantity without demonstrating that the physical system itself measures, represents, or causally uses that quantity as an online objective.

Macroscopic Life should retain a causal-use requirement wherever it speaks of system-owned objectives.

# 11. FREE ENERGY / ACTIVE INFERENCE — HIGH-VALUE TEST CASE, HIGH-RISK INTERPRETATION

The free-energy principle and active inference are especially important because they provide mathematical descriptions of self-organizing systems using optimization-like language, priors, expected free energy, and preferred states.

But the literature includes substantial debate about explanatory scope and the relation between model formalism and physical mechanism.

Hostile anchors:

- Raja, V., Valluri, D., Baggs, E., Chemero, A., & Anderson, M. L. (2021). “The Markov blanket trick: On the scope of the free energy principle and active inference.” *Physics of Life Reviews* 39:49–72. DOI 10.1016/j.plrev.2021.09.001.
- Andrews, M. (2021/2022 collection context). “The Free Energy Principle: Good Science and Questionable Philosophy in a Grand Unifying Theory.”
- “Free energy: a user's guide.” *Biology & Philosophy* (2022).
- Kirchhoff, M. D., Kiverstein, J., & Robertson, I. (2025). “The Literalist Fallacy and the Free Energy Principle: Model Building, Scientific Realism, and Instrumentalism.” *British Journal for the Philosophy of Science* 76(3). DOI 10.1086/720861.

Chapter consequence:

Do not infer:

`system can be modeled as minimizing free energy → system owns free-energy minimization as a goal`

without additional causal/mechanistic evidence.

Permanent brake:

> **MATHEMATICAL OPTIMIZATION IS NOT AUTOMATICALLY PHYSICAL OBJECTIVE OWNERSHIP.**

This strengthens the observer-loss-function section substantially.

# 12. MORPHOGENESIS AND REGENERATION — STRONGEST WONDER CASE, BUT NOT CONSENSUS ON GOAL LANGUAGE

Developmental/regenerative biology provides powerful examples of large-scale anatomical outcomes being restored after perturbation through distributed cellular processes.

High-value anchors:

- Levin, M. (2020). “The Biophysics of Regenerative Repair Suggests New Perspectives on Biological Causation.” *BioEssays* 42. DOI 10.1002/bies.201900146.
- Levin, M. (2021). “Bioelectric signaling: Reprogrammable circuits underlying embryogenesis, regeneration, and cancer.” *Cell* 184. DOI 10.1016/j.cell.2021.02.034.
- Levin, M. (2012). “Morphogenetic fields in embryogenesis, regeneration, and cancer: non-local control of complex patterning.” *BioSystems* 109. DOI 10.1016/j.biosystems.2012.04.005.

These papers explicitly use language such as anatomical homeostasis, invariant anatomical outcomes, target morphology, and collective cellular coordination.

This makes morphogenesis/regeneration an excellent **positive research program** for Chapter 10.

But hostile restraint is essential:

- much of the strongest “target morphology” and goal-directed framing comes from a particular research program associated with Levin and collaborators;
- empirical restoration of anatomy does not by itself settle whether “goal” is the best ontology;
- local gene-regulatory, mechanical, biochemical, and bioelectric mechanisms remain possible implementation-level explanations;
- successful intervention in bioelectric patterning is evidence that higher-level state variables can be causally useful, but not automatic proof of intelligence, cognition, or subjective purpose.

Permanent brake:

> **TARGET MORPHOLOGY IS AN EXPERIMENTAL HYPOTHESIS BEFORE IT IS AN ONTOLOGY.**

Narrative recommendation: morphogenesis should probably be Chapter 10's biological wonder beat after the marble/thermostat sequence.

# 13. BIOELECTRIC PATTERNING — CAUSALLY IMPORTANT, VOCABULARY MUST REMAIN DISCIPLINED

Levin's 2021 *Cell* review describes endogenous membrane-potential distributions and gap-junction networks as coordinating cellular behavior toward large-scale anatomical outcomes.

This is highly relevant to Macroscopic Life because it shows a plausible physical mechanism by which distributed local cells can participate in large-scale pattern regulation.

What it supports:

- bioelectric state can be experimentally manipulated;
- large-scale anatomical patterning can respond to distributed electrical signaling;
- anatomical outcomes can be robust under perturbation;
- causal control can exist at scales larger than one cell.

What it does not automatically establish:

- symbolic representation of target anatomy;
- consciousness;
- general intelligence;
- whole-system subjective purpose;
- applicability to ecosystems or planets.

This field should be treated as a tractable model system for testing the project's goal criteria, not as proof of Macroscopic Life.

# 14. ORGANIZATIONAL SELF-MAINTENANCE — A COMPETING INTRINSIC-GOAL CRITERION

The organizational literature argues that biological self-maintenance itself can ground intrinsic teleology: the activities of the system contribute to maintaining the conditions of existence of the organization.

Macroscopic Life should not dismiss this as mere metaphor.

Instead treat it as a serious competitor to the project's stronger target/control test.

Hostile question:

> If closure-of-constraints/self-maintenance already grounds legitimate intrinsic teleology, what additional scientific work does Macroscopic Life's Goal Ownership Test provide?

Answer to carry forward:

Macroscopic Life is not trying to replace organizational teleology. It is trying to test **property ownership at unfamiliar higher levels** and to distinguish self-maintaining organization from passive persistence, lower-level closure, externally defined outcomes, and observer-selected objectives.

This distinction should be explicit in the eventual manuscript.

# 15. ECOLOGICAL SELF-MAINTENANCE — DO NOT ASSUME ORGANISMAL CRITERIA SCALE

The 2025 ecological-regulation review is particularly valuable because it reports broad ecosystem regulation terminology while distinguishing dynamic stability/feedback from narrower control capacities associated with organismal regulatory organization.

Thus:

`ecosystem feedback/stability → ecosystem regulation language may be legitimate`

but:

`ecosystem regulation language → system-owned goal`

is invalid without additional evidence.

The forest experiment should explicitly test whether the relevant constraint-setting/control architecture belongs to the forest boundary or is inherited entirely from organisms and environmental coupling.

# 16. COLLECTIVE REGULATION — POSITIVE CASES EXIST WITHOUT CENTRAL CONTROLLER

Social-insect literature shows colony-level task allocation emerging from distributed worker behavior, local thresholds, interaction rates, and feedback.

Anchor:

- Beshers, S. N., & Fewell, J. H. (2001). “Models of Division of Labor in Social Insects.” *Annual Review of Entomology* 46:413–440. DOI 10.1146/annurev.ento.46.1.413.

The important lesson is not that colonies necessarily have “goals,” but that distributed control can produce colony-level regulation without centralized command.

Permanent brake:

> **NO CENTRAL CONTROLLER DOES NOT MEAN NO CONTROL, AND DISTRIBUTED CONTROL DOES NOT BY ITSELF ESTABLISH GOAL OWNERSHIP.**

This helps protect Figure 12.

Scientific verdict: safe as a distributed-solution statement if the prose immediately prevents ontology inflation.

No figure mutation required in Pass 11B.

# 18. VIABILITY — STRONGER THAN ARBITRARY METRICS, STILL NOT SUFFICIENT

Pass 11A's viability section survives conceptually.

A viability boundary differs from an arbitrary observer metric because leaving it can destroy the organization being studied. This gives viability genuine physical significance.

But the fact that trajectories remain inside a viability region can arise from passive constraints, local regulation, or selection-shaped architecture.

Therefore:

> **VIABILITY MAKES SOME OUTCOMES PHYSICALLY CONSEQUENTIAL; IT DOES NOT BY ITSELF IDENTIFY WHO OWNS THE REGULATION.**

This is especially important for proposed ecosystem/planetary goals.

# 19. SELECTED EFFECT VERSUS PRESENT PURSUIT — ARCHITECTURE SURVIVES

The Pass 11A line survives with qualification:

> **WHAT SELECTION PRESERVED IS NOT AUTOMATICALLY WHAT THE PRESENT SYSTEM PURSUES.**

Selected-effect theories ground certain function claims historically; organizational and causal-role theories ground functions differently. Macroscopic Life must not pretend all biological functions reduce to selection history.

Safer narrative use:

Selection can explain why a mechanism exists without implying that an organism explicitly computes the historical quantity that made the mechanism selectable.

# 20. FITNESS — DO NOT TURN AN EVOLUTIONARY QUANTITY INTO AN ONLINE CONTROLLER

The architecture's fitness firewall survives.

Evolutionary fitness is generally an analytical population-level quantity, not a literal scalar that organisms must sense and maximize online.

Permanent brake:

> **A QUANTITY CAN EXPLAIN EVOLUTIONARY CHANGE WITHOUT BEING A CONTROL VARIABLE INSIDE THE ORGANISM.**

This is scientifically safer than saying organisms never behave as if maximizing proxies; the key distinction is implementation.

# 21. “SACRIFICE” NEEDS NONANTHROPOMORPHIC OPERATIONALIZATION

The phrase is narratively powerful but potentially anthropomorphic.

Use operationally:

- variable A is allowed to depart farther from its usual range;
- resource allocation shifts away from A;
- control effort preserving B increases;
- B remains constrained while A degrades.

Then reader-facing prose can call this a “sacrifice” as shorthand after the mechanism is clear.

Permanent brake:

> **TRADEOFF IS MEASUREMENT FIRST; SACRIFICE IS METAPHOR SECOND.**

# 22. GOAL OWNERSHIP TEST — SURVIVES WITH TWO REVISIONS

The 13-part Goal Ownership Test remains scientifically useful as project synthesis.

Two changes are required.

## Revision A — do not require explicit target representation

Replace any implication that the system must encode a symbolic target with:

**Target/constraint implementation** — identify a physical organization whose state/dynamics make deviations from the candidate outcome causally consequential for control.

The target can be embodied in distributed organization.

## Revision B — add self-maintenance/closure rival

The Fair Rival set must include:

- passive attractor/dynamics;
- feedback network;
- component homeostasis;
- organizational closure/self-maintenance model;
- evolutionary/selected-effect account;
- flexible statistical model;
- externally imposed objective;
- alternative target hypotheses.

Macroscopic Life must beat or clarify these serious theories rather than ignoring them.

# 23. REMOVE THE GOAL TEST — SURVIVES

The property-specific replacement test remains excellent:

> **IF THE GOAL CAN BE REMOVED WITHOUT LOSS, THE GOAL HAS NOT YET EARNED THE SCIENCE.**

But “loss” must include more than compact prose.

Candidate losses:

- held-out prediction under novel perturbations;
- counterfactual prediction;
- tradeoff prediction;
- intervention selection;
- generalization across changing implementations;
- explanatory compression under equal resources.

If a mechanistic dynamical model supplies the same capabilities, goal language remains optional at the project level.

# 24. COUNTERFACTUAL AND UNREACHABLE-TARGET TESTS — STRONG

Pass 11A's counterfactual tests survive literature contact.

A target hypothesis should earn predictive work in conditions not used to infer it:

- disable one route;
- create conflicting constraints;
- shift operating regime;
- make proposed target temporarily unreachable;
- test whether control reorganizes in the predicted fashion.

These tests are especially important because passive attractor and post hoc optimization stories can fit ordinary recovery.

# 25. ALTERNATIVE-GOAL COMPETITION — ESSENTIAL

The line:

> **A GOAL DISCOVERED ONLY AFTER THE SYSTEM CHOOSES IS A STORY UNTIL IT PREDICTS THE NEXT CHOICE.**

survives and may be Chapter 10's strongest methodological signature.

This directly guards against post hoc objective fitting.

Operational requirement:

Candidate goals and priority structures must be pre-specified, learned in discovery data then frozen, or compared prospectively against alternatives.

# 26. REVISED PROJECT LADDER

Pass 11A did not specify a full goal ladder. Literature suggests using evidentiary categories rather than pretending to classify all teleology.

Recommended project sequence:

`Passive Convergence → Regulated Stability → Compensatory Regulation → Constraint-Prioritized Control → System-Owned Goal Candidate`

Explicitly **Macroscopic Life project synthesis**.

Definitions:

## Passive Convergence
Trajectories return/converge through dynamics without demonstrated regulatory control.

## Regulated Stability
Active mechanisms counter deviations or maintain variables/ranges.

## Compensatory Regulation
Distinct disturbances recruit different mechanisms that preserve a common outcome/constraint.

## Constraint-Prioritized Control
When goals/constraints conflict, the system predictably preserves some at the expense of others under prospectively tested tradeoffs.

## System-Owned Goal Candidate
An independently justified system contains/instantiates target or constraint organization that causally governs flexible control, survives external-objective and lower-level/organizational rivals, predicts novel tradeoffs and counterfactuals, and adds prospective prediction/intervention advantage.

No category implies consciousness or intention.
