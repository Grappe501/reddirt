## Newly verified authority records

### BIB-026 — Zimorski et al. 2014 — endosymbiotic organelle origins
Verena Zimorski, Chuan Ku, William F. Martin, and Sven B. Gould. 2014. “Endosymbiotic Theory for Organelle Origins.” *Current Opinion in Microbiology* 22: 38–48. DOI: 10.1016/j.mib.2014.09.008. PMID: 25306530.

**Supports:** Chapter 4 endosymbiotic origin of mitochondria/chloroplasts and the claim that permanent higher-order biological integration can emerge from formerly separate lineages.

**Brake:** endosymbiosis establishes evolutionary precedent; it does not imply every symbiotic association becomes an organism.

### BIB-027 — Gilbert, Sapp & Tauber 2012 — symbiosis and biological individuality
Scott F. Gilbert, Jan Sapp, and Alfred I. Tauber. 2012. “A Symbiotic View of Life: We Have Never Been Individuals.” *Quarterly Review of Biology* 87 (4): 325–341. DOI: 10.1086/668166. PMID: 23397797.

**Supports:** Chapters 4 and 6 discussion of symbionts, microbiome, immune/developmental integration, and the difficulty of drawing a single universal organism boundary.

**Brake:** the holobiont framing is scientifically debated and must not be presented as settled organism ontology.

### BIB-028 — Suárez & Stencel 2020 — plural/part-dependent biological individuality
Javier Suárez and Adrian Stencel. 2020. “A Part-Dependent Account of Biological Individuality: Why Holobionts Are Individuals and Ecosystems Simultaneously.” *Biological Reviews* 95 (5): 1308–1324. DOI: 10.1111/brv.12610. PMID: 32406121.

**Supports:** Chapter 6 plural criteria and the proposition that judgments of individuality can depend on which biological relation or part is being analyzed.

**Brake:** this is a defended account in an active conceptual debate, not a universal definition of individuality.

### BIB-029 — Keller 2002 — developmental robustness
Evelyn Fox Keller. 2002. “Developmental Robustness.” *Annals of the New York Academy of Sciences* 981: 189–201. DOI: 10.1111/j.1749-6632.2002.tb04918.x. PMID: 12547680.

**Supports:** Chapter 8 developmental robustness and the distinction between reliable whole-form production and a simplistic blueprint metaphor.

**Brake:** reaction-diffusion is not treated as the sole or universal developmental mechanism.

### BIB-030 — Netea et al. 2020 — trained immunity definition
Mihai G. Netea et al. 2020. “Defining Trained Immunity and Its Role in Health and Disease.” *Nature Reviews Immunology* 20: 375–388. PMID: 32132681. PMCID: PMC7186935.

**Supports:** Chapter 10 innate immune systems can display altered later responsiveness after prior stimulation through persistent biological reprogramming.

**Brake:** functional immune memory is not conscious recollection.

### BIB-031 — Crisp et al. 2016 — plant stress memory and resetting
Peter A. Crisp, Diep Ganguly, Steven R. Eichten, Justin O. Borevitz, and Barry J. Pogson. 2016. “Reconsidering Plant Memory: Intersections between Stress Recovery, RNA Turnover, and Epigenetics.” *Science Advances* 2 (2): e1501340. DOI: 10.1126/sciadv.1501340. PMID: 26989783. PMCID: PMC4788475.

**Supports:** Chapter 10 prior plant stress can leave persistent states that alter later responses, while resetting/forgetting is also biologically important.

**Brake:** the chapter must distinguish persistent causal state from human-like memory.

### BIB-032 — Siddique et al. 2024 — plant stress-memory mechanisms
Abu Bakar Siddique, Sumaya Parveen, Md Zahidur Rahman, and Jamilur Rahman. 2024. “Revisiting Plant Stress Memory: Mechanisms and Contribution to Stress Adaptation.” *Physiology and Molecular Biology of Plants* 30 (2): 349–367. DOI: 10.1007/s12298-024-01422-z. PMID: 38623161. PMCID: PMC11016036.

**Supports:** Chapter 10 contemporary review of epigenetic, transcriptional, hormonal, metabolic, and priming mechanisms implicated in plant stress memory.

**Brake:** mechanisms and duration remain incompletely understood; do not imply a single plant-memory mechanism.
