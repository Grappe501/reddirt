# Source-integrity gate

## Verified core source anchors

### Major evolutionary transitions

Stuart A. West, Roberta M. Fisher, Andy Gardner, and E. Toby Kiers, “Major Evolutionary Transitions in Individuality,” *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences* 112(33) (2015): 10112–10119. DOI `10.1073/pnas.1421402112`; PMID `25964342`; PMCID `PMC4547252`.

The source explicitly frames major transitions as cases in which entities that could previously replicate independently cooperate to form a new, more complex life form. This supports the chapter's central reversal while not supplying a universal definition of every kind of individuality.

### Organismality / cooperation-conflict framework

David C. Queller and Joan E. Strassmann, “Beyond Society: The Evolution of Organismality,” *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B* 364 (2009): 3143–3155. DOI `10.1098/rstb.2009.0095`.

Joan E. Strassmann and David C. Queller, “The Social Organism: Congresses, Parties, and Committees,” *Evolution* 64(3) (2010): 605–616. DOI `10.1111/j.1558-5646.2009.00929.x`.

Used as an influential organismality framework, not field-wide definition.

### Evolutionary transitions / individuality

Richard E. Michod, “Evolution of the Individual,” *The American Naturalist* 150 Suppl. 1 (1997): S5–S21. DOI `10.1086/286047`.

Richard E. Michod and Denis Roze, “Transitions in Individuality,” *Proceedings of the Royal Society B* 264 (1997): 853–857. DOI `10.1098/rspb.1997.0119`.

### Conflict nuance

Andrew F. G. Bourke, “Conflict and Conflict Resolution in the Major Transitions,” *Proceedings of the Royal Society B* 290 (2023): 20231420. DOI `10.1098/rspb.2023.1420`.

### Dictyostelium

Joan E. Strassmann and David C. Queller, “Evolution of Cooperation and Control of Cheating in a Social Microbe,” *PNAS* 108 (2011): 10855–10862; PMID `21690338`; PMCID `PMC3131822`.

Pauline Schaap, “Evolutionary Crossroads in Developmental Biology: Dictyostelium discoideum,” *Development* 138 (2011): 387–396. DOI `10.1242/dev.048934`.

### Holobiont/hologenome debate

Angela E. Douglas and John H. Werren, “Holes in the Hologenome: Why Host-Microbe Symbioses Are Not Holobionts,” *mBio* 7(2) (2016): e02099-15. DOI `10.1128/mBio.02099-15`.

Kevin R. Theis et al., “Getting the Hologenome Concept Right: an Eco-Evolutionary Framework for Hosts and Their Microbiomes,” *mSystems* 1(2) (2016): e00028-16. DOI `10.1128/mSystems.00028-16`; PMID `27822520`; PMCID `PMC5069740`.

The two sources are intentionally paired because the chapter uses the disagreement to prevent automatic inference from host-microbe association to one evolutionary individual.

### Clonal individuality

The genet/ramet distinction and variable physiological integration are supported by foundational and modern clonal-plant literature. The manuscript deliberately avoids the false `one unchanged genome = one individual` shortcut and acknowledges somatic variation within long-lived clones.
