# Source rechecks

## Queller & Strassmann organismality

Verified:

David C. Queller and Joan E. Strassmann, “Beyond Society: The Evolution of Organismality,” *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B* 364 (2009): 3143–3155. DOI `10.1098/rstb.2009.0095`.

Their proposal treats high cooperation and very low internal conflict as central to organismality. Importantly, they explicitly reject several intuitive traits as universally essential, including physical contiguity, indivisibility, clonality/high relatedness, development from a single cell, germ-soma separation, and same-species membership.

Also verified:

Joan E. Strassmann and David C. Queller, “The Social Organism: Congresses, Parties, and Committees,” *Evolution* 64(3) (2010): 605–616. DOI `10.1111/j.1558-5646.2009.00929.x`.

Use these as one influential organismality framework, not the universal definition of an organism.

## Major transitions

Verified:

Stuart A. West, Roberta M. Fisher, Andy Gardner, and E. Toby Kiers, “Major Evolutionary Transitions in Individuality,” *PNAS* (2015). The paper characterizes major transitions as involving formation of a cooperative group followed by transformation into a more integrated entity, emphasizing division of labor, interdependence, coordination, and low within-group conflict.

Also verified:

Richard E. Michod, “Evolution of the Individual,” *The American Naturalist* 150 Suppl. 1 (1997): S5–S21. DOI `10.1086/286047`.

Richard E. Michod and Denis Roze, “Transitions in Individuality,” *Proceedings of the Royal Society B* 264 (1997): 853–857. DOI `10.1098/rspb.1997.0119`.

Andrew F. G. Bourke, “Conflict and Conflict Resolution in the Major Transitions,” *Proceedings of the Royal Society B* 290 (2023): 20231420. DOI `10.1098/rspb.2023.1420`.

The manuscript's “evolutionary achievement” framing is defensible as project prose, provided it is not represented as the field's single formal definition.

## Clonal plants

Verified literature supports the genet/ramet distinction. A genet derives from one zygote and can consist of multiple ramets; adult ramets may become physiologically autonomous or remain integrated depending on species and circumstances. The manuscript's use of this case is sound.

## Dictyostelium

Verified:

Joan E. Strassmann and David C. Queller, “Evolution of Cooperation and Control of Cheating in a Social Microbe,” *PNAS* (2011), PMCID `PMC3131822`, PMID `21690338`.

The social stage follows food depletion; amoebae aggregate into a multicellular motile slug and then a fruiting body; roughly a fifth of cells form the stalk while others form spores. Aggregation creates vulnerability to internal conflict/cheating. The chapter's basic description survives.

Also useful:

Pauline Schaap, “Evolutionary Crossroads in Developmental Biology: Dictyostelium discoideum,” *Development* 138 (2011): 387–396. DOI `10.1242/dev.048934`.

## Holobiont adversary

Verified:

Angela E. Douglas and John H. Werren, “Holes in the Hologenome: Why Host-Microbe Symbioses Are Not Holobionts,” *mBio* 7(2) (2016): e02099-15. DOI `10.1128/mBio.02099-15`.

This is explicitly a critique of the strong hologenome paradigm. It supports the manuscript's warning that host-microbiome association does not automatically establish a single cooperative unit of selection. Final chapter should pair this critical source with a pro/qualified holobiont source from the 13B ledger rather than making the debate appear one-sided.

# The 27 required repairs

## R1 — Remove “ontology” from the hurricane section

Current phrase:

`The existence of a meaningful edge does not settle the ontology inside it.`

Problem: sounds more philosophical than the surrounding experimental prose and can imply that the chapter is settling metaphysical ontology.

Repair direction:

`The existence of a meaningful edge does not tell us what kind of biological unit, if any, lies inside it.`

## R2 — Tighten clonal lineage wording

Avoid implying that all ramets necessarily share an unchanged genome. Somatic mutation can generate variation within long-lived clones.

Repair direction: replace “one genome lineage” with “one clonal lineage” or “one genet.”

## R3 — Add physiological-autonomy precision

The ramet paragraph should state that ramets are potentially autonomous modules and that integration varies. Do not imply physical separation always equals physiological independence.

## R4 — Correct colony reproduction language

`Reproduction may be concentrated in a small reproductive caste` is broadly acceptable but should be phrased in terms of reproductive output/roles rather than suggesting all colonies map cleanly to one caste architecture.

## R5 — Name organismality framework

The social-insect paragraph should explicitly say “under influential cooperation/conflict organismality frameworks” or equivalent. This prevents the reader from hearing “biologists agree colonies are organisms.”

## R6 — Strengthen evolutionary/physiological firewall

After listing individuality frameworks, add one sentence:

> Evidence for one kind of individuality cannot silently substitute for evidence for another.

This should become permanent doctrine.

## R7 — Avoid “selection and reproduction acquire important higher-level organization” as the only transition formulation

The line is defensible but compressed. Clarify that different major-transition frameworks operationalize the transition differently and that higher-level heritable fitness/selection is especially relevant to evolutionary individuality.

## R8 — Refine “cells that could once” sentence

Current wording can sound as if extant cells inside a multicellular lineage individually had that history. Make it lineage-level:

> Multicellular lineages descend from unicellular ancestors in which cells existed as complete reproductive individuals.

## R9 — “At some point” is too threshold-like

Replace with language allowing gradual transitions and framework dependence.

## R10 — Protect the evolutionary-achievement signature

Keep:

> **INDIVIDUALITY MAY BE AN EVOLUTIONARY ACHIEVEMENT, NOT A STARTING CONDITION.**

But immediately label the scope: this is strongest for evolutionary transitions in individuality, not a claim that every kind of individuality must be historically “achieved” in the same sense.

## R11 — Add 2023 conflict nuance

Bourke 2023 argues conflict remains central but revisits claims that it is always equally important at transition origins. The prose should avoid implying that active conflict-suppression mechanisms are always prerequisites at the initial origin of every transition.

## R12 — Cancer example precision

Do not let “unconstrained proliferation” stand as the whole cancer analogy. Use it as a simple illustration of lower-level proliferation conflicting with organism-level persistence, not as an explanation of cancer.

## R13 — Dictyostelium numerical wording

Use “about one-fifth” only with the Strassmann/Queller source attached directly; otherwise say “a fraction.” Exact proportions can vary with conditions/genotype.

## R14 — Distinguish slug from fruiting body sequence

Clarify aggregation → motile slug → fruiting body rather than compressing aggregation directly into fruiting-body production.

## R15 — Holobiont source balance

Pair Douglas & Werren with at least one source representing stronger/qualified holobiont or hologenome arguments from Pass 13B. The prose can remain skeptical of automatic elevation, but bibliography must show the live debate.

## R16 — Remove “selection act on the association” ambiguity

Specify “selection at the host-microbe collective level in the sense required by the evolutionary-individual claim.” Selection acts in many contexts; the issue is level and heritable fitness consequences.

## R17 — Vector needs stronger visual warning

The formula `I = [B,G,P,C,R,T,E]` looks more standardized than the prose admits. Precede it with “For our notebook only” or equivalent and follow it with an explicit statement that the letters are prompts, not validated axes with common units.

## R18 — Rename G dimension

`G = integration / global causal organization` risks “global” sounding centralized or total. Use `I_n` or another symbol for integration, or simply avoid assigning letters in reader prose. Recommended: keep the conceptual vector sentence but remove the formal equation from main narrative and reserve it for a technical note/sidebar.

## R19 — Do not let vector become universal taxonomy

Add:

> Another scientific framework might choose different dimensions entirely.

## R20 — Membership paragraph must acknowledge endogenous boundaries

Some biological individuality frameworks do not require a sharp spatial crossing event. Add that membership may be developmental, genealogical, functional, or organizational rather than literally crossing a line in space.

## R21 — Turnover section: molecules vs cells

Avoid implying whole-body cellular turnover as a uniform phenomenon. Many cell types have very different turnover rates and some are long-lived. Phrase generically: bodies persist despite extensive turnover at multiple material levels.

## R22 — Split/fusion test applicability

Explicitly label these as tests “where the biology permits interpretable division or fusion.” Otherwise the test can sound universal and destructive.

## R23 — Forest membership vocabulary

A forest component does not necessarily become “incorporated” in a biologically meaningful sense. Define the candidate membership relation prospectively rather than borrowing organism language.

## R24 — Forest repair vocabulary

“Repair” can anthropomorphize succession/recovery. Use “recovery or reconstitution” unless a repair function has independently been demonstrated.

## R25 — Model C wording

Current Model C risks mixing physiological/causal evidence with evolutionary individuality. Rewrite:

> Model C may be earned only for the explicitly named individuality framework. An evolutionary-individual claim must satisfy evolutionary criteria; a physiological-individual claim must satisfy physiological criteria. Success in one cannot be imported into the other.

## R26 — Closing “individuality can continue through another whole” is opaque

The reproduction bridge is good, but the sentence is harder than needed. Suggested progression:

> If a whole can become an individual, another question follows. Can that higher-level organization generate another system of the same relevant kind?
>
> **Can the whole reproduce itself?**

## R27 — Endnotes must stop being placeholders before prefreeze

The current working endnotes contain phrases such as “final citation set to be locked.” Pass 13F must replace every placeholder with verified bibliographic entries and map each note to the exact claim it supports.
