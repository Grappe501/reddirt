## 1. Primary scientific question after literature attack

Retain the 15A question, with one precision upgrade:

> **What evidence shows that differences among proposed higher-level parent and offspring units depend causally on lineage in a way that is not exhausted by component inheritance, shared environments, inherited environments, developmental reconstruction, or post-founding selection?**

This is stronger than asking simply what “passes” between generations.

## 2. The inheritance firewall

The chapter must explicitly distinguish:

### Inheritance
A causal relation across generations through which ancestry contributes to descendant state or developmental possibilities under a specified framework.

### Heritability
A population- and environment-specific statistical property concerning variation. In quantitative genetics, heritability is not a synonym for inheritance and is not a property of an individual trait in isolation.

Visscher, Hill & Wray (2008) emphasize that heritability depends on the population and circumstances and can change with measurement, environment, migration, selection, and inbreeding.

Therefore:

> **INHERITED DOES NOT MEAN HIGHLY HERITABLE. HIGH HERITABILITY DOES NOT IDENTIFY THE MECHANISM OF INHERITANCE.**

This is a strong doctrine but probably too technical for a display signature in the introductory book.

## 3. Parent–offspring resemblance is not enough

Danchin et al. (2011) review inclusive inheritance and emphasize that non-genetic processes can contribute to parent–offspring resemblance. But resemblance can also arise through shared environments, developmental responses, assortative processes, population structure, or selection.

Therefore the chapter must preserve:

> **RESEMBLANCE IS NOT HEREDITY BY ITSELF.**

This is closely related to Chapter 13's frozen `SIMILARITY DOES NOT ESTABLISH LINEAGE.` Do not create redundant signature clutter. In Chapter 14, use resemblance as the opening trap, but likely keep it in prose rather than as a major typographic signature.

## 4. Heredity is not DNA alone

The literature supports a broad but disciplined inheritance landscape. Genetic sequence inheritance is central to modern biology, but non-genetic inheritance mechanisms are legitimate research subjects.

Danchin et al. (2011) review genetic, epigenetic, parental, ecological, and cultural inheritance under an inclusive-inheritance framework.

Fitz-James & Cavalli (2022) review transgenerational epigenetic inheritance involving DNA methylation, histone modifications, and non-coding RNAs, while emphasizing substantial taxonomic differences.

Required brake:

> **NON-GENETIC INHERITANCE IS REAL AS A CATEGORY OF MECHANISMS; THE STRENGTH, FIDELITY, DURATION, AND EVOLUTIONARY IMPORTANCE OF PARTICULAR MECHANISMS MUST BE ESTABLISHED CASE BY CASE.**

Do not write as if all proposed inheritance channels have equal empirical status.

## 5. The reconstruction problem returns inside heredity

Fitz-James & Cavalli make the crucial distinction between direct replicative inheritance and indirect reconstruction of epigenetic signals in descendants.

This forces a correction to any simplistic “what physically crosses the generation boundary?” language.

A descendant state may be lineage-dependent even when the state itself is rebuilt rather than copied token-for-token.

The correct question becomes:

> Does ancestry constrain or generate descendant state through a reproducible causal pathway that defeats fair non-lineage rivals?

Potential doctrine:

> **WHAT SURVIVES A GENERATION NEED NOT SURVIVE AS THE SAME MATERIAL TOKEN.**

Not frozen.

## 6. Memory is not heredity

Chapter 8 remains compatible.

Memory concerns history changing later behavior/state within a continuing system. Heredity concerns causal dependence across a parent–offspring or lineage relation.

The same molecular or ecological mechanism could participate in both depending on scale and generational architecture, but the property claims differ.

Keep:

> **PERSISTENCE THROUGH TIME IS NOT, BY ITSELF, INHERITANCE ACROSS GENERATIONS.**

## 7. Ecological inheritance requires precision

Odling-Smee et al. (2013) explicitly recognize organism-caused environmental modification and its legacy over time as ecological inheritance within niche-construction theory. This supports treating inherited environmental structure as a serious causal pathway.

But ecological inheritance does not automatically imply inheritance by a proposed ecosystem individual.

The level-ownership firewall from Chapters 11–13 remains mandatory:

> An inherited environment can influence descendants of organisms inside a forest before any forest-level inheritance has been established.

This is one of the chapter's strongest adversaries.

## 8. Common garden logic is useful but not magical

The 15A architecture proposes matched environments / exchanged conditions. Preserve this experimental logic, but do not imply that one common-garden experiment cleanly partitions genetic and environmental causation in complex systems.

For a higher-level forest hypothesis, analogous designs may include:
- matched receiving environments;
- reciprocal transplantation of candidate founding components;
- provenance tracking;
- cross-environment establishment;
- manipulation of inherited environmental legacies;
- component-only versus structured founding treatments.

These are analogues, not necessarily literal classical common-garden experiments.

## 9. Selection can counterfeit faithful inheritance

This 15A insight survives and should be strengthened.

Suppose parent populations differ. Many descendant variants are initially generated, but only variants resembling the parental state survive. Observed parent–offspring resemblance may then reflect selection after generation rather than high-fidelity transmission of the state itself.

Therefore measurements must distinguish, where possible:

`state at founding/birth → state during development → state after filtering/selection`.

Potential signature:

> **WHAT SURVIVES SELECTION IS NOT NECESSARILY WHAT WAS FAITHFULLY TRANSMITTED.**

Strong, but hold for narrative architecture.

## 10. Heritability must not become the chapter's main metric

Visscher et al. (2008) is a strong warning. Heritability is context-dependent and population-specific. High heritability does not mean a trait is environmentally immutable, and low heritability does not mean inheritance mechanisms are absent.

For Macroscopic Life, do not define higher-level inheritance by a single h²-like statistic.

Instead use prospective lineage prediction, causal contrasts, provenance, intervention/natural experiments, and replication/triangulation.

## 11. Variation must remain distinct from inheritance

Variation is difference among units. Heredity concerns lineage-dependent recurrence or reconstruction of differences.

A system can vary without those differences being inherited. A system can inherit features that show little observed variation in a particular population.

Therefore:

`Variation ≠ Heredity`

and

`Heredity + Variation ≠ Selection`

The chapter can discuss both heredity and variation because variation supplies the contrast needed to test inheritance. But selection itself should remain the next chapter/property escalation.

### 15B decision on chapter scope

**KEEP HEREDITY + VARIATION IN ONE CHAPTER, but make heredity the property under test and variation the experimental contrast.**

Do not build two equal mini-chapters.

## 12. Price equation belongs mostly to the next chapter

The Price equation can represent mappings between parent and offspring assemblages and can be recursively expanded for nested levels. This makes it relevant to later higher-level selection analysis.

However, literature also warns against treating covariance decompositions as automatic causal proof of selection at a level.

Therefore Chapter 14 should mention no Price-equation machinery unless absolutely necessary. Save it for the selection chapter, where the distinction between statistical covariance and causal level ownership becomes central.

## 13. Forest inheritance experiment — repaired architecture

Start with two prospectively individuated candidate parent forests:

`P1 carries state D1`
`P2 carries state D2`

Candidate descendants:

`O1` and `O2`

Do not ask only whether O1 resembles P1 and O2 resembles P2.

Construct rivals:

### Rival A — Component inheritance
Descendant differences are explained by genes, species composition, propagules, symbionts, or other lower-level inherited components.

### Rival B — Shared current environment
P and O resemble each other because similar environments independently generate similar states.

### Rival C — Ecological inheritance
Parent-altered environments affect descendants, but the inheritance belongs to organismal/component lineages rather than a forest-level individual.

### Rival D — Developmental reconstruction
Descendants rebuild D from inherited cues/components without D itself being directly replicated.

### Rival E — Post-founding selection/filtering
Many states are generated, but ecological filtering eliminates alternatives and leaves parental resemblance.

### Hypothesis H — Higher-level inheritance
A prospectively defined property of P predicts or causally contributes to corresponding descendant organization in O beyond the best fair rivals.

## 14. The inheritance ownership test survives

Keep the project-specific **Inheritance Ownership Test**, but sharpen it:

> After accounting for component ancestry, current environment, inherited environment, reconstruction pathways, and post-founding selection, does a parent-level state improve prospective prediction or intervention on descendant state?

This is a Macroscopic Life synthesis, not a standard named test.

## 15. Transmission fidelity must be treated carefully

Fidelity is relevant but cannot be universalized.

Evolution does not require perfect copying. Some inheritance systems may be noisy, reconstructive, biased, or context-sensitive. The scientifically relevant question is whether lineage carries enough structured dependence for descendant differences to be predictably related to ancestry under the claim being tested.

Potential doctrine:

> **HEREDITY DOES NOT REQUIRE PERFECT COPYING. IT REQUIRES LINEAGE-DEPENDENT CAUSAL CONTINUITY.**

Strong candidate for narrative architecture.

## 16. Parent state must be specified prospectively

Avoid post-hoc trait invention.

If P1 and P2 differ in hundreds of ways, researchers cannot inspect O1/O2 and afterward select whichever difference happens to match.

Pre-register:
- candidate inherited state D;
- measurement scale;
- expected direction/range of descendant effect;
- plausible transmission/reconstruction pathways;
- rival variables;
- failure criteria.

This is the inheritance equivalent of Chapter 13's anti-shopping rule.

## 17. The inherited state must belong to the proposed level

A forest-level D cannot merely be the allele frequency of one tree species relabeled as a forest property.

Candidate higher-level states might involve network topology, spatial organization, interaction structure, disturbance-response architecture, community-level developmental trajectory, or other aggregate/relational properties—but only if they are independently justified and measured at that level.

No example should be declared genuinely heritable in advance.

## 18. Observer-defined categories remain a major threat

If humans classify forests into Type A and Type B, parent–offspring category recurrence may reflect the classifier rather than a transmitted biological distinction.

The property must be quantitatively specified and observer-independent enough to support prospective prediction.

The Chapter 1 observer lesson remains active.

## 19. Prediction Advantage architecture survives with a brake

## 20. Intervention is powerful but not mandatory

Potential interventions:
- exchange current environments;
- exchange or scramble candidate founding structures;
- preserve component ancestry while altering parent-level organization;
- preserve parent-level historical pathway while varying component mixtures;
- alter inherited environmental legacy.

But some lineage questions are historical and cannot be experimentally replayed. Provenance, natural experiments, longitudinal data, phylogenetic/historical reconstruction, and convergent triangulation may be required.

Do not make intervention a universal Model C requirement.

### Model B — Higher-Level Inheritance Utility

## 22. Model C does not yet earn evolution

Even if higher-level heredity is established, Darwinian evolution at that level still requires additional conditions, especially variation tied to differential reproductive success over generations.

This is the correct bridge to selection.

Do not let “heritable variation” automatically become “evolution by natural selection.”

## 23. Earth brake

Planetary similarities across hypothetical generations would be radically difficult to interpret. Shared stellar environments, chemistry, panspermia, deliberate engineering, component organisms, or convergent planetary dynamics could all generate resemblance.

No Earth-level inheritance claim can be made merely because terrestrial life or technology contributes to another biosphere.

Nested ownership remains mandatory.

`Earth must earn Earth.`
