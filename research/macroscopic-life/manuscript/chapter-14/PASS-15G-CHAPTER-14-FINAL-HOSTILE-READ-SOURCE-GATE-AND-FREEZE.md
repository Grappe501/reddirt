## Why the chapter survives

The chapter does not infer heredity from resemblance. It asks whether descendant state depends on ancestry across an independently justified lineage after fair rivals are attacked. It keeps memory, persistence, heritability, component inheritance, current environment, inherited environment, developmental reconstruction, filtering/selection, and observer-defined classification separate enough to compete.

The forest example remains a hostile hypothetical. No forest-level hereditary property is asserted. The chapter requires D to be prospectively specified and independently justified at the proposed higher level, preventing post-hoc trait shopping and simple relabeling of component composition.

The chapter also avoids a copying-only model of heredity. Reconstructive mechanisms are allowed, while the operational Macroscopic Life standard still requires lineage-dependent causal continuity. Founding-state measurement is used where informative but is not treated as a universal requirement for inherited traits that emerge through development.
