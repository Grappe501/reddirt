## 28 required repairs

### 1. Demote `RESEMBLANCE IS NOT YET HEREDITY.`
Pass 15B explicitly warned that this duplicates Chapter 13's frozen `SIMILARITY DOES NOT ESTABLISH LINEAGE.` Keep the thought in prose but remove display-signature treatment.

### 2. Opening family examples need phenotype/behavior channel separation
Eyes/face are fine intuitive biological examples; posture/voice/gesture can be learned or culturally transmitted. Make that ambiguity explicit immediately so the prose does not imply all family resemblance is one inheritance category.

### 3. “What actually made the journey” is too token-transfer oriented
The literature correction says inherited state may be reconstructed. Replace with language such as: “what causal dependence on ancestry survived the generational transition?”

### 4. Variation paragraph needs observer-category brake
D cannot merely be a human label or arbitrary cluster. Add that D must be quantitatively or operationally specified independently enough to support prospective measurement.

### 5. Memory/heredity firewall needs framework qualification
“The causal difference must cross a lineage relation” can sound like literal transfer. Rewrite: descendant state must depend causally on ancestry across a lineage relation. Preserve the distinction without requiring a token crossing.

### 6. Common-garden section needs an explicit classical-to-forest bridge
The prose is correct but should state that common-garden logic is borrowed as causal logic; the later forest experiment uses analogous contrasts, not necessarily literal classical common gardens.

### 7. Non-genetic inheritance list needs source-anchored wording
The list is broad. Tie it directly to an inclusive-inheritance review and explicitly say these are heterogeneous mechanisms/frameworks rather than a settled unified inheritance system.

### 8. Human transgenerational epigenetic caution needs exact source lock
Working note [2] cannot remain generic. Lock a high-quality review and distinguish intergenerational exposure effects from true transgenerational inheritance.

### 9. `AN INHERITANCE CHANNEL MUST BE IDENTIFIED, NOT ASSUMED.` should remain prose doctrine, not a third/fourth signature
Pass 15B's strongest signature budget favored the reconstructive-heredity and selection-counterfeit lines. Demote this line typographically unless final rhythm proves otherwise.

### 10. “Some inherited states can be rebuilt” needs careful scope
Use “under some biological frameworks and mechanisms” or equivalent. Do not imply every reconstructively recurring state qualifies as inheritance.

### 11. `HEREDITY DOES NOT REQUIRE PERFECT COPYING...` needs one precision adjustment
The second clause should not accidentally define all heredity universally. Preferred repair: `HEREDITY DOES NOT REQUIRE PERFECT COPYING. THE STRONGER CLAIM REQUIRES LINEAGE-DEPENDENT CAUSAL CONTINUITY.` Or preserve current line only if source review confirms the project is explicitly presenting it as its operational standard rather than universal textbook definition.

### 12. Selection-counterfeit example conflates environmental filtering and selection
Environmental filtering can generate differential persistence without necessarily constituting evolutionary selection in the strict sense. Distinguish ecological/developmental filtering from differential survival/reproduction tied to variant properties.

### 13. “Only descendants resembling D remain” needs causal precision
State whether D-like variants survive/persist disproportionately. Avoid implying selection intentionally preserves resemblance.

### 14. Founding measurement is not universally available
Keep “where possible.” Some inherited states emerge only during development. Absence of D at founding does not automatically defeat inheritance if lineage-dependent developmental reconstruction predicts later D.

### 15. Heritability definition is slightly muddy
Current sentence mixes genetic variation and “transmissible variation under a defined model.” Tighten using Visscher, Hill & Wray: distinguish broad-sense and narrow-sense heritability if needed, but do not overteach. At minimum say heritability is a population- and environment-specific variance ratio under a specified model, not a synonym for causal inheritance.

### 16. High heritability “does not identify the molecular mechanism” is too narrow
Change “molecular mechanism” to “causal inheritance mechanism.” Some relevant mechanisms are not molecular in the narrow sense.

### 17. Forest D examples need one stronger higher-level criterion
Network-level recovery trajectory, topology, spatial organization, etc. are plausible candidates, but the draft must state that D cannot simply be an aggregate label redescribing component composition. It must have independently justified relational/system-level content.

### 18. Add explicit anti-shopping sentence to forest D
The draft says D must be chosen before outcome. Add that researchers cannot inspect hundreds of P/O differences and then promote whichever one matches as inherited.

### 19. “Swap the Ground” should include inherited versus current environment separation
Current environment and parent-created environmental legacy are distinct rivals. Make clear that matched current conditions can still preserve inherited environmental differences.

### 20. “Swap the Parts” must avoid implying literal exchange is always possible
The current “where possible” helps. Add provenance/statistical decomposition/natural contrasts as equally legitimate routes where manipulation is impossible.

### 21. “Swap the Legacy” needs ownership neutrality
The sentence “is the legacy part of a forest-level inheritance pathway?” is good. Add that ecological inheritance may be fully real at organismal/population levels while forest-level ownership remains unsupported.

### 22. Add observer-defined classification as a named rival
Pass 15B required this and the draft does not make it explicit enough. A Type A/Type B forest classification can recur because of human binning. D must support observer-independent prospective measurement rather than merely category recurrence.

### 23. Inheritance Ownership Test uses “transmitted” too narrowly
Replace “property transmitted by the proposed whole” with “lineage-dependent property of the proposed whole” or “property whose descendant state depends on the proposed parent whole.” This keeps reconstructive inheritance inside the test.

### 24. Prediction Advantage must retain the full four-part doctrine explicitly
The draft does this well, but 15F should preserve exact consistency with earlier chapters: prospective target, fair rival resources, held-out/new conditions, calibration/uncertainty. Do not compress these away.

### 25. Model A should distinguish selection/filtering
Do not collapse ecological filtering, developmental filtering, and evolutionary selection into one word. The next chapter needs selection cleanly reserved.

### 26. Model C should not require a unique “causal pathway” if multiple redundant pathways exist
The draft is mostly safe, but the sentence after positive PA asks for “the causal pathway.” Change to “a defensible causal architecture or set of pathways.” Heredity may be multiply realized.

### 27. Earth section needs one ownership callback
Add the nested-level warning: heredity among organisms within Earth cannot be promoted to heredity of Earth. This mirrors the component/whole firewall and prevents scale inflation.

### 28. Replace all placeholder endnotes before prefreeze
Notes [1]–[4] are explicitly unfinished. 15F must contain complete verified bibliographic entries and no “final source lock should...” language.
