# P1 — Scientific repairs required

## P1-1 — Opening forest must be unmistakably hypothetical

The opening is vivid enough that a reader could initially mistake the 4:17 a.m. sensor sequence for a real field study. Chapter 6 already established the need to distinguish worked examples from empirical reports.

Repair: insert a light narrative cue near the beginning such as “Imagine an experiment we have not yet run” or equivalent. Do not damage the scene. The reader should know the forest is constructed before citations begin.

Reason: invented precision (`4:17`) can mimic empirical reporting.

## P1-2 — “The trees are not signaling” is stronger than the example earns

Current move: a pressure front is revealed, followed by “The trees are not signaling to one another.”

A common driver can explain the observed synchronization without signaling, but its discovery does not logically prove that no signaling of any kind also occurs.

Repair: narrow to the evidentiary claim: the observed pattern no longer requires tree-to-tree signaling; the common driver is sufficient for that pattern unless additional evidence survives controls.

Doctrine: ordinary mechanism can defeat the stronger explanation without proving absence of every other mechanism.

## P1-3 — Plant signaling paragraph needs modality restraint

The draft groups electrical, calcium, chemical, hydraulic, and reactive-oxygen-related signaling under one citation. The Choi et al. review strongly supports electrical/calcium signaling, but not every modality in that sentence should be made to ride on a single citation without verification.

Repair: either narrow the cited sentence to the verified electrical/calcium claims, or add separately verified sources before retaining the broader modality list.

No bibliography decoration.

## P1-4 — Quorum sensing must not become the universal template for “communication”

The quorum-sensing section is useful, but communication terminology varies across biological disciplines. Signal production/detection/regulatory consequence is well established; whether every such process should be described with the same sender-message semantic framework is partly definitional.

Repair: keep quorum sensing as a positive mechanistic control, but state that the chapter's Source→Signal→Channel→Receiver→State Change→Functional Consequence chain is an operational scaffold for this project, not a universal consensus definition of biological communication.

## P1-5 — Resonance paragraph needs explicit forcing/coupling distinction

The draft correctly says synchronization may arise through coupling and resonance may amplify response near characteristic frequency. But “resonance” and “synchronization” are adjacent in the prose and could blur into one phenomenon for a lay reader.

Repair: explicitly distinguish resonance under forcing from synchronization/entrainment among coupled or driven oscillators. They can interact, but they are not synonyms.

Keep: `Frequency is a measurement, not an explanation.`

## P1-6 — Firefly example needs citation or removal from final proof

“Fireflies can synchronize flashes” is factual and specialist enough to cite if retained. Same for any specific historical pendulum-clock claim if later added.

Repair: source the firefly claim in the scholarly pass or replace with a generic coupled-oscillator example requiring no biological inference.

## P1-7 — Wood-wide-web section needs bibliographic precision and symmetric treatment

The section is appropriately skeptical but risks sounding as if the entire common-mycorrhizal-network research program has been debunked. The scientific point is narrower: existence of fungal connections and some transfer processes does not establish the stronger popular claims, and evidence strength varies by claim/context.

Repair:
- distinguish network existence from specific transfer mechanisms and from communication/agency interpretations;
- verify exact bibliographic metadata for Karst et al.;
- do not use a later mycoheterotrophy paper as if it were a direct adjudication of every CMN claim;
- include at least one appropriate primary/review source representing positive evidence if the prose discusses demonstrated transfer/connectivity.

The chapter should attack inference inflation, not a field.

## P1-8 — “Information exists for the scientist” must be framed as epistemic shorthand

Statistical information is not literally owned by the scientist. The line works rhetorically, but experts could object that mutual/statistical information is a formal relation between variables, independent of whether a scientist observes it.

Repair: preserve the signature candidate if desired, but immediately clarify its intended meaning: the scientist may be able to exploit a predictive statistical relation before there is evidence that the biological system uses that variable functionally.

This turns a potentially sloppy ontological claim into a precise epistemic distinction.

## P1-9 — Functional information needs a stronger intervention criterion

Current prose moves from intervention toward functional information correctly, but a causal influence alone is still too weak. A rock hitting a window changes the window; that does not make the rock an informational signal for the window.

Repair: require state-dependent/selective use within an identified regulatory architecture, not merely downstream causal effect.

Candidate rule:
`CAUSAL INFLUENCE IS NOT YET FUNCTIONAL INFORMATION.`

## P1-10 — Ant colony higher-level-unit language must avoid overgeneralization

The draft says evolutionary/ecological evidence can support treating the colony as a meaningful higher-level unit “for some questions.” Good restraint, but final proof should tie this to appropriate superorganism/evolutionary-transition literature if retained.

Repair: cite or make the example more generic. Do not imply all ant colonies meet the same individuality criteria.

## P1-11 — Development sentence risks “no representation anywhere” overclaim

“No individual cell contains a miniature finished body” is safe as metaphor, but it should not slide into a claim that developmental systems contain no distributed positional or pattern information.

Repair: preserve the miniature-body point while acknowledging that cells can contain and respond to molecular, positional, mechanical, and regulatory state information.

## P1-12 — Ownership Test needs a stronger non-circular operational statement

The prose correctly says communication cannot define its own boundary. But “belongs to the organization” remains partly verbal unless linked to measurable consequences.

Repair: specify that ownership strengthens when disrupting the signal selectively degrades a previously established higher-level variable/function, across component substitutions or realizations, and the higher-level model gains prediction/intervention advantage over fair lower-level rivals.

This is project synthesis. Label it as such in notes/methods, not as consensus doctrine.

## P1-13 — The forest perturbation ladder must distinguish feasible intervention from destructive confounding

“Interrupt a connection” can change many variables simultaneously. Cutting fungal connections, roots, branches, or chemical pathways may damage components and generate new signals.

Repair: require intervention specificity, sham controls, collateral-damage measurements, and multiple independent perturbation methods where possible.

A disrupted effect is not automatically evidence for the proposed channel if the intervention damages the system broadly.

## P1-14 — “Restored pathway” language risks causal overconfidence

Current line says if an effect disappears selectively and returns when pathway is restored, the hypothesis becomes stronger. Correct direction, but restoration may be imperfect and manipulations may have off-target effects.

Repair: retain as convergent evidence, not proof.

## P1-15 — Prediction Advantage must use a fair rival

The draft asks whether the higher-level model predicts better than “a strong lower-level model using the same relevant data and resources.” Good. Preserve and explicitly connect to Fair Rival Resource Rule. Otherwise an artificially weak micro-model can manufacture higher-level advantage.

## P1-16 — Model labels create a taxonomy collision

Chapter 5's canonical Models A/B/C classify levels of higher-level organizational evidence. Chapter 7 currently calls its three forest outcomes Model A/B/C in a way that may not map perfectly onto the canonical definitions.

This is high risk.

Repair: either map each outcome explicitly to the canonical model definitions or stop naming the Chapter 7 outcomes A/B/C and call them Outcome 1/2/3 until classification occurs.

Do not silently redefine frozen terminology.

## P1-17 — “Communication is not agency” is correct but needs property-specific caution

Preserve the ladder:
communication ≠ agency ≠ intelligence ≠ consciousness.

Add that evidence for one property neither automatically establishes nor automatically excludes another; each requires its own operational tests.

## P1-18 — Memory bridge needs trace/memory hierarchy

The ending correctly states persistence alone is not memory. Strengthen continuity with the established hierarchy:
Trace → Retention → Functional Memory → Selective Memory → Adaptive Memory.

Repair without turning the ending into a textbook. Chapter 8 should inherit the formal hierarchy; Chapter 7 only needs the first distinction: a retained trace capable of re-entering causal processing is necessary but not sufficient for memory.

# P2 — Narrative/prose repairs

## P2-1 — Too many one-line paragraphs

The frozen house style says paragraphs are the primary unit and one-line paragraphs are rare emphasis devices. Draft v0.1 uses them frequently: “Maybe the forest is talking,” “Now resonance becomes scientifically interesting,” “Signals are everywhere,” “The whole does not need a throne,” etc.

Repair: consolidate roughly 50–65% of the one-line paragraphs into surrounding prose. Keep only the strongest pivots.

## P2-2 — Signature-line inflation

Chapter 7 currently contains many lines formatted as potential aphorisms. Chapters 1–6 went through a signature-scarcity pass for a reason.

Freeze candidates should be limited to perhaps 2–3 new lines. Recommended hierarchy:

`Propagation is not communication` and `Frequency is a measurement, not an explanation` are excellent scientific brakes but need not both receive signature-line treatment in final typography.

## P2-3 — Thermostat/city example echoes Chapter 4 too closely

The city-air-conditioner example works scientifically but repeats thermostat imagery shortly after Chapter 4's signature thermostat sequence.

Repair: consider a synchronized sprinkler/lighting/sensor network or retain the city example but remove thermostat vocabulary. Chapter 7 needs its own memorable adversary.

## P2-4 — “Forest That Learned to Talk” heading prejudges the case

The heading is rhetorically catchy but may temporarily reinforce the exact interpretation the section dismantles.

Repair candidate: `The Forest We Said Was Talking`, `The Network Beneath the Story`, or similar. Preserve curiosity without asserting learning/talking.

## P2-5 — Middle third is concept-dense

Repair: add a quiet connective paragraph after mycorrhiza or before “Information for Whom?” explaining that the same problem has now appeared twice: observing a physical relation is easier than establishing what role that relation plays for a system.

## P2-6 — Earlier-chapter recap is too explicit

“Chapter 3 required... Chapter 4 required... Chapter 5 required... Chapter 6 required...” reads like framework documentation.

Repair: convert to narrative recall without chapter-number bookkeeping. The reader should feel accumulated discipline rather than receive a syllabus recap.

## P2-7 — Experimental ladder reads like a numbered protocol despite no numbering

“First... Second... Third...” through ninth is clear but long and method-heavy.

Repair: retain the sequence but compress into 3 narrative phases: establish the phenomenon; attack the mechanism; test the level and replicate. Formal nine-step ladder can live in a figure/endnote/sidebar.

## P2-8 — Three Forests section repeats Chapter 6's Model architecture

The section is conceptually valuable but too close to Chapter 6's explicit classification sequence.

Repair: make the emotional point through outcomes rather than reteaching the whole model system. The surprising result should be that real communication may survive while forest-level ownership fails—and that this is still a discovery.

## P2-9 — Ending is strong but can lose 15–20%

“The Thing That Remains” successfully opens Chapter 8. Several examples of persistence can be compressed. Keep the scar/crater/bent-metal contrast or one biological series, not every available example.

Final line `Can the whole remember?` should remain isolated.
