## Verified Tier-2 sources

### 1. Polarization vision

Rüdiger Wehner. `Polarization vision—a uniform sensory capacity?` Journal of Experimental Biology. 2001;204(Pt 14):2589–2596. DOI: `10.1242/jeb.204.14.2589`. PMID: `11511675`.

Use: supports the general statement that insects can use polarized-light information, especially celestial polarization, for orientation/navigation.

Limit: do not imply a universal polarization phenomenology across insects or claim that false-color images reproduce subjective experience.

Additional background if needed: Rüdiger Wehner, `Neurobiology of polarization vision`, Trends in Neurosciences. 1989;12(9):353–359. DOI: `10.1016/0166-2236(89)90043-X`. PMID: `2480677`.

### 2. Membrane potential / ordinary bioelectricity

Lina Abdul Kadir, Michael Stacey, Richard Barrett-Jolley. `Emerging Roles of the Membrane Potential: Action Beyond the Action Potential.` Frontiers in Physiology. 2018;9:1661. DOI: `10.3389/fphys.2018.01661`. PMID: `30519193`. PMCID: `PMC6258788`.

Use: supports the broad claim that resting membrane potential and ion-channel-mediated electrical states are fundamental biological phenomena beyond only classical neuronal action potentials.

Limit: this source does not by itself establish cognition, consciousness, or whole-organism agency from bioelectric phenomena.

For the canonical action-potential mechanism, retain Hodgkin and Huxley (1952) as the foundational lineage where a historical source is desired; a modern physiology review/textbook may be preferable for reader-facing production.

### 3. Discovery versus confirmation / preregistration

Brian A. Nosek, Charles R. Ebersole, Alexander C. DeHaven, David T. Mellor. `The preregistration revolution.` Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America. 2018;115(11):2600–2606. DOI: `10.1073/pnas.1708274114`. PMID: `29531091`. PMCID: `PMC5856500`.

Use: supports the distinction between generating hypotheses from existing observations and testing predictions with new observations, and the value of specifying research questions/analysis plans before observing outcomes.

Limit: the book's exact Discovery–Confirmation Firewall is project doctrine, not a quotation or consensus rule inherited wholesale from this paper.

### 4. Out-of-sample prediction / prediction versus explanation

Tal Yarkoni and Jacob Westfall. `Choosing Prediction Over Explanation in Psychology: Lessons From Machine Learning.` Perspectives on Psychological Science. 2017;12(6):1100–1122. DOI: `10.1177/1745691617693393`. PMID: `28841086`.

Use: supports the methodological value of prospective/out-of-sample predictive performance and the distinction between explanatory fit and predictive accuracy.

Limit: this is methodological ancestry for Prediction Advantage; the Macroscopic Life `PA` and `PA_out` constructs remain project-defined.

### 5. Hysteresis / retained physical state

James E. Ferrell Jr. `Self-perpetuating states in signal transduction: positive feedback, double-negative feedback and bistability.` Current Opinion in Cell Biology. 2002;14(2):140–148. DOI: `10.1016/S0955-0674(02)00314-9`. PMID: `11891111`.

Use: supports the existence of bistability and self-perpetuating physical states in biochemical signaling systems.

Supplementary empirical example if needed: Pomerening, Sontag, and Ferrell, `Building a cell cycle oscillator: hysteresis and bistability in the activation of Cdc2`, Nature Cell Biology / related cell-cycle hysteresis literature. The current Chapter 4 prose does not require this additional source unless a concrete cell-cycle example is inserted.

Limit: hysteresis or bistability alone is not functional memory, agency, or goal representation.

### 6. Adaptive control / reinforcement learning

Bahare Kiumarsi, Kyriakos G. Vamvoudakis, Hamidreza Modares, Frank L. Lewis. `Optimal and Autonomous Control Using Reinforcement Learning: A Survey.` IEEE Transactions on Neural Networks and Learning Systems. 2018;29(6):2042–2062. DOI: `10.1109/TNNLS.2017.2773458`. PMID: `29771662`.

Use: supports the claim that reinforcement-learning methods can adapt control policies/solutions from measured trajectories and feedback rather than merely execute one fixed static rule.

Limit: adaptive control does not establish biological or conscious agency.
