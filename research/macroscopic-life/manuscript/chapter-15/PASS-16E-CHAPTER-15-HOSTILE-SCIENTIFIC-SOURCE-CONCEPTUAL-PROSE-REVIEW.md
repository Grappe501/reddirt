## 31 required repairs

### 1. Opening “success” language needs one early evolutionary brake
The relay analogy is excellent, but reader-facing prose should signal before the biological transition that athletic winning is only an analogy for explicit success currency, not a model of biological fitness.

### 2. “Components can differ in success” should distinguish survival, reproduction, and representation
Selection concerns differences that affect lineage representation under the framework, not generic success. Tighten the first biological paragraph.

### 3. Team reassignment thought experiment is too clean if read literally as causal proof
Keep it as intuition, but add that real biological reassignment can change context and interactions. It illustrates the by-product problem; it is not a universal experimental design.

### 4. “Groups containing more individually successful components may themselves produce more descendants” needs level-language precision
For MLS-1 cases, group output may be measured through member reproduction rather than literal group descendants. Do not import the stronger Chapter 13 descendant-whole meaning into every group-selection framework.

### 5. Price paragraph needs exact descriptive/causal wording
“The Price equation ... can separate covariance associated with differences among groups from change occurring within groups” is acceptable, but 16F should say it partitions evolutionary change under a chosen grouping/representation and does not independently determine causal ontology or level ownership.

### 6. Add explicit alternative-grouping dependence to the Price discussion
Pass 16B emphasizes that partition terms can depend on how units are grouped. This currently appears later only in the forest example. Add the general conceptual brake near the Price equation.

### 7. Contextual analysis needs one sentence of actual reader explanation
The draft names contextual analysis without telling the reader what it asks. Add: roughly, whether a group/context variable predicts individual fitness after individual traits are accounted for. Immediately note that this still depends on model specification and is not a universal causal detector.

### 8. Add soft/local selection as an explicit adversary
Pass 16B required this. Local competition can generate group/context terms without a naive increase in total group productivity. The reader does not need technical detail, but one sentence should block the inference that a residual group-context effect automatically means higher-level reproductive advantage.

### 9. Add counterfactual component fitness / pseudo-group comparison
The literature pass identified this as a strong ownership route. Add a brief thought experiment: how would components perform outside the organized collective or in a comparison group lacking the relevant higher-level organization? Use as evidence route, not universal criterion.

### 10. “Two competitions” risks making selection sound literally like conscious competition
Use “selection processes” or “levels of differential reproduction” at least once. Preserve readable metaphor while blocking agency language.

### 11. Resource-taking example needs a frequency/fitness formulation
A component strategy can rise in frequency within a collective while reducing collective reproduction. This is more precise than saying it simply “succeeds.”

### 12. Conflict/ETI paragraph needs source qualification
Do not imply all major transitions are explained through the same conflict-suppression pathway. Say many major-transition frameworks emphasize cooperation, conflict mediation, specialization, and alignment, with mechanisms varying across transitions.

### 13. Third new display signature may be too much
Current chapter has three new signatures plus inherited Model B and individuality callbacks. Hostile recommendation: demote `WHEN THE PART CAN LOSE WHILE THE WHOLE WINS...` to strong prose unless final rhythm clearly needs it. Keep the partition and success-currency signatures as the likely two chapter-owned lines.

### 14. “A forest does not [have a finish line]” needs success-currency nuance
Good prose, but avoid implying all organismal fitness currencies are obvious. Fitness itself can be context-dependent even at familiar biological levels. Add a light brake.

### 15. Forest “more propagules” language can accidentally sound like forest reproduction
Specify component propagules unless a higher-level founding package has already been justified.

### 16. “Selection needs something that varies...” needs Darwinian conditions phrased carefully
Variation, inheritance, and differential success are a useful scaffold, but do not present them as one universally sufficient definition of selection across all formal frameworks. Frame as the operational scaffold for the stronger claim in this chapter.

### 17. MLS-1/MLS-2 paragraph needs exact source lock and terminology caveat
The current caveat is good. 16F must verify the original/framework source and avoid saying all authors use MLS-1/MLS-2 identically.

### 18. “If the answer is yes, we have crossed...” is too categorical before causal rival defeat
Change to: “If that relationship survives the causal tests that follow, we may have crossed...”

### 19. Forest setup should explicitly inherit observer-independent D from Chapter 14
State that D cannot be merely a researcher-defined category and must have independently justified higher-level content.

### 20. Forest component rival needs “selection of versus selection for” wording
A lineage with D becoming common does not show that D was the causally favored property. Linked/correlated higher-level traits remain rivals in addition to component traits.

### 21. Add linked-trait/hitchhiking rival before adaptation section
Pass 16B requires selection-for precision. D may predict success because it covaries with causal property E. This must be attacked before saying selection acts on D.

### 22. Environmental rival should separate sorting from selection
Habitats can nonrandomly sort lineages or components, producing D-success association. Avoid using “environment picked the winner” in a way that personifies selection; retain heading if desired but make mechanism explicit.

### 23. Migration paragraph needs fusion/fission/recolonization
Pass 16B explicitly includes dispersal, fusion, fission, extinction/recolonization dynamics. Add these because they are especially relevant to higher-level lineage bookkeeping.

### 24. Drift/demography paragraph should include unequal lineage size and bottlenecks
Current founder effects and stochasticity are good but incomplete relative to literature control.

### 25. Selection Ownership Test must include linked higher-level traits and local competition
Current rival list is missing both. Add them so D does not win merely because the lower-level rivals lose.

### 26. PA section needs one stronger warning against proxy prediction
The draft says prediction is not causation. Add that positive PA can arise from a stable proxy for environment, composition, or linked property. Causal ownership therefore requires evidence that changing or counterfactually varying D changes expected higher-level success under the model.

### 27. Model A should explicitly include linked-trait and local-competition sufficiency
Do not let Model A look artificially weak.

### 28. Model C wording should say D “causally contributes under specified conditions,” not imply a context-free universal effect
Selection coefficients and fitness effects are environment/context dependent. Lock the conditions.

### 29. Adaptation section needs historical-function precision
Current section is strong. Add that showing current selection on D does not establish that D originated or was historically maintained because of that effect. This is the key selection/adaptation distinction.

### 30. “Selection does more than sort what already exists” is rhetorically risky
Selection changes frequencies among variants; novelty itself arises through mutation, recombination, developmental change, innovation, migration, etc. Repair closing to say selection can reshape populations/lineages by differentially retaining and amplifying variation, while not being the sole source of novelty.

### 31. Replace all five placeholder endnotes before prefreeze
No “Final source lock should...” language may survive into 16F. Full verified bibliographic metadata is required.

## Strongest causal ownership route

The strongest addition required by the literature is a counterfactual question:

> If the relevant components were placed outside the proposed higher-level organization—or in a scientifically defensible pseudo-group lacking that organization—would their expected reproductive success account for the observed lineage outcome?

If yes, the higher-level causal claim weakens.

If component performance changes specifically because of the organized higher-level context, higher-level causal structure becomes more credible. This remains evidence, not a universal necessary test.

## Strongest success-currency rule

The chapter should freeze this distinction:

> A quantity can be biologically important without being the reproductive currency of the level under test.

Biomass, persistence, stability, productivity, component offspring, and area can all matter without automatically measuring higher-level reproductive success.
