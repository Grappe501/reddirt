The literature strongly supports treating evolutionary transitions in individuality as changes in the units and hierarchical organization of evolution, but there is no single universally accepted diagnostic package. Major frameworks emphasize overlapping but nonidentical features: changes in information transmission, loss of independent replication, cooperation and conflict control, collective reproduction, reproductive specialization, life-cycle organization, and the emergence of collectives capable of participating directly in Darwinian evolution.

The most important correction to Pass 17A is that **fitness decoupling cannot be used as a literal universal meter of individuality moving from one level to another**. Recent theoretical work shows serious problems with commensurable cross-level fitness comparisons and argues that apparent decoupling can be a misleading metaphor. The chapter's proposed multidimensional formulation is therefore stronger than a binary fitness-transfer story.

## 1. Major transitions are not one doctrine

Maynard Smith and Szathmáry's influential framework emphasized major changes in how hereditary information is stored and transmitted and noted repeated cases in which formerly independently replicating entities become parts of larger units. Their 1995 Nature review explicitly rejected a general evolutionary law of increasing complexity: there is neither a theoretical reason to expect lineages to increase in complexity through time nor empirical evidence for a universal trend of that kind.

This strongly supports the chapter's anti-ladder firewall.

Required correction 1:

Do not define all major evolutionary transitions as transitions in individuality. Maynard Smith and Szathmáry included transitions such as sex, genetic-code organization, and language/information transmission that do not map cleanly onto a new higher-level organism.

Required correction 2:

Use **evolutionary transition in individuality (ETI)** when the chapter specifically means the origin of a new higher-level evolutionary individual. Use **major evolutionary transition** only when discussing the broader historical literature.

Source anchor:
John Maynard Smith and Eörs Szathmáry, “The Major Evolutionary Transitions,” *Nature* 374 (1995): 227–232. DOI 10.1038/374227a0.

## 2. West et al.: individuality, not sterility or complexity, is the central question

West, Fisher, Gardner, and Kiers (2015) focus specifically on major transitions that create new forms of individual. Their operational definition emphasizes two conditions: entities that could previously replicate independently become mutually dependent or contingently irreversible parts of a larger unit, and within-group conflict becomes sufficiently reduced/aligned for the larger unit to be treated as a fitness-maximizing individual under their framework.

They explicitly warn that sterility, altruism, complexity, ecological impact, and gene transfer are not themselves definitions of a major transition.

This strongly supports Pass 17A's firewall.

Required correction 3:

The five-dimensional Macroscopic Life scaffold must not be presented as the standard West et al. definition. It is a synthesis across frameworks.

Required correction 4:

“Mutual dependence” and “contingent irreversibility” are important in West et al.'s formulation but must not be silently universalized across all philosophical and biological frameworks of individuality.

Source anchor:
Stuart A. West, Roberta M. Fisher, Andy Gardner, and E. Toby Kiers, “Major Evolutionary Transitions in Individuality,” *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences* 112 (2015): 10112–10119. DOI 10.1073/pnas.1421402112.

## 3. Queller and Strassmann: cooperation/conflict offers a different organismality framework

Queller and Strassmann (2009) define organismality through very high cooperation and very low conflict among parts. Their framework deliberately allows unconventional organisms and argues that physical contiguity, clonality, development from a single cell, germ–soma separation, and even some forms of genetic cotransmission are not universally essential.

This is extremely useful for Macroscopic Life because it defeats several mammal-centric assumptions.

Required correction 5:

Do not write that physical fusion, clonality, single-cell bottlenecks, or germ–soma separation are necessary for organismality.

Required correction 6:

The candidate signature from 17A—“A new individual does not require the end of conflict. It requires conflict to stop defining the whole.”—is rhetorically attractive but scientifically too vague. Demote it. Queller-Strassmann's framework would support low conflict/high cooperation, but “stop defining the whole” is not operational.

Source anchor:
David C. Queller and Joan E. Strassmann, “Beyond Society: The Evolution of Organismality,” *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B* 364 (2009): 3143–3155. DOI 10.1098/rstb.2009.0095.

## 4. Fitness decoupling is the largest conceptual hazard

Hammerschmidt, Rose, Kerr, and Rainey (2014) experimentally evolved bacterial collectives under regimes rewarding collective persistence. In one regime, an evolved life cycle incorporated switching between phenotypic states, and the authors interpreted the result through development and fitness decoupling during a transition toward Darwinian individuality.

This remains an important experimental demonstration of life-cycle evolution and collective-level adaptation under imposed selection regimes.

However, later work has challenged literal interpretations of fitness decoupling. The 2022 analysis “Tradeoff breaking as a model of evolutionary transitions in individuality and limits of the fitness-decoupling metaphor” argues that commensurable particle- and collective-fitness measures can become mathematically equivalent bookkeeping descriptions and that cross-level fitness comparisons are conceptually and empirically difficult. It proposes tradeoff breaking as a potentially more informative route in some transitions.

Required correction 7:

Do not describe fitness as literally “moving” or being transferred upward.

Required correction 8:

Do not require numerical decoupling of particle and collective fitness as a universal ETI criterion.

Required correction 9:

Promote the 17A candidate doctrine, with slight repair:

> **A TRANSITION IN INDIVIDUALITY IS NOT A MOMENT WHEN FITNESS MOVES UPWARD. IT IS A CHANGE IN HOW REPRODUCTION, HEREDITY, CONFLICT, DEPENDENCE, AND CAUSAL ORGANIZATION ARE DISTRIBUTED ACROSS LEVELS.**

This is a Macroscopic Life synthesis, not a quotation or universal definition.

Required correction 10:

Add **tradeoff breaking** as a literature-grounded alternative lens: specialization can allow a collective to escape tradeoffs that constrain individual components, but tradeoff breaking itself does not automatically prove full higher-level individuality.

Source anchors:
Katrin Hammerschmidt, Caroline J. Rose, Benjamin Kerr, and Paul B. Rainey, “Life Cycles, Fitness Decoupling and the Evolution of Multicellularity,” *Nature* 515 (2014): 75–79. DOI 10.1038/nature13884.

“Tradeoff Breaking as a Model of Evolutionary Transitions in Individuality and Limits of the Fitness-Decoupling Metaphor,” *eLife* (2022), PMCID PMC9470156. Exact author/volume/article metadata must be verified before manuscript endnote lock.

## 5. Experimental multicellularity: strong mechanism, not complete historical replay

Ratcliff, Denison, Borrello, and Travisano (2012) selected *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* for rapid settling and observed rapid evolution of multicellular clustering. Evolved clusters reproduced through multicellular propagules, displayed a juvenile phase and determinate growth, were largely uniclonal, and evolved a simple division of labor involving programmed cell death that increased propagule production.

This is one of the strongest reader-facing cases because it demonstrates that several multicellular life-history features can evolve rapidly under controlled selection.

Required correction 11:

Do not say the experiment recreated the historical origin of complex multicellular organisms.

Required correction 12:

Make the imposed selection regime explicit. Experimenters chose settling selection; evolution generated the biological response.

Required correction 13:

Do not call every cluster a new organism merely because clustering evolved. The strongest evidence is the evolution of a clonal multicellular life cycle, propagule reproduction, and collective traits under selection.

Source anchor:
William C. Ratcliff, R. Ford Denison, Mark Borrello, and Michael Travisano, “Experimental Evolution of Multicellularity,” *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences* 109 (2012): 1595–1600. DOI 10.1073/pnas.1115323109.

## 6. Volvocine algae destroy the ladder metaphor

Volvocine algae provide an unusually informative comparative system because extant species display varied combinations of unicellularity, multicellularity, body size, developmental traits, and germ–soma specialization. Phylogenetic reconstruction indicates that multicellularity arose once in the relevant volvocine clade, while partial and complete germ–soma division of labor evolved repeatedly and some specializations were subsequently lost.

This is exactly what the chapter needs.

The species are not a living staircase from *Chlamydomonas* to *Volvox*. Extant species are related endpoints with different combinations of ancestral and derived traits.

Required correction 14:

Never arrange extant volvocine taxa as ancestor-to-descendant rungs.

Required correction 15:

Explicitly mention repeated gains and losses of specialization. This gives empirical force to the anti-progress argument.

Required correction 16:

Germ–soma specialization is evidence about division of labor and reproductive organization, not a universal definition of individuality.

Source anchors:
Matthew D. Herron, “Origins of Multicellular Complexity: Volvox and the Volvocine Algae,” *Molecular Ecology* 25 (2016): 1213–1223. DOI 10.1111/mec.13551.

“Evolution of Reproductive Development in the Volvocine Algae,” PMCID PMC3098969. Exact author metadata must be verified before endnote lock.

## 7. Reversibility is real enough to block inevitability

The volvocine record includes losses of reproductive/somatic specialization. This does not mean every mature ETI is freely reversible, but it demonstrates that traits associated with increasing multicellular specialization do not form a universal one-way escalator.

Required correction 17:

Replace “ratchet” language with **stabilization / dependency / reversibility** language unless discussing a specific mechanism for which irreversibility is demonstrated.

Required correction 18:

Do not require irreversibility as a universal criterion. Treat reduced reversibility or loss of independent capacity as strong evidence in some frameworks.

## 8. Eusocial colonies prevent a physical-fusion definition

Queller-Strassmann explicitly permit some social insect colonies to qualify as organisms under their cooperation/conflict framework. West et al. also treat certain eusocial transitions as important cases of higher-level individuality, emphasizing mutual dependence and conflict alignment.

The critical value for this chapter is conceptual: higher-level individuality need not require the components to fuse into one continuous body.

Required correction 19:

Do not generalize “superorganism” to all eusocial colonies. Colony-level organismality is framework- and case-dependent.

Required correction 20:

Do not treat reproductive caste specialization alone as sufficient. Colony founding, dependence, conflict structure, reproduction, and lineage organization remain relevant.

## 9. Bottlenecks are mechanisms, not definitions

The literature strongly supports the importance of clonality/high relatedness and bottlenecks in many transitions, particularly multicellularity, because they can reduce internal genetic conflict. Ratcliff's snowflake yeast clusters are largely uniclonal; many multicellular life cycles pass through a single-cell stage.

But Queller-Strassmann explicitly reject single-cell development and clonality as universal organism requirements, and social insect colonies demonstrate different architectures.

Required correction 21:

Keep the 17A rule exactly in spirit:

`bottleneck = possible transition mechanism, not universal definition.`

## 10. Cooperation is not individuality

Stable mutualisms, biofilms, consortia, and cooperative groups can show extensive functional integration while component lineages remain evolutionarily distinguishable.

Queller-Strassmann's willingness to classify some mutualistic associations as organismal also reveals a deeper issue: different individuality frameworks can classify borderline systems differently.

Required correction 22:

Model C must always name the individuality framework being used. Do not present one binary universal “individual” verdict where the literature itself is plural.

## 11. Historical inference requires a separate evidence architecture

Herron's review emphasizes integration of phylogenetic, developmental, genomic, biomechanical, and experimental evidence. Experimental evolution can expose mechanisms; comparative phylogenetics can reconstruct historical changes; extant diversity can test correlated transitions.

Required correction 23:

Freeze a two-column conceptual distinction:

**Present individuality evidence** — what the system is now.

**Transition-history evidence** — how that organization arose.

Neither can silently substitute for the other.

Potential doctrine:

> **A PRESENT INDIVIDUAL DOES NOT CONTAIN A COMPLETE RECORD OF HOW IT BECAME ONE.**

Use as prose unless signature budget permits.

## 12. The Transition Dependence Test survives, but must be narrowed

The project-specific test from 17A is scientifically useful if framed as an intervention/counterfactual route rather than a universal ETI test.

Repaired form:

> When experimentally or comparatively tractable, does disrupting a candidate higher-level organizational mechanism—while preserving component identities, resources, and environment as far as possible—reduce collective reproduction, inheritance, or lineage stability beyond what the strongest component/association model predicts?

Required correction 24:

Rename it **Organizational Dependence Test**. “Transition Dependence” confuses evidence about present dependence with evidence about historical transition.

It cannot by itself establish how the dependence evolved.

## 13. The Transition Ownership Test survives with one major addition

Current 17A form asks whether inherited higher-level organizational change explains redistribution of reproductive control, conflict mediation, dependence, and lineage persistence.

Required correction 25:

Add a **historical comparator requirement**. A transition claim needs evidence of change across time/lineages/experimental generations, not merely a highly integrated endpoint.

Repaired project synthesis:

> Relative to a justified ancestral, comparative, or experimentally reconstructed baseline, do inherited organizational changes alter reproduction, heredity, dependence, conflict structure, and causal integration such that the higher-level unit gains explanatory and evolutionary status under the stated individuality framework beyond the strongest association model?

## 14. Prediction Advantage remains useful but cannot establish historical transition

`PA_transition = Performance(H_transition) - Performance(B_association)` survives.

H can predict collective lineage behavior using higher-level variables. B treats components as sufficient evolutionary individuals embedded in cooperation, mutualism, ecology, or shared environment.

Positive PA can show present scientific utility.

It cannot establish that a transition occurred historically.

Required correction 26:

The reader chapter must explicitly separate:

`present higher-level predictive/causal utility`
from
`historical evidence of an ETI.`

## 18. Forest protocol after literature grounding

The forest experiment survives only if it distinguishes present integration from historical transition.

Required structure:

1. define candidate forest individual under explicit framework;
2. establish reproduction/heredity/selection from Chapters 13–15;
3. identify D prospectively;
4. identify comparative/ancestral/experimental baseline;
5. test whether D changes reproductive organization, dependence, conflict mediation, specialization, or lineage stability;
6. test Organizational Dependence where feasible;
7. compare H_transition against B_association;
8. defeat component adaptation, mutualism, environmental filtering, ecological inheritance, composition, and boundary rivals;
9. require historical/comparative evidence before calling the result a transition;
10. allow Model B when higher-level organization matters but historical ETI is not established.

No current literature found in this pass establishes forests as evolutionary individuals undergoing ETI. The forest remains a deliberately hostile candidate.

## 19. Earth brake after literature grounding

The brake strengthens.

Earth contains documented ETIs. This is evidence that transitions occur **within** Earth's biosphere, not that Earth is itself the next rung.

Global atmospheric transformation, biogeochemical cycling, biosphere–geosphere coupling, human technological networks, and planetary feedbacks can all increase integration or causal coupling without supplying planetary parent-offspring lineages or evidence of a planetary ETI.

No scientific ladder licenses:

`molecule → cell → organism → colony → ecosystem → planet`.

The existence of lower transitions does not predict that every larger nested level will individuate.
