## Status

PUB-6B — ACT I VISUAL PACKAGE: BUILT

## Purpose

Act I, **The World We Cannot See**, must visually break the reader's assumption that human perception, scale, and timescale provide a complete inventory of reality without visually implying that Macroscopic Life has already been demonstrated.

The visual language remains locked to: **documentary science + field notebook + modern systems map + restrained cosmic scale**. Emotional register: **awe under restraint**.

## Figure 1 — The Embedded Observer

**Placement:** Chapter 1, immediately after the intelligent-microbe thought experiment is established.

**Core image:** A nested-scale cutaway moving from a tiny embedded observer through tissue/cellular environment to the outline/context of a human body. The embedded observer has rich local information but no privileged view of the containing whole.

**Scientific job:** Make the epistemic constraint intuitive: an intelligent component can map its local environment accurately while failing to infer the organizational scale containing it.

**Required visual logic:**
- local sensory field is detailed;
- information fades with scale/distance from the observer;
- larger human-scale organization is legible to the book reader but not presented as directly available to the embedded observer;
- no mystical glow, supernatural iconography, or claim that humans occupy the same relationship to a cosmic organism;
- caption explicitly labels this as a thought experiment about observation limits.

**Caption:** *An observer can build an accurate science of its local environment without possessing direct access to the larger organizational level that contains it. The diagram is a thought experiment about observational limits, not evidence that humans inhabit a larger organism.*

## Figure 2 — Same Landscape, Different Worlds

**Placement:** Chapter 2, after the first survey of nonhuman sensory systems.

**Core image:** One restrained natural landscape shown as overlapping sensory-access layers: human visible light, snake infrared sensitivity, bee ultraviolet sensitivity, bat echolocation, shark electroreception, bird magnetic/polarization-related orientation cues. The goal is not to pretend these experiences can be literally reconstructed; it is to map detector access to physical information humans do not directly sense.

**Scientific job:** Separate **physical signal present** from **signal directly available to unaided human perception**.

**Required visual logic:**
- common physical environment remains constant;
- each organism receives a different subset of available information;
- labels distinguish established sensory capability from artistic visualization;
- avoid psychedelic false-color spectacle;
- no aura language and no implication that known nonhuman senses validate unknown human sensory claims.

**Caption:** *Organisms sharing one environment do not receive one identical perceptual world. Different sensory systems sample different physical information. False-color and overlay conventions are explanatory devices, not literal depictions of subjective experience.*

## Figure 3 — The Clock Is Another Sense

**Placement:** Chapter 3, near the transition from biological rhythms to geological and evolutionary timescales.

**Core image:** A logarithmic or stepped temporal ladder connecting milliseconds, seconds, days, years, centuries, millennia, and geological time, with representative processes placed at the scale where their motion becomes legible.

**Scientific job:** Show that temporal sampling can conceal dynamics just as a missing sensory channel can conceal a signal.

**Required visual logic:**
- emphasize observation window rather than equating slow change with life;
- include fast biological/electrical events, organismal rhythms, development, ecological succession, geological motion, and evolutionary change;
- explicitly separate `dynamic process` from `living process`;
- no claim that mountains, planets, or geological systems are alive.

**Caption:** *Processes become visible or invisible depending on the duration and resolution of observation. A process occurring outside a human observation window may be difficult to recognize, but slowness alone is not evidence of life.*

## Act I Visual Sequence

1. **Scale constraint** — The Embedded Observer.
2. **Sensory constraint** — Same Landscape, Different Worlds.
3. **Temporal constraint** — The Clock Is Another Sense.

Together the sequence produces one disciplined transformation: **we do not perceive all physical information, from every scale, across every relevant duration.** It does not ask the reader to accept Macroscopic Life.

## Production Guardrails

- Scientific diagram first; decorative illustration second.
- Restrained palette suitable for print and ebook conversion.
- Legible in grayscale where practical.
- Minimum reliance on embedded text; labels must remain editable in final layout.
- Every false-color or conceptual overlay must be identified as such.
- No visual should convert analogy into evidence.
- No visual should depict a confirmed planetary, cosmic, or civilization-scale organism.
- Figures must survive the hostile-review question: **What does this image actually establish?**
