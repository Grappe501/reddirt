The composition is unusually strong for a first pass. It has excellent visual hierarchy, readable panel logic, a useful homunculus contrast, a strong component-to-system transition, and an effective evidence/nonclaim structure.

But the figure currently makes several claims more strongly than the Chapter 12 architecture permits. # 2. P1 — `INTELLIGENCE FROM MANY PARTS, NOT A BOSS` IS TOO STRONG

Current V1 panel heading:

> `Intelligence from many parts, not a boss.`

This prejudges the central question.

The scientifically safer proposition is that distributed organization can implement system-level capabilities without one component serving as a homunculus.

Required V2 direction:

### Heading

**DISTRIBUTED IMPLEMENTATION**

### Subheading

**A capability can depend on interactions among many parts without one part containing the whole computation.**

Required brake:

> **DISTRIBUTED ≠ INTELLIGENT BY DEFAULT**

---

# 3. P1 — `MANY SIMPLE UNITS` IS A BAD GENERALIZATION

The V1 network panel says:

> `Many simple units process information in parallel.`

This fails for human societies, scientific institutions, and many biological collectives because the components may themselves be highly capable agents.

It also invites a false theory that distributed intelligence requires simple components.

Replace with:

> **Many components can process different information and influence one another.**

And:

> **No single component need contain the complete system state or computation.**

Do not use `simple units` as the general rule.

---

# 4. P1 — `LOCAL RULES CREATE COORDINATED SYSTEM BEHAVIOR` NEEDS A MECHANISTIC BRAKE

Current wording is broadly defensible but visually encourages the leap:

`local rules → coordination → intelligence`.

Required canonical distinction:

> **LOCAL RULES CAN PRODUCE COHERENT GLOBAL BEHAVIOR WITHOUT A CENTRAL COMMANDER.**

Immediately pair it with:

> **COHERENCE CAN EMERGE FROM SIMPLE INTERACTION RULES. INTELLIGENCE REQUIRES AN ADDITIONAL PERFORMANCE CLAIM.**

This is one of the most important brakes in the entire figure.

---

# 5. P1 — INTERNET AND HUMAN SOCIETIES UNDER `REAL-WORLD EXAMPLES` ARE SCREENSHOT-UNSAFE

This is the largest visual inference problem.

Panel 4 is titled:

> `REAL-WORLD EXAMPLES — No little man, real intelligence.`

It then places:

- ant colonies;
- human brain;
- internet;
- human societies

in parallel columns.

Detached from the rest of the figure, this visually asserts that the internet and human societies are demonstrated intelligent systems in the same scientific sense as a human brain.

That is not what the architecture permits.

Required V2 heading:

**COMPARATIVE DISTRIBUTED SYSTEMS**

Subheading:

> **Different systems can exhibit distributed information processing or collective task performance through different mechanisms. Similarity does not establish identical cognitive architecture.**

Recommended columns:

1. **Human brain** — primary biological anchor.
2. **Social insect colony** — collective task performance.
3. **Scientific network / institution** — conscious people + artifacts + procedures + communication.

Remove the standalone `internet` column from Figure 12. Chapter 13 is the correct place to examine the planetary technological network.

Locked footer:

> **SIMILAR TASK PERFORMANCE ≠ IDENTICAL COGNITIVE ARCHITECTURE**

# 6. P1 — ANT-COLONY COPY OVERCLAIMS

Current ant copy includes statements equivalent to:

> colony finds food, optimizes foraging routes, adapts to change.

`Optimizes` is especially dangerous unless tied to a defined criterion and empirical comparison.

Use mechanism-level language instead:

> **Local interactions, recruitment, feedback, division of labor, and environmental traces can produce colony-level task performance without a central commander.**

Do not imply universal colony intelligence.

If a specific foraging result is used in publication copy, it must be sourced and tied to a specific species/task.

---

# 7. P1 — TEST 8 WORDING DRIFTS FROM THE LOCK

V1 asks approximately:

> Does the system have information that no single component provides, and does it improve prediction or problem-solving?

This is directionally correct but less rigorous than the architecture.

Canonical V2 question must be:

> **Does the state or relationship of the proposed whole provide reproducible task-relevant information that cannot be recovered from any single component alone and that improves prediction of system behavior beyond appropriate component-only models?**

This is long, but the figure can divide it into two deterministic lines:

**WHOLE-ONLY INFORMATION**

1. Is task-relevant information present in system relationships/state that no single component contains alone?
2. Does adding those variables improve out-of-sample prediction beyond an appropriate component-only model?

Then:

> **WHOLE-ONLY INFORMATION IS INFORMATION IN ORGANIZATION — NOT INFORMATION FLOATING ABOVE MATTER.**

This firewall is missing from V1 and is mandatory.

---

# 8. P1 — BOTTOM-LINE LANGUAGE COLLAPSES CAPABILITY INTO INTELLIGENCE

V1 footer:

> `THE WHOLE CAN SOLVE A PROBLEM THAT NO SINGLE COMPONENT SOLVES ALONE.`

This line is canonical and should remain.

But the adjacent bottom-line panel says approximately:

> `A system can be intelligent without a little man at the top if it has organization, information, interaction, problem-solving capability, measurable advantage.`

That is too checklist-like and implies sufficiency.

Organization + information + interaction + problem solving + advantage do not automatically settle the system boundary, ownership of capability, agency, or individuality.

Replace the checklist with:

### EVIDENCE FOR A SYSTEM-LEVEL CAPABILITY

- defined system boundary;
- defined task;
- task-relevant information integration;
- organization-dependent performance;
- prospective performance metric;
- perturbable causal dependence;
- improvement beyond component-only baseline.

Footer:

> **SYSTEM-LEVEL CAPABILITY MAY BE REAL BEFORE HIGHER-ORDER INDIVIDUALITY IS ESTABLISHED.**

---

# 9. P1 — `INTELLIGENCE IS A PROPERTY OF WHAT THE SYSTEM DOES, NOT WHERE IT SITS` IS TOO ABSOLUTE

This V1 bottom-line sentence is rhetorically attractive but scientifically too broad.

It bypasses the central attribution problem: which system?

Replace with:

> **INTELLIGENCE CLAIMS REQUIRE A DEFINED SYSTEM, TASK, INFORMATION PATHWAY, PERFORMANCE CRITERION, AND COMPARISON.**

If a shorter aphorism is desired:

> **CAPABILITY MUST BE ATTRIBUTED TO THE LEVEL THAT EARNS IT.**

That is much safer and more aligned with model competition.

---

# 10. P2 — THE HOMUNCULUS PANEL STRAW-MANS CENTRALIZATION

V1's `traditional picture` can be read as suggesting mainstream neuroscience literally assumes a little man.

It does not.

The homunculus is an explanatory failure mode, not the current scientific model of cognition.

Rename:

**THE HOMUNCULUS TRAP**

Subheading:

> **A tempting explanatory shortcut: place an internal observer inside the system.**

Then:

> **WHO INTERPRETS THE OBSERVER?**

This makes the target an explanatory regress rather than neuroscience itself.

---

# 11. P2 — HUMAN BRAIN COPY NEEDS SPECIALIZATION

V1 emphasizes billions of neurons and no single neuron understanding the whole task.

Add:

> **Distributed does not mean uniform: neural systems contain specialization, recurrent connectivity, hubs, bottlenecks, and hierarchical as well as heterarchical organization.**

Figure-sized version:

> **DISTRIBUTED ≠ UNSTRUCTURED**

Do not imply that cognition is a homogeneous swarm of neurons.

---

# 12. P2 — `NO CENTRAL CONTROLLER` NEEDS QUALIFICATION

Several panels use language equivalent to `no central controller`.

That phrase is useful for rejecting a homunculus but can imply that distributed systems lack centralized control structures altogether.

Use:

> **NO SINGLE COMPONENT NEEDS TO CONTAIN AND INTERPRET THE WHOLE COMPUTATION.**

This is the actual claim.

---

# 13. P2 — `EMERGENCE ≠ MYSTERY` IS GOOD BUT INCOMPLETE

Keep the idea but replace generic language with the architecture's stronger firewall:

> **EMERGENCE NAMES A LEVEL RELATION. IT DOES NOT BY ITSELF EXPLAIN THE MECHANISM.**

This is more scientifically useful than saying emergence is not magic.

---

# 14. P2 — `MEASURABLE ADVANTAGE` IS UNDERDEFINED

V1 repeatedly uses performance/advantage language without always identifying task, baseline, or prospective metric.

Chapter 11 already taught the reader not to accept this.

Figure 12 must inherit that discipline.

Use:

> **DEFINED TASK + PROSPECTIVE METRIC + APPROPRIATE BASELINE**

Then ask whether organization-dependent variables improve performance or prediction.

---

# 15. P2 — WHOLE-ONLY INFORMATION NEEDS RELATIONAL EXAMPLES

The concept will be misunderstood if presented only verbally.

V2 should visually show that the candidate information can be in:

- relationships;
- correlations;
- topology;
- timing;
- spatial configuration.

A small diagram can show identical component values arranged differently, producing different system states.

Caption:

> **SAME PARTS. DIFFERENT RELATIONSHIPS. DIFFERENT SYSTEM STATE.**

This would be one of the strongest explanatory additions to V2.
