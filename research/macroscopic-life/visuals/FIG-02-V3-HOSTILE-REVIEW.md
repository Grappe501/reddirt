# 1. Core Claim

**PASS**

Title:

`Same World, Different Sensory Access`

Subtitle:

`Different species detect and use different kinds of physical information in the same environment.`

This is appropriately narrow and empirical.

The figure no longer claims to reconstruct complete subjective animal experience.

# 2. Epistemic Brake

**PASS — LOCK**

Bottom line:

`A signal's absence from unaided perception is a reason to measure — not evidence that an unsupported hidden phenomenon exists.`

This successfully protects the chapter from the inference:

`humans cannot sense X → therefore my proposed invisible X exists.`

Keep this principle in the final figure.

# 3. Human Instruments Section

**PASS WITH ONE WORDING FIX**

The section correctly establishes that science extends human detection through instruments.

The current examples are useful: radio/infrared telescopes, microphones, thermal cameras, mass spectrometry.

Do not imply instruments literally extend biological perception. Prefer `extend measurement` or `extend observational access` in the final deterministic copy.

---

# 4. Human Panel

**PASS WITH P2 COPY REVISION**

The figure appropriately acknowledges smell, taste, touch, and balance rather than reducing humans to vision and hearing.

Current wording says those senses provide `valuable but specific information.` This is acceptable but vague.

Final deterministic copy should simply say that the figure highlights selected channels and is not a complete taxonomy of human sensory systems.

---

# 5. Bee Panel

**P2 — QUALIFY**

The generated copy still says `Bees detect ultraviolet light` and `They also detect polarized light` as universal statements.

Final copy should say:

`Many bees detect ultraviolet wavelengths unavailable to human vision, and polarization sensitivity can contribute to navigation in some contexts.`

Avoid presenting one identical sensory system across all bees.

---

# 6. Pit-Viper Panel

**PASS WITH P2 COPY REVISION**

Major improvement: the thermal mouse is explicitly labeled as false-color illustration.

Remaining phrase `locate warm-blooded prey in complete darkness` is rhetorically stronger than necessary and makes the pit system sound like independent camera vision.

Prefer:

`Pit organs detect infrared radiation associated with temperature differences and can contribute to prey localization, including under low-light conditions.`

The figure should continue to state that the false-color image is explanatory, not a reconstruction of subjective vision.

---

# 7. Bat Panel

**PASS WITH P2 REVISION**

The `see with sound` metaphor is gone. Good.

Current copy says bats use echoes to `detect objects, navigate, and catch prey with remarkable precision in the dark.` Broadly acceptable, but final copy can be more mechanistic:

`Returning echoes provide information about features such as distance, direction, motion, and object structure.`

This better serves the chapter's information-access argument.

# 8. Shark Panel

**PASS — MAJOR IMPROVEMENT**

The previous decorative field/aura presentation has been replaced with a causal schematic:

`prey activity → electrical signal in seawater → ampullae of Lorenzini`

This is much safer and more scientifically useful.

Final production should label the arrows/signal diagram as **schematic** and avoid implying the pictured waveform/field geometry is a direct visualization of the shark's subjective percept.

---

# 9. Migratory Bird Panel

**PASS WITH P2 LANGUAGE REVISION**

The figure now explicitly acknowledges that biological mechanisms remain under investigation. Good.

However, the sentence `evidence shows they can sense magnetic information and use it for navigation` can be sharpened to distinguish orientation/compass evidence from a single known sensory mechanism.

Preferred:

`Many migratory birds use information associated with Earth's magnetic field as one cue for orientation and navigation. The biological mechanisms remain an active area of research.`

The globe/field overlay should be marked **schematic** in final production.

---

# 10. Top-Right Evolution Claim

**P2 — REVISE**

Current text:

`Each species has evolved sensory systems that sample specific information relevant to its survival and niche.`

This is directionally reasonable but slightly adaptationist: it can imply every sensory property is directly optimized for a present-day survival function.

Prefer:

`Sensory systems differ among species as a result of evolutionary history and ecological demands.`

This is safer and sufficient.

---

# 11. “Why It Matters” Panel

**P2 — TRIM**

`Deeper connection` and `respect for the many ways life interacts with and adapts to the same world` are philosophically pleasant but not necessary to the scientific argument.

For the short book, this space is better used for three disciplined consequences:

1. **Broader observation** — unaided human perception samples only part of available physical information.
2. **Better measurement** — instruments allow reproducible access to additional signals.
3. **Epistemic discipline** — sensory limitation justifies investigation, not unsupported belief.

This will reduce poster-like motivational language and strengthen the scientific tone.

# 14. Final Scientific Copy Lock

The final figure should communicate only these propositions:

1. Different organisms possess different sensory systems.
2. Those systems provide access to different subsets of physical information in a shared environment.
3. Human unaided perception is therefore not a complete detector of environmental signals.
4. Scientific instruments can detect and measure signals outside unaided human sensory ranges.
5. Failure to perceive a signal is not evidence that a proposed signal exists.
6. Claims about otherwise inaccessible phenomena require reproducible measurement.

Nothing stronger is needed.
