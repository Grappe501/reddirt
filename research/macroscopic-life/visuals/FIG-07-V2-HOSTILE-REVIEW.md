# Remaining hostile-review findings

# 1. Panel 1 still oversimplifies the pump

The generated text labels:

> `Ion pump (active, uses ATP)`

This is acceptable as a visual placeholder for a primary active pump but too general as canonical scientific copy.

Not every transporter directly hydrolyzes ATP, and the publication architecture explicitly distinguishes pumps/transporters more carefully.

## Final deterministic copy

Use:

### Ion channel
> `Selective pathway through which ions can move according to electrochemical driving forces when the channel is open.`

### Pump / transporter
> `Membrane proteins that move ions or other solutes; some directly or indirectly use cellular energy to maintain gradients.`

The generated green protein may remain illustrative if treated as a generic energy-dependent transporter/pump rather than a universal transport model.

**Severity: P2 / deterministic-copy correction**

---

# 2. Panel 1 visual arrow can still imply simple top-to-bottom pump direction

The channel and pump are both depicted with large vertical arrows. This is visually clean but can imply that channel movement and pump action are just opposite versions of the same transport process.

## Final production correction

Use different arrow semantics:

- channel: ion movement according to electrochemical driving force;
- pump/transporter: maintained transport tied to an energy source or coupled gradient where appropriate.

Do not label one arrow simply `passive` and the other universally `active`.

**Severity: P2**

---

# 3. Panel 2 action-potential trace is still too neuron-like

V2 improved the panel by removing the neuron illustration, but the graph still resembles a canonical action potential with a sharp positive spike.

That visually reintroduces the neural/excitable-cell bias.

Chapter 7's broader point is that electrical state matters beyond classical action potentials.

## V3 requirement

Replace the action-potential-shaped trace with a generic membrane-potential shift or state transition that does not imply a neuronal spike.

Possible representation:

`MEMBRANE POTENTIAL STATE A → CHANGE IN CHANNEL ACTIVITY / IONIC CURRENT → MEMBRANE POTENTIAL STATE B → CELLULAR RESPONSE`

A small inset can say:

> `Rapid action potentials are one familiar special case.`

**Severity: P2, but important to chapter identity**

---

# 4. Panel 2 still risks electrical-state → information collapse

The text is improved, but the causal architecture needs a visible receptor/response condition.

A change in voltage or ionic current is not automatically biologically meaningful information.

## V3 requirement

Canonical chain:

`CHANGE IN ELECTRICAL STATE`
→
`VOLTAGE-/ION-SENSITIVE CELLULAR MECHANISM`
→
`ALTERED CELLULAR RESPONSE`

Examples may include channel state, calcium signaling, secretion, contraction, migration, metabolism, or gene-regulatory effects depending on context.

This visibly preserves:

`ELECTRICAL STATE ≠ SIGNAL ≠ INFORMATION`.

**Severity: P2**

# 8. `Endogenous electric field (galvanic current)` should be corrected

An electric field and an electric current are related but not synonymous.

`Galvanic current` in parentheses risks conflating field with current.

## V3 requirement

Use separate terms:

- `endogenous electric field`
- `ionic current` where current is actually intended.

Electrotaxis/galvanotaxis can be named in caption/prose, but do not equate an electric field with a current.

**Severity: P2 scientific terminology**

# 9. `Nearby cells detect the electrical change` is too anthropomorphic/unspecified

Then:

> `In some cell types, electric fields can bias directional migration.`
