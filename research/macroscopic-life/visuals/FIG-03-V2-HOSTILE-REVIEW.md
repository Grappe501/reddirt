# Macroscopic Life — FIG-03 v2 Hostile Review

## Verdict

**P1 — ARCHITECTURE NOW WORKS; SCIENTIFIC COPY AND SCALE LOGIC REQUIRE ONE MAJOR CLEANUP PASS**

Figure 3 v2 is a large improvement over v1. The figure now has the correct conceptual spine: processes unfold across different timescales, ordinary human observation samples only a limited temporal window, and instruments/records can reveal dynamics that are too fast or too slow to observe directly.

The mountain/geological-change hook is now present and effective.

The remaining problems are not a reason to abandon the new architecture. They are a reason to clean it aggressively before lock. Most importantly, several exact time bins are scientifically arbitrary or internally inconsistent, the top-right copy repeats the rejected `time is information` claim, and the deep-time panel conflates biological evolution with planetary history.

# 3. Top-Right Statement

**P1 — REJECT**

The generated poster again says:

`Time is a form of information.`

This was explicitly rejected in the v1 hostile review.

Replace with:

> `Timing, duration, sequence, and rate of change can carry biologically and physically useful information.`

The next sentence also says every organism has evolved mechanisms to `detect, measure, and respond to change at the time scales that matter for its survival and niche.` This is too universal and teleological.

Replace with:

# 4. Exact Time Bins

**P1 — REMOVE FALSE PRECISION**

The poster assigns exact exponent ranges to each conceptual panel. Several boundaries are arbitrary and do not match the examples cleanly.

Examples:

- `milliseconds 10^-3–10^-1 s` excludes many neural and muscular processes outside that window;
- `seconds 10^-1–10^1 s` is not a meaningful universal category for coordinated action;
- `hours to days 10^1–10^5 s` begins at ten seconds, not hours;
- `seasons to years 10^5–10^8 s` begins around a day;
- `decades to centuries 10^8–10^11 s` begins at only a few years;
- `thousands to millions of years 10^11–10^14 s` begins at a few thousand years but ends at only a few million years;
- the axis labels and panel bins do not always correspond.

The logarithmic axis is useful. The categorical bins should be qualitative and the numerical axis should be deterministic and mathematically correct.

## Required correction

Keep one properly constructed logarithmic axis beneath the panels.

Use qualitative panel labels such as:

- `VERY FAST — milliseconds to seconds`
- `EVERYDAY — seconds to hours`
- `DAILY/CYCLIC — hours to days`
- `SEASONAL — months to years`
- `LONG-TERM — decades to centuries`
- `GEOLOGICAL — thousands to millions of years`
- `DEEP TIME — millions to billions of years`

Do not print artificial exponent boundaries inside each panel.

Prefer:

> `Many biological processes vary over daily cycles or respond to changes unfolding over hours to days.`

# 8. Seasons-to-Years Panel

**PASS WITH COPY CLEANUP**

Migration, breeding cycles, plant growth/dormancy are appropriate examples of recurring seasonal and annual dynamics.

No major conceptual problem remains.

# 9. Decades-to-Centuries Panel

**P2 — IMPORTANT DISTINCTION**

The panel currently says longer-lived organisms and ecosystems `respond across decades and centuries` and uses tree rings as an example.

Tree rings are primarily useful here as **records of annual growth conditions available to later observers**, not evidence that a tree centrally senses or integrates centuries as one timescale.

Forest succession and long-term ecological change are stronger examples.

Final wording should distinguish:

- process duration;
- persistence of organisms/ecosystems;
- physical records used by observers.

---

# 10. Geological Panel

**PASS WITH P2 PRECISION**

Glacier movement, river/canyon development, mountain uplift, and plate tectonics strongly support the chapter.

However, glacier motion can occur on daily/annual observable timescales as well as produce long-term landscape change. Do not classify `glacial movement` exclusively as a millions-of-years process.

Prefer `glacial landscape change` or use plate motion, uplift, erosion, basin formation, canyon incision, and long-term ice-sheet change.

---

# 11. Deep-Time Panel

**P1 — REBUILD CONTENT**

The panel combines:

- evolution of species;
- continental drift;
- climate cycles;
- rise of complex life;
- planetary imagery.

These processes do not share one single mechanism or characteristic time interval. More importantly, `planetary change` can occur at nearly every timescale.

The panel should be explicitly about **records and processes reconstructed across millions to billions of years**.

Recommended examples:

- evolutionary diversification/extinction over geological intervals;
- supercontinent cycles/continental rearrangement;
- long-term mountain/erosional history;
- major changes in atmospheric/ocean chemistry;
- planetary formation/history only if clearly separated from biological evolution.

Do not imply evolution occurs only in deep time; evolutionary change can be observed over much shorter intervals.

**PASS — STRONG; COPY CLEANUP REQUIRED**

# 13. Key Takeaways

**P1 — GENERATED COPY REVERTED TO REJECTED CLAIMS**

The poster's takeaway box reintroduced several v1 statements that were explicitly rejected.

The final deterministic six should be:

1. `Biological and physical processes unfold across widely different timescales.`
2. `What appears static, instantaneous, or absent can depend on the temporal resolution and duration of observation.`
3. `Unaided human observation has limited temporal resolution and duration.`
4. `Instruments and records allow us to resolve faster events and reconstruct slower processes.`
5. `Changing the observation window can reveal dynamics hidden at ordinary human timescales.`
6. `Temporal mismatch can hide a process from ordinary observation. Slowness, speed, or long duration alone is not evidence of life, intelligence, or higher-order agency.`

These are now the canonical Figure 3 takeaway statements.
