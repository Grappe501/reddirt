# Macroscopic Life — FIG-02 v2 Hostile Review

## Figure
**Figure 2 — Same Landscape, Different Sensory Access**

## Verdict
**P1 — MAJOR REVISION REQUIRED, BUT CONCEPTUAL ARCHITECTURE IS NOW CORRECT**

This version is substantially better than v1. The central scientific argument is now the right one: different organisms have access to different physical signals in a shared environment, and human instrumentation can extend access beyond unaided human senses. The figure also contains the crucial brake that an undetected signal is not permission to assume an unsupported phenomenon exists.

The remaining problems are primarily scientific wording, unsupported numerical precision, one recurring subjective-perception claim, and several visualization choices that could mislead if detached from the caption.

**PASS**

# 2. P1 — Subjective Experience Is Still Overclaimed

The subtitle says:

> `Every species experiences the same environment through a different set of sensory windows.`

`Experiences` is broader than the evidence shown. The figure establishes sensory detection and information access, not the complete subjective phenomenal experience of each species.

Likewise, the upper-right text refers to `perspectives` and `complete reality.`

## Required revision

Use:

> `Different species detect different portions of the physical information available in a shared environment.`

or:

> `Different sensory systems provide access to different physical signals in the same environment.`

Avoid implying that the infographic reconstructs an animal's subjective world.

---

# 3. P1 — Generated Numerical Precision Must Be Removed or Independently Sourced

The figure includes multiple numerical claims:

- human visible light ~400–700 nm;
- human hearing ~20 Hz–20,000 Hz;
- bee UV/visible ~300–700 nm;
- pit-viper infrared ~5,000–30,000 nm;
- bat echolocation ~20,000–120,000 Hz, `up to 200,000 Hz`;
- shark electric field ~0.1–10 µV/cm;
- chemical detection `parts per billion or lower`;
- migratory bird field ~25–65 microtesla.

Some of these are useful order-of-magnitude educational ranges, but several vary by species, context, age, experimental setup, wavelength sensitivity threshold, or behavioral definition. The generated figure gives them a precision that visually reads as canonical.

## Required revision

For the publication figure either:

1. remove most numerical ranges and keep qualitative signal categories; or
2. deterministically insert only independently sourced ranges with species/context qualification.

Preferred short-book solution: **reduce numbers aggressively.** The scientific point does not require a wall of exact ranges.

---

# 4. P1 — Shark Electric-Field Visualization Still Risks Field/Aura Misreading

The shark panel uses broad concentric/dotted field lines around the animal and prey. This is less problematic than the rejected human-aura image in FIG-07, but the same principle applies: decorative field geometry can be mistaken for a literal depiction of the animal's perceptual world or of visible energy.

## Required revision

Use a restrained schematic:

`prey muscle/nerve activity → weak local electric potential in seawater → ampullae of Lorenzini → neural signal`

Do not surround the shark with dramatic luminous field graphics.

Label the visualization explicitly as a schematic representation of a physical signal.

---

# 5. P1 — Migratory-Bird Magnetoreception Needs Stronger Scientific Qualification

The figure says:

> `Many migratory birds can detect Earth's magnetic field, helping them navigate across thousands of miles.`

This broad claim is reasonable at the level of magnetic compass/navigation evidence, but the visual compass/grid may imply a settled, compass-like sensory organ and a single established mechanism.

Magnetoreception mechanisms remain an active research area, with evidence involving multiple candidate mechanisms and species-specific systems.

## Required revision

Use wording such as:

> `Many migratory birds use information associated with Earth's magnetic field as one cue for orientation and navigation. The underlying biological mechanisms remain an active area of research.`

Visually label the magnetic overlay as **schematic**.

---

# 6. P2 — Bee Panel Is Too Universal

`Bees see ultraviolet light` is broadly useful educational shorthand, but the figure should avoid implying one identical sensory range and polarization use across every bee species and behavioral context.

## Revision

Prefer:

> `Many bees detect ultraviolet wavelengths unavailable to human vision, revealing floral patterns that can guide foraging. Some also use polarized-light information in navigation.`

This is more accurate and keeps the mechanism tied to function.

---

# 7. P2 — Pit-Viper Language Should Avoid Literal Heat Vision

The panel title `Infrared heat` is acceptable shorthand, but the inset thermographic mouse can still imply that a pit viper sees a camera-like thermal image.

Pit organs detect infrared radiation and provide spatial thermal information, but the subjective percept should not be represented as known.

## Revision

Label the inset:

`False-color illustration of thermal contrast — not a literal reconstruction of snake vision.`

Panel heading can become:

**Infrared-sensitive pit organs**

---

# 8. P2 — Bat Panel Uses “See” Metaphor

The text says returning echoes allow bats to `see` their environment.

This is a common metaphor but unnecessary in a figure whose entire job is to separate sensory modalities.

## Revision

Use:

> `Bats emit high-frequency calls and use returning echoes to estimate features such as distance, direction, motion, and object structure.`

Avoid quotation-mark `see` language.

---

# 9. P2 — Human Panel Risks “Five-Senses” Simplification

The human panel does acknowledge smell, taste, touch, and balance, which is good. But the overall figure should not imply humans have only vision/hearing while the other organisms possess exotic senses.

Humans also detect temperature, proprioception, vestibular acceleration, nociception, internal physiological state, and other modalities.

## Revision

Add a small note:

> `Humans also possess multiple internal and body-position senses; the panel highlights only selected environmental channels for comparison.`

---

# 10. P2 — “No Organism Perceives the Entire Reality” Is Philosophically Stronger Than Needed

Key takeaway 3 says:

> `No organism perceives the entire reality — only a useful version for its survival.`

The first half is intuitively defensible but impossible to establish literally for `entire reality`; the second introduces an adaptationist formulation that can sound as if every sensory feature exists only because it is directly useful for survival.

## Required replacement

> `No known organism detects every kind of physical signal present in its environment.`

This is narrower, empirical, and sufficient.

---

# 11. P2 — “Invisible Does Not Mean Nonexistent” Needs Its Brake Attached

This phrase is rhetorically powerful and true in the intended physical-detection context, but it is also highly reusable by pseudoscientific arguments.

It should never stand alone.

## Required paired wording

> `Some real physical signals fall outside unaided human detection. Their existence is established through reproducible measurement — not by invisibility itself.`

**Electroreception and chemical sensing**
Mechanism schematic rather than glowing field art.

### MIGRATORY BIRD
**Magnetic orientation cues**
Mechanism explicitly described as an active research area.

### TOOL BRIDGE
Keep telescopes / microphones / thermal imaging / magnetometers / chemical analysis as examples of instruments extending measurement.

### FINAL BRAKE

> `Different sensory systems sample different physical information from a shared world. Human instruments extend that access further. A signal's absence from unaided perception is a reason to measure — not evidence that an unsupported hidden phenomenon exists.`
