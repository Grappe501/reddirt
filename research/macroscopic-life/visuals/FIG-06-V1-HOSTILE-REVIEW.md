# Macroscopic Life — FIG-06 v1 Hostile Review

## Verdict

**P1 — VISUALLY STRONG, BUT IT REVERTED TO A GENERIC `ORGANISMHOOD SPECTRUM` AND MISSED THE CHAPTER'S CENTRAL BOUNDARY EXPERIMENT. STRUCTURAL REBUILD REQUIRED.**

Figure 6 v1 looks coherent and belongs aesthetically with Figures 4–5, but hostile review finds that it answers the wrong question. It largely asks `which systems count as individuals?` and repeats Figure 5's comparison of bacteria, multicellular organisms, eusocial colonies, coral, and endosymbiosis.

Chapter 6 must instead ask:

> **Within one familiar organism, what measurable processes determine where that individual begins and ends?**

The strongest version of Figure 6 should therefore center one human organism and deliberately produce several different boundary maps: physical/epithelial, physiological/regulatory, immune/membership, developmental/lineage, ecological/symbiotic, and functional/extended effects.

Do not polish v1. Rebuild v2 around `one organism — several measurable boundaries.`

# 7. Human body case study

**P0/P1 — SCIENTIFICALLY WRONG GENERATED COPY**

The generated human-body panel says:

- `Trillions of microbial partners (microbiome)`;
- `Cells cannot survive alone`;
- `Highly integrated functions`;
- `Reproduces as a unit`;
- conclusion `One individual (with essential partners).`

Several problems:

### `Cells cannot survive alone`
Too categorical. Many human cells can survive for varying periods ex vivo under appropriate conditions; independence varies dramatically by cell type and environment.

### `essential partners`
This overstates the microbiome. Some microbial functions can be important or even necessary in particular contexts, but `the microbiome` is not one fixed set of essential partners required to define every human individual.

### `trillions`
Unnecessary generated quantitative claim.

Replace this panel with the symbiosis distinction:

`PARTNER, RESIDENT, COMPONENT — THESE ARE DIFFERENT CLAIMS`

Canonical:

> `Human-associated microbes can be persistent, functionally important, and physiologically influential without being equivalent to host cells or automatically belonging to the human individual under every biological criterion.`

# 11. Missing immune-membership complexity

**P1 — MAJOR OMISSION**

The generated figure says immune systems distinguish self from non-self. That is too simplistic for a figure specifically about biological boundaries.

Required panel:

`MEMBERSHIP IS NOT A SIMPLE SELF / NON-SELF SWITCH`

Examples:

- host cells are usually tolerated;
- commensal microbes can be tolerated in context;
- pathogens can trigger responses;
- transplanted tissue may be rejected or tolerated under immunosuppression;
- tumors arise from host lineage yet can evade or interact with immune surveillance;
- pregnancy involves genetically distinct fetal/placental tissues with specialized immune regulation.

Canonical:

> `Immune recognition contributes to biological membership and boundary maintenance, but immune systems respond to context, location, molecular patterns, damage, developmental history, and regulatory signals rather than applying one perfect self/non-self label.`

This is exactly the kind of counterintuitive science Chapter 6 needs.

# 19. Pseudoscience / Macroscopic-Life audit

**CURRENT: FAIL / REBUILD**

The figure's closing move toward ecosystems/civilizations, combined with `individuality is a spectrum` and the generic continuum, creates exactly the inferential shortcut we have been trying to prevent.

A skeptical reader could detach the figure and infer:

`boundaries are fuzzy → individuality is relative → ecosystems/civilizations may be organisms.`

That is not what the established biology demonstrates.

V2 must instead establish:

`boundaries can be multiple, dynamic, and permeable → each proposed boundary still requires measurable mechanisms → functional connection is not enough.`

That is both scientifically stronger and much more useful later.
