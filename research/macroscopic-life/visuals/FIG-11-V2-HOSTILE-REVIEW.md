This belongs primarily to Chapter 10 memory/priming unless a separate predictive cue causes advance preparation before a later event.

### Required action

## P1-5 — `Prediction claims knowledge of a specific future event` is scientifically wrong as a general distinction

The Key Distinctions panel says prediction claims knowledge of a specific future event and requires stronger evidence.

That is too narrow and philosophically loaded.

Scientific predictions are often probabilistic.

A forecast can predict:

- a probability distribution;
- increased likelihood;
- a range;
- a conditional outcome;
- an expected state;
- timing with uncertainty.

Prediction does not require `knowledge` of one specific future event.

### Required replacement

> **Prediction commits prospectively to an expected future state, event, timing, or probability distribution strongly enough that later outcomes can count for or against the prediction.**

Then:

> **Prediction does not require certainty. It requires prospective content and the possibility of error.**
