# FIG-01 — The Embedded Observer (Revised)

## Verdict

**P1 — REBUILD CONCEPTUAL SPINE**

## Improvement

The revised figure more clearly communicates nested scales and embedded observation. It is visually coherent with the emerging book style.

## Remaining failure

The canonical Chapter 1 thought experiment is not Earth → forest → deer → nervous system → neuron → consciousness.

The chapter's epistemic engine is an **intelligent microscopic observer embedded inside a human body** that can construct an accurate local science while remaining unable to directly infer the containing human organism.

The revised image still turns the argument into a general hierarchy-of-scale/perception figure. This weakens the precise thought experiment and creates an unnecessary Earth-as-living-system implication at the opening of the book.

### Specific issue

Labeling Earth as `THE LARGER SYSTEM` alongside `A LIVING ORGANISM`/`A LIVING CREATURE` visually prepares the reader to classify Earth as a containing life level before the book has earned that move.

## Required correction

Return to the canonical nested cutaway:

`microscopic intelligent observer → local tissue/cellular environment → organ/body context → human organism`

The human reader sees all levels; the embedded observer has access only to the local level.

Use a clearly marked thought-experiment observer rather than a real microbe if necessary to avoid attributing human-like intelligence to microorganisms.

## Required brake

`Thought experiment: observational limits do not demonstrate that humans themselves are components of a larger organism.`

# FIG-07 — The Body Electric (Revised)

## Verdict

**P1 — MAJOR SCIENCE/CLAIM REVISION**

## Improvements

- The text now explicitly says electromagnetic fields are weak and not a mystical aura.
- The body-electric framing is more grounded in measurable electrophysiology.
- Membrane potential, neural signaling, ECG/EEG, and sensory transduction are more clearly distinguished.

## Remaining failures

### 1. The aura-shaped field remains

The visual itself still depicts a human surrounded by large symmetrical field lines. The caption may deny an aura, but the screenshot test fails: detached from its prose, the image can be reused as an apparent scientific aura diagram.

**Required correction:** remove the body-surrounding field-line image entirely. Replace it with instrumentation: electrodes, voltage traces, localized tissue voltage maps, or near-source measurement diagrams.

### 2. Chapter emphasis is still too nervous-system-centric

The canonical Chapter 7 visual must make the important move beyond neurons:

`membrane potential → coupled cells/gap junctions → tissue-scale voltage pattern → wound/developmental response`

The current central nervous-system rendering makes the reader think `body electric = nerves plus heart` rather than developmental/non-neural bioelectricity.

### 3. Organs claim is too broad

`ORGANS — Use electrical signals to coordinate function (e.g., stomach, intestines, pancreas)` needs mechanism-specific qualification. Electrical activity differs substantially across excitable tissues, epithelia, endocrine tissue, smooth muscle, and developmental contexts.

## Required rebuild spine

Make the primary figure a **multiscale mechanism diagram**, not an electrified human silhouette.

Include:
- membrane ion gradients;
- membrane potential;
- ion channels;
- gap junction coupling;
- tissue voltage state;
- wound current/electrotaxis;
- developmental patterning example;
- measurement instrument cue.

Neural and cardiac examples can remain as familiar anchors but should not dominate.

# FIG-14 — Civilization Under the Organism Test (Revised)

## Verdict

**P1 — MAJOR CONCEPTUAL REVISION**

## Improvements

- `This is a functional comparison, not a biological identity` is substantially better than the prior wording.
- Important limitations are more visible.
- The image explicitly says civilization is not a single organism.

## Remaining failures

### 1. The conclusion still overclaims

`Life can emerge at many scales. Civilization may be one of them.` visually presents civilization as a candidate *scale of life* rather than a candidate for testing higher-order individuality.

Replace with something closer to:

> `Civilization has system-level capabilities no individual human possesses. Whether those capabilities amount to higher-order biological individuality remains an open question.`

### 2. Reproduction is misclassified

`We reproduce ideas, technologies, institutions, and cultures` is not equivalent to organism-level reproduction/heredity. Cultural replication is relevant to information propagation but does not satisfy the canonical reproduction/heredity problem for a civilization-level individual.

The reproduction cell should be **weak/unresolved**, not affirmative.

### 3. Boundaries are too confidently asserted

Atmosphere/resource/information flows do not establish a civilization-level self-maintaining boundary. Boundary should be mixed/unresolved.

### 4. Homeostasis is too generously analogized

Markets and governments can regulate variables, but that does not automatically constitute integrated organism-level homeostasis. Mark as partial/heterogeneous.

### 5. Defense is ambiguous

Military, law, disaster response, cybersecurity, and public health can protect subgroups or institutions while also producing internal conflict. This is not clean system-level immune equivalence.

## Required design change

Return to the canonical **assessment matrix** instead of ten affirmative feature cards.

The visual should make civilization look impressive on communication, distributed memory, infrastructure, prediction, and some repair/adaptation while visibly weak or unresolved on:

- coherent boundary;
- conflict suppression;
- unified higher-level goals;
- reproduction/heredity;
- Darwinian individuality;
- model competition against simpler network explanations.

The figure should make the hypothesis harder, not easier.
