# FINAL CAPTION DRAFT

> **Figure 10. How the Past Stays Active — From Physical Trace to Altered Later Response.** The word `memory` is used differently across neuroscience, immunology, cell biology, computing, and organizational studies. This figure therefore presents a comparative analytical scaffold rather than a universal memory mechanism. For the book's functional comparison, a candidate memory process begins when a prior event or state produces a physical change that persists and later becomes causally relevant to a different response or state. Neural, adaptive immune, cellular/developmental, and externalized/organizational examples achieve this through different physical carriers and mechanisms. Persistence alone is insufficient: a physical trace may record history without later being used by the material or system that carries it. Component turnover likewise does not establish organismhood; the relevant question is whether information important to a proposed system remains causally available despite turnover, through mechanisms that can be identified and perturbed. Functional memory does not imply conscious recollection, and memory distributed across components does not by itself establish a larger conscious rememberer.
