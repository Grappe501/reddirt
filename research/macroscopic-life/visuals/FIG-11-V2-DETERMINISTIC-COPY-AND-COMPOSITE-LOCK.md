# 5. SCIENTIFIC DEFINITION OF PREDICTION

Use this exact deterministic definition:

> **Prediction commits prospectively to an expected future state, event, timing, or probability distribution strongly enough that later outcomes can count for or against it.**

Do not use the generated V2 language claiming prediction requires `knowledge of a specific future event.`

Scientific predictions can be probabilistic.

# 6. TEST 6 — PREDICTION ADVANTAGE

Header:

**TEST 6 — PREDICTION ADVANTAGE**

Canonical question:

> **Does advance preparation based on present information about recurring structure improve performance compared with an otherwise appropriate system that reacts only after the event occurs?**

Minimum test sequence:

1. **IDENTIFY** — Define the proposed predictive cue or internal state.
2. **MECHANISM** — Show how it causes advance preparation.
3. **COMPARE** — Measure performance against an appropriate reactive baseline.
4. **COMMIT PROSPECTIVELY** — Specify the expected relationship before the outcome is known.
5. **ALLOW FAILURE** — Show conditions under which the prediction can be wrong or costly.
6. **PERTURB** — Disrupt the proposed cue, clock, model, or pathway.
7. **RETEST** — Ask whether the advantage disappears or changes predictably.
8. **RULE OUT SIMPLER MODELS** — Immediate triggering, generalized stress, ordinary reaction, or lower-level component explanations must be considered.

Locked line:

> **PREDICTION ADVANTAGE IS A PERFORMANCE CLAIM, NOT A CONSCIOUSNESS CLAIM.**

Perturbation banner:

> **DISRUPT THE PROPOSED CUE, CLOCK, MODEL, OR PATHWAY. DOES THE ADVANTAGE CHANGE PREDICTABLY?**
