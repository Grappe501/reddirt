# P1-1 — THE FIGURE DOES NOT MAKE THE CENTRAL `ABSENCE → PRESENT PHYSICAL CHANGES` MOVE STRONGLY ENOUGH

The architecture's most important mechanistic insight is:

> **A macroscopic absence can become microscopic actionable signals.**

V1 instead moves quickly from amputation to `early responses reorganize the site.`

That misses the chapter's deepest explanatory step.

V2 should dedicate the second panel to:

**WHAT CHANGES IN THE TISSUE THAT REMAINS?**

Show only categories:

- wound edge / lost contacts;
- changed geometry;
- chemical/inflammatory cues;
- mechanical conditions;
- ionic/electrical conditions;
- neural/vascular disruption.

Centerpiece:

> **ABSENCE BECOMES MEASURABLE DIFFERENCES IN WHAT REMAINS.**

This should replace much of V1's blastema-heavy early-response panel.

---

# P1-2 — `DEDifferentiated cells (re-enter cell cycle)` IS TOO GENERAL

The generated figure implies dedifferentiation as a generic defining mechanism of salamander limb regeneration.

Cellular contributions to salamander blastemas are lineage- and tissue-dependent; different source populations can behave differently, and broad `cells dedifferentiate` language is too blunt for a canonical figure.

V2 should use:

> **Regeneration-associated cells proliferate and change state; their origins and lineage restrictions vary by tissue and system.**

Or avoid this detail entirely in the figure and reserve it for prose/endnotes.

---

# P1-3 — BLASTEMA COPY IS TOO SIMPLIFIED

V1 says:

> `A blastema (pool of progenitor cells) forms in many regenerating species.`

This is unsafe as a broad cross-species statement.

`Blastema` has system-specific usage, and planarian regeneration should not simply be collapsed into the salamander blastema model.

V2 should either:

- restrict blastema explicitly to the salamander limb example; or
- remove the term from the main universal pathway.

Recommended:

> **In salamander limb regeneration, a blastema forms from regeneration-associated cells beneath the wound epidermis.**

Then firewall:

> **A blastema is not a miniature limb or complete anatomical blueprint.**

---

# P1-4 — PANEL 3'S SIGNAL MAP IS OVER-SPECIFIC AND SCIENTIFICALLY RISKY

The colored list:

- FGF = proximal/outgrowth
- Wnt = outgrowth/identity
- RA = proximal-distal pattern
- BMP/Notch/etc.

looks authoritative but compresses highly context-dependent pathways into one-word functions.

This is exactly the kind of AI-generated scientific legend that should not become canonical.

V2 should replace the pathway legend with mechanism classes:

**POSITIONAL STATE**
**LOCAL SIGNALING**
**CELL–CELL INTERACTION**
**NERVE-DEPENDENT INPUTS**
**MECHANICS / GEOMETRY**

If named pathways are retained at all, they belong in deterministic caption/endnotes, not in the visual core.

# P1-5 — `CELLS INTERPRET MULTIPLE OVERLAPPING SIGNALS` IS ACCEPTABLE, BUT `POSITION AND IDENTITY EMERGE FROM LOCAL INTERACTIONS` IS TOO TOTALIZING

Position-related states can be inherited from developmental history and retained by surviving tissues; they do not necessarily newly `emerge` only after injury.

V2 should say:

> **Surviving positional states interact with wound-induced signals and local tissue conditions.**

This creates the correct bridge to Chapter 10:

> **Past developmental history can remain physically represented in surviving tissue and alter what that tissue does after injury.**

---

# P1-6 — PANEL 5 OVERSTATES A UNIVERSAL `CORRECT SIZE AND SHAPE` RESULT

V1 says:

> `Regeneration produces the correct size and shape for the organism.`

That is too categorical.

Regeneration can be incomplete, variable, age-dependent, species-dependent, and imperfect.

Replace with:

> **Successful regeneration requires appropriate patterning, integration, proportion, and termination.**

And:

> **Regenerative outcomes vary across species, tissues, life stages, and conditions.**

Do not visually define regeneration by perfect restoration.

---

# P1-7 — SPECIES PANEL CONTAINS UNSAFE / DISTRACTING CLAIMS

The axolotl text says it regenerates `limbs, tail, parts of heart and brain.` Even if defensible under carefully qualified literature, this is not needed for the chapter and invites a citation fight unrelated to the argument.

The planarian line says `regenerates entire body from a fragment`, which is too universal and lacks species/fragment qualification.

The lizard is also conceptually distracting because tail regeneration is not equivalent to salamander limb regeneration and regenerated lizard tails differ anatomically from originals.

V2 should simplify this panel drastically.

Recommended title:

**REGENERATIVE CAPACITY VARIES**

Use:

- salamander limb — complex appendage regeneration;
- selected planarian species — extensive body-pattern regeneration;
- mammalian skin/liver or lizard tail only as clearly labeled contrasting repair/regrowth examples if space genuinely helps.

Canonical footer:

> **Different organisms restore different structures by related and distinct mechanisms. `Regeneration` is not one uniform capability.**
