# Macroscopic Life — FIG-06 v1 Build Spec

## Figure
**Figure 6 — Where Does the Individual End?**

## Chapter function
Figures 4 and 5 established that biological individuals can be built from living components and that higher-level individuality can evolve. Figure 6 asks the boundary question:

> **If individuality is constructed through relationships and regulation, what actually determines which components and processes belong to the individual?**

This figure must replace the intuitive equation `individual = everything inside the skin` with a mechanistic view of biological boundaries while preserving a strong brake: fuzzy, dynamic, or relational boundaries are still boundaries, and they do not license arbitrary expansion of the self.

---

# 1. Core thesis

> `Biological boundaries are maintained by interacting physical, physiological, developmental, immune, ecological, and regulatory processes. Different questions can therefore reveal different but measurable boundaries of the same organism.`

Critical brake:

> `A boundary can be dynamic, permeable, or context-dependent without disappearing. Blurred boundaries are not evidence that every connected system forms one individual.`

---

# 2. Primary visual — one human, multiple measurable boundaries

Center the composition on one ordinary human organism, surrounded by nested/overlapping boundary layers.

Do NOT use mystical glowing aura imagery.

The layers should be clearly technical/schematic and differently labeled:

### PHYSICAL / EPITHELIAL BOUNDARY
Skin and epithelial surfaces physically separate tissues from much of the external environment while allowing controlled exchange.

### PHYSIOLOGICAL / REGULATORY BOUNDARY
Circulation, endocrine regulation, neural signaling, fluid compartments, homeostatic feedback, and coordinated metabolism link distant tissues into organism-level physiology.

### IMMUNE / MEMBERSHIP BOUNDARY
Immune systems distinguish and respond to cells, molecules, microbes, grafts, damage, and threats through context-dependent recognition and tolerance mechanisms. Avoid simplistic `self vs non-self` wording as a complete theory.

### DEVELOPMENTAL / LINEAGE BOUNDARY
Most human cells share developmental history from the zygote, but chimerism, transplantation, pregnancy, microbial symbiosis, and other cases show that lineage alone is not a complete boundary criterion.

### ECOLOGICAL / SYMBIOTIC INTERFACE
Human-associated microbial communities occupy surfaces and cavities and interact causally with host physiology without simply becoming equivalent to human cells.

### FUNCTIONAL / EXTENDED EFFECTS
Organisms can alter environments and use external structures/tools in ways that feed back on survival and reproduction. Extended phenotype or tool incorporation does not automatically make the external object part of the biological individual.

---

# 3. Boundary question panel

Title:
`ONE ORGANISM — SEVERAL BOUNDARY QUESTIONS`

Questions:

- `What is physically enclosed?`
- `What is physiologically regulated together?`
- `What shares developmental lineage?`
- `What is recognized/tolerated by immune processes?`
- `What is required for characteristic function?`
- `What persists as membership through turnover?`
- `What participates in reproduction/heredity at the proposed level?`

Footer:
`Different questions can produce different boundary maps. The maps must still be measurable.`

---

# 4. Controlled exchange panel

Show that organism boundaries are not sealed walls.

Sequence:

`ENVIRONMENT ⇄ BOUNDARY ⇄ ORGANISM`

Flows:
- oxygen / carbon dioxide;
- water;
- nutrients;
- heat;
- waste;
- signals;
- microbes/particles under regulated and unregulated conditions.

Canonical statement:

> `Living boundaries maintain organization while continuously exchanging matter, energy, and information with the environment.`

Brake:

> `Exchange across a boundary does not erase the boundary.`

---

# 5. Immune boundary — replace simple self/non-self cartoon

Title:
`MEMBERSHIP IS NOT A SIMPLE SELF / NON-SELF SWITCH`

Use examples:
- host cells usually tolerated;
- commensal microbes can be tolerated in context;
- pathogens can trigger responses;
- transplanted tissue may be rejected or tolerated under immunosuppression;
- tumors arise from host lineage yet may evade or interact with immune surveillance;
- pregnancy involves genetically distinct fetal/placental tissues with specialized immune regulation.

Canonical takeaway:

> `Immune recognition contributes to biological membership and boundary maintenance, but immune systems respond to context, location, molecular patterns, damage, developmental history, and regulatory signals rather than applying one perfect self/non-self label.`

---

# 6. Transplant / replacement thought experiment

Title:
`HOW MUCH CAN CHANGE WHILE THE INDIVIDUAL PERSISTS?`

Show a simple timeline:

`organism at t1 → cell turnover / microbiome change / implanted device / transplanted organ → organism at t2`

Do not make philosophical identity claims.

Canonical:

> `Many components can be replaced or altered while organism-level organization and continuity persist.`

Then:

> `Persistence therefore depends on more than retaining exactly the same material components.`

---

# 7. Symbiosis / microbiome panel

Title:
`PARTNER, RESIDENT, COMPONENT — THESE ARE DIFFERENT CLAIMS`

Use gut/skin schematic.

Distinguish:

`HUMAN CELL`
`ASSOCIATED MICROBE`
`TRANSIENT ENVIRONMENTAL ORGANISM`

Canonical:

> `Human-associated microbes can be persistent, functionally important, and physiologically influential without being equivalent to host cells or automatically belonging to the human individual under every biological criterion.`

This panel must prevent the sloppy `you are mostly microbes / you are an ecosystem` trope.

---

# 8. Extended phenotype / tools panel

Title:
`FUNCTION CAN EXTEND BEYOND THE BODY WITHOUT ERASING ORGANISM BOUNDARIES`

Examples:
- beaver dam;
- spider web;
- bird nest;
- human prosthetic or tool.

Canonical:

> `An organism can causally organize structures outside its body. External functional coupling can matter biologically without making every affected structure part of the organism itself.`

This distinction is essential preparation for later civilization/network chapters.

---

# 9. Boundary comparison matrix

Rows:
- physical enclosure;
- physiological coupling;
- developmental lineage;
- immune interaction/tolerance;
- functional dependence;
- turnover persistence;
- reproductive/hereditary participation.

Columns/examples:
- human somatic cell;
- gut microbe;
- transplanted organ;
- prosthetic device;
- external tool/structure.

Use qualitative terms:
`strong / partial / variable / weak / not applicable`

No organism-membership score and no single final `yes/no part of self` row.

Purpose: demonstrate that membership depends on the biological question and evidence.

---

# 10. What does NOT follow

Prominent scientific firewall:

> `A vague or permeable boundary is not the absence of a boundary.`

> `Functional connection does not automatically establish common biological individuality.`

> `Symbiosis does not automatically merge partners into one organism.`

> `The extended phenotype does not mean an organism literally becomes everything it influences.`

> `Boundary questions must be resolved with measurable mechanisms, not metaphor.`

---

# 11. Canonical six takeaways

1. `Living organisms maintain boundaries while continuously exchanging matter, energy, and information with their environments.`
2. `Physical enclosure is only one dimension of biological boundary; physiological regulation, developmental history, immune processes, and reproduction can define additional boundary relationships.`
3. `Many material components can turn over or be replaced while organism-level organization persists.`
4. `Symbiotic microbes can strongly influence a host without automatically becoming equivalent to host cells or members of the same biological individual under every criterion.`
5. `Organisms can extend functional effects into tools, nests, webs, dams, and other external structures without erasing the distinction between organism and environment.`
6. `A fuzzy, permeable, or context-dependent boundary still requires measurable membership and integration; connectedness alone does not establish individuality.`

---

# 12. Chapter 7 handoff

Chapter 6 must end by moving from boundary to coordination.

Canonical question:

> `If the parts of an organism cannot see the whole, what physical processes allow the whole to coordinate them?`

This leads directly to Chapter 7 — The Body Electric.

Alternative supporting line:

> `A boundary tells us what may belong to the system. Persistence requires the system to coordinate what happens inside it.`

# 14. Screenshot-safe conclusion

> `Biological individuality does not require a perfectly sealed edge, but it does require demonstrable processes that maintain membership, coordination, persistence, and distinction from the environment.`

Footer candidate:

`BOUNDARIES CAN BE PERMEABLE WITHOUT BEING MEANINGLESS.`

---

# 15. Acceptance target

A skeptical reader should leave Figure 6 understanding simultaneously:

1. the skin is not the only biologically meaningful boundary;
2. living boundaries are dynamic and exchange-rich;
3. different biological questions can produce different measurable boundary maps;
4. microbiomes, transplants, pregnancy, turnover, prosthetics, and extended phenotypes complicate simplistic boundary intuitions;
5. none of this licenses the claim that every connected network or environment is part of one organism.

The figure should make the boundary problem scientifically richer while making arbitrary Macroscopic Life claims harder, not easier.
