## Verdict

The V4 render is visually strong and substantially better than the quarantined V3, but it does **not** pass publication hostile review.

The failure is scientific, not aesthetic.

The render correctly removes Model D and correctly foregrounds the distinction among complexity, integration, and individuality. However, several example labels silently misuse the final A/B/C/U/X model taxonomy as though Model A, Model B, and Model C were grades or types of biological individual. That is incompatible with the frozen doctrine.

## P1 — Model taxonomy is misused in the example strip

The rendered example tiles include language such as:

- `Cell ... Individual (Model A)`
- `Colony ... Individual (Model B)`
- `Organism ... Individual (Model C)`
- `Ecosystem ... Candidate individual (Model U / Model X)`

This is not what A/B/C/U/X means in Book One.

The final taxonomy is an **outcome classification for a specified hypothesis/test**:

- Model A — lower-level/association sufficiency;
- Model B — higher-level organizational utility;
- Model C — property-specific stronger claim;
- U — unresolved;
- X — invalid test/inference.

A cell is not intrinsically “Model A.” A coral colony is not intrinsically “Model B.” A human organism is not intrinsically “Model C.” An ecosystem is not intrinsically “Model U/X.” The model label depends on the particular property, boundary, rivals, evidence, and test.

This is exactly the kind of silent category migration the book is designed to prevent.

**Required repair:** remove all A/B/C/U/X labels from the example tiles. Figure 14 does not need model taxonomy at all.

## P1 — The examples make categorical individuality judgments too cheaply

The render labels the snowflake `Not an individual (in the biological sense)`, hurricane `Often not an individual`, cell `A minimal individual`, colony `A higher-level individual`, organism `An individual at the organism level`, and ecosystem `A possible individual at an ecological level`.

Some of these are useful intuitions, but the chapter's governing point is that *individual* is framework-dependent. The visual should not teach the reader that a six-tile lookup table can decide individuality by object class.

The coral-colony tile is especially risky because colonial organisms and modular organisms raise exactly the boundary/individuality complications Chapter 12 is trying to teach.

**Required repair:** convert the examples from verdicts to **questions/evidence burdens**.

Recommended example language:

- Snowflake — `Complex pattern; biological individuality is not the relevant default claim.`
- Hurricane — `Coherent dynamic system; useful adversary for weak individuality criteria.`
- Cell — `Strong biological individuality case under many frameworks; still physically nested.`
- Colony — `Individuality can depend on physiological, developmental, or evolutionary framework.`
- Organism — `Familiar strong individuality case; composed of lower-level individuals/components.`
- Ecosystem — `Candidate higher-level organization; stronger individuality must be tested, not assumed.`

## P1 — Individuality panel is too singular

The heading asks:

`What is a distinct, persisting whole in its environment?`

and the bullets imply one generic individuality checklist.

But Chapter 12 explicitly teaches that physiological, evolutionary, developmental, ecological, autonomy-based, and other individuality frameworks can identify different empirical questions and sometimes different boundaries.

The panel needs to say **framework specified** visibly, not merely rely on the lower warning band.

**Required repair:** change the panel heading to:

`INDIVIDUALITY — FRAMEWORK SPECIFIED`

Question:

`Under the named framework, what evidence makes this proposed whole a scientifically useful individual?`

Suggested callouts can remain boundary, persistence, causal integration, membership consequences, conflict mediation, lineage/reproduction — but they should be presented as **possible criteria whose relevance depends on framework**, not a universal checklist.
