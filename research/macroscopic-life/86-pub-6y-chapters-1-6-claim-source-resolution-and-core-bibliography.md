# CHAPTER 5 — WHEN PARTS BECOME A WHOLE

This chapter carries the highest source burden in Chapters 1–6.

## [5.1] Major evolutionary transitions / individuality

**Burden:** B5.

### Core resolved source

West, Fisher, Gardner & Kiers (2015), *Major evolutionary transitions in individuality*, PNAS.

Key support:

- major transitions can involve previously more independently replicating entities becoming components of a more complex higher-level entity;
- formation of a cooperative group and transformation into an integrated entity are distinct problems;
- cooperation alone does not establish a transition;
- division of labor, communication, dependence, and reduced conflict recur in important transitions;
- not all cooperative groups are on a trajectory toward higher-level individuality.

### Additional core source

Michod (2007), *Evolution of individuality during the transition from unicellular to multicellular life*.

Supports the evolutionary-individuality framing, including transfer/reorganization of fitness and the transition from cells toward multicellular evolutionary individuals.

### Historical theoretical anchor

Michod & Roze (1997), *Transitions in individuality*.

Supports the centrality of cooperation and conflict regulation in the emergence of higher-level individuality.

**Status:** STRONGLY RESOLVED.

## [5.2] Conflict suppression / fitness alignment

**Burden:** B5.

**Resolved sources:** West et al. (2015); Bourke (2023), *Conflict and conflict resolution in the major transitions*; Michod & Roze (1997).

Important nuance: the literature contains disagreement over exactly when active conflict-suppression mechanisms are required versus when restrictive group formation, clonality, monogamy, or aligned inclusive-fitness interests prevent substantial conflict from arising in the first place.

Therefore the book should say:

> mechanisms and evolutionary conditions that reduce, prevent, constrain, or mediate destructive lower-level conflict

rather than implying that every transition requires a separately evolved policing system.

**Current prose largely respects this.**

**Status:** RESOLVED WITH LIVE-THEORY NUANCE.

# CRITICAL SOURCE WARNINGS DISCOVERED IN THIS PASS

## 1. Do not source `individuality` from one school only

The literature contains competing frameworks.

The book is strongest when it acknowledges that plurality rather than pretending one definition won.

## 2. Do not turn West et al.'s criteria into universal necessary-and-sufficient laws

Their major-transition framework is highly useful but represents a particular evolutionary focus.

Book One intentionally asks a broader question spanning physiological, organizational, and evolutionary dimensions.

## 3. Do not treat conflict suppression as always an active policing mechanism

Some transitions minimize conflict through group formation, clonality, monogamy, vertical transmission, or aligned interests rather than later suppression.

## 4. Do not equate holobiont with evolutionary individual

Host-microbe functional integration and host-microbe individuality are separate claims.

## 5. Do not use magnetoreception as though its molecular receptor problem is solved

Behavioral evidence is much stronger than receptor-level consensus.

This uncertainty is useful for the book because it demonstrates how science can accept a phenomenon while still disputing mechanism.

## 6. Do not use an exact number of independent multicellularity origins unless the definition and source are supplied

`Evolved repeatedly` is safer and scientifically sufficient for Book One.

## 7. Do not imply that biological scaling has one settled universal cause

Scaling patterns are real; causal interpretation remains contested in important respects.
