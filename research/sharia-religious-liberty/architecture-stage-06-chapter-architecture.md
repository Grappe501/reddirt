# Architecture Stage 6 — Chapter Architecture

## Purpose

Stage Six converts the seven-Part architecture into a working chapter system.

These are **functional chapter titles**, not final literary titles. We should not become attached to names yet. Each chapter is defined by its job, governing question, likely opening, evidence package, counterargument burden, principal reveal, reporting dependency, and transition.

The proposed architecture is:

- Prologue
- 24 core chapters across seven Parts
- Epilogue

This is large enough to carry the research without producing encyclopedic chapters and compact enough to preserve narrative momentum.

---

## Governing question

A collage-like but narrative sequence of modern warnings about Sharia, preferably anchored in one concrete American or Arkansas scene rather than quotations stacked without story.

# STAGE SIX STRESS TEST

## Does every chapter change the reader?

If a chapter merely contains interesting information, cut it or merge it.

## Does every chapter create the next chapter?

The reader should rarely feel that a new subject has simply been introduced because the author researched it.

## Are the founders overloaded?

Potential risk. Chapters 9–14 form a six-chapter founding sequence. This is justified only if written as discovery and human narrative rather than document summary.

## Is Part II too academic?

Potential risk. Chapters 4–6 must remain story-driven and relatively compact. The comparative-law material is subordinate.

## Is Arkansas too late?

Under the current architecture, deliberately yes. The reader reaches Arkansas with enough constitutional vocabulary to understand why local stories matter. However, Arkansas scenes can be used as brief interludes/foreshadowing earlier if reporting produces compelling material.

## Are Muslim voices too late?

This is deliberate but must be handled ethically. We should foreshadow living Muslim voices earlier and use published American Muslim evidence in Part II. The full original-reporting turn comes in Part VI because the reader is finally prepared to ask serious questions rather than treating interviewees as introductory explainers.

## Does the book end with law rather than people?

No. Chapter 26 and the Epilogue return to human conscience.

# STAGE SIX GATE

Stage Six is complete if the following architecture holds:

1. Every major Part now contains chapters with distinct jobs.
2. No chapter exists merely because a research pass exists.
3. The Muslim-president debate receives its own centerpiece chapter.
4. Jefferson's Qur'an remains subordinate to Jefferson's documented civil-rights language.
5. The founding contradiction receives its own chapter rather than a footnote.
6. The modern scare is preceded by genuine hard cases.
7. Arkansas receives four major documentary/legal chapters before the full reporting turn.
8. Muslim interviews are divided between ordinary lived religion and hard-case interrogation.
9. A serious Christian skeptic receives a full narrative chapter rather than a token quotation.
10. The final chapters solve jurisdiction and conscience rather than theology.
11. The Epilogue returns the argument to an actual human scene.
12. Nothing in the architecture requires proving America was built on Sharia.

If this gate holds, Stage Seven should be the **Architecture Stress Test and Pre-Reporting Gate**: attack the entire Prologue–26–Epilogue structure from the perspectives of a skeptical historian, Islamic-law scholar, conservative Christian, Muslim reader, former Muslim, constitutional lawyer, general trade reader, and narrative editor; identify redundancies, credibility failures, missing voices, pacing problems, and research/reporting requirements before the architecture is declared ready.
