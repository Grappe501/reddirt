# Sharia, the Constitution, and Religious Liberty — Research Archive

## Purpose
This folder is the working research archive for a long-form exposé/book project examining Sharia, Islamic legal thought, the Bible, Thomas Jefferson, the American founding, the Constitution and Bill of Rights, and modern religious-liberty politics.

The project is intended to defend universal religious liberty while rigorously distinguishing documented historical influence from plausible transmission and from mere parallel.

## Working premise
Religious liberty is only meaningful when it protects the religion a majority distrusts. Muslims were not merely accidental modern beneficiaries of constitutional language: founding-era Americans repeatedly used Muslims, then commonly called “Mahometans,” as a test case when debating whether liberty of conscience and eligibility for public office were truly universal.

The project will not claim that the United States Constitution was derived from Sharia unless documentary evidence supports that claim. Instead, evidence will be graded:

- A — Direct documented influence
- B — Strong historical connection
- C — Plausible intellectual transmission
- D — Significant parallel; causation unknown
- E — Superficial resemblance; do not publish as evidence

## Core methodological rule
Never collapse resemblance into causation. Every claim must identify whether it is a primary-source fact, documented intellectual connection, historically plausible transmission, comparative legal parallel, interpretation, or unresolved research question.
