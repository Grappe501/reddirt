# Research Pass 20 — Master Claim Audit

## Purpose

Pass 20 is the evidentiary gate between research and narrative construction.

The first nineteen passes generated historical facts, constitutional claims, comparative-law parallels, interpretations, modern political evidence, and proposed reporting hypotheses. This audit asks a harder question:

> What can the finished book actually say—and how strongly can it say it?

The project began with a deliberately provocative intuition: “America was built on Sharia.” The research does not support that as a literal historical claim. The research has instead produced a more defensible and more consequential thesis about religious liberty, Muslim inclusion, constitutional supremacy, and the difference between resemblance and genealogy.

This document therefore does four things:

1. grades the project's major claims;
2. kills or rewrites claims that exceed the evidence;
3. identifies unresolved claims requiring specialist review or original reporting;
4. establishes the factual spine permitted to govern the eventual master outline.

---

## Evidence grades

### A — Directly documented
Primary sources, controlling legal texts, judicial opinions, direct institutional records, or unusually strong documentary evidence establish the proposition.

### B — Strongly established
Multiple high-quality sources and mainstream scholarship support the proposition, but the claim may involve synthesis, interpretation, uncertain quantification, or contextual qualification.

### C — Plausible but qualified
There is serious evidence or scholarship supporting the proposition, but causation, scope, interpretation, or scholarly consensus remains disputed.

### D — Comparative parallel only
The resemblance is meaningful and publishable as comparison, but no historical causal relationship has been demonstrated.

### E — Unsupported, misleading, or unusable
The available evidence does not justify publication in the proposed form.

### Separate designation: CONFLICT
A historical or modern religious doctrine conflicts with modern American constitutional norms if government attempts to enforce it. This is not an evidence grade; it is a substantive classification.

# I. CENTRAL THESIS AUDIT

## Claim 1 — “America was built on Sharia.”

**Grade: E as literal historical causation.**

The research does not establish direct Islamic-law authorship or substantial documented Sharia influence on the U.S. Constitution.

There are plausible transmission questions involving Islamic civilization, European legal development, commercial law, trusts/waqf, and the common-law hypothesis. These do not establish that American constitutional framers consciously borrowed constitutional architecture from Sharia.

**Disposition: KILL as factual thesis.**

It may appear only as the provocative proposition the investigation tests and ultimately revises.

---

## Claim 2 — “The Constitution contains important principles that have meaningful analogues within Islamic legal and ethical thought.”

**Grade: D, with individual propositions sometimes B.**

Meaningful parallels include consultation, justice, contractual obligation, documentary evidence, property protection, proportionality, public welfare, and legal constraint.

These parallels do not establish genealogy.

**Disposition: KEEP with explicit parallel-not-parentage language.**

---

## Claim 3 — “Islamic civilization contributed significantly to the intellectual and legal development of Europe.”

**Grade: B in broad form.**

Arabic-to-Latin intellectual transmission, Mediterranean contact zones, Islamic scholarship, commercial interaction, and intellectual exchange are historically well established.

**Disposition: KEEP.**

Do not turn broad civilizational transmission into proof of specific constitutional borrowing.

---

## Claim 4 — “Islamic law substantially caused the emergence of English common law.”

**Grade: C.**

John A. Makdisi's common-law hypothesis is serious scholarship and identifies proposed parallels involving contract/debt, novel disseisin, and the lafif/jury. The thesis remains contested and does not establish a settled genealogy.

**Disposition: KEEP only as contested scholarly hypothesis.**

Required language: “One scholarly hypothesis argues…” or equivalent.

---

## Claim 5 — “The American founding generation explicitly contemplated Muslims when debating universal religious liberty.”

**Grade: A.**

Jefferson's writings, Richard Henry Lee, Washington-related evidence, Virginia petitions, Massachusetts commentary, and especially North Carolina's Article VI ratification debate explicitly name Muslims/Mahometans.

**Disposition: CORE CLAIM.**

This is one of the strongest pillars of the book.

---

## Claim 6 — “The possibility of a Muslim federal officeholder, including a Muslim president, was recognized during ratification.”

**Grade: A.**

North Carolina's 1788 ratification debate explicitly discussed Mahometans and the possibility of occupying the presidency/chair under the no-religious-test provision.

**Disposition: CORE CLAIM.**

Do not say every founder endorsed a Muslim president. Some speakers raised the possibility as an objection.

---

## Claim 7 — “America accidentally forgot to exclude Muslims from federal office.”

**Grade: E.**

The historical record points the other direction: critics noticed the absence of a test and explicitly objected to its implications.

**Disposition: KILL.**

Publishable inverse:

> The possibility was noticed, debated, and the federal Constitution still contained no Christian test.

# XXI. THESIS EVOLUTION

## Original thesis

> America was built on Sharia.

**Status: REJECTED as historical claim.**

## Second-stage thesis

> Sharia contains many principles similar to American constitutionalism.

**Status: PARTLY SUPPORTED, but too comparison-heavy and vulnerable to false genealogy.**

## Third-stage thesis

> The founders created religious liberty broad enough to include Muslims.

**Status: STRONGLY SUPPORTED.**

## Mature thesis after Pass 20

> America was not built on Sharia, and it was not constitutionally built to make biblical law sovereign either. It was built through a deeply Christian society into a federal constitutional order that increasingly placed civil rights and federal public office beyond the power of government to condition on religious identity. Muslims were explicitly contemplated within that argument. The constitutional achievement is not that Islam and Christianity are identical, harmless, or politically interchangeable. It is that neither must own the government for its believers to belong.

**Status: PUBLISHABLE with careful historical qualification.**

# XXII. THE BOOK'S BURDEN OF PROOF

The book does **not** need to prove:

- Islam is peaceful in every interpretation;
- Islamic law is compatible with every modern right;
- Christianity was unimportant to the founding;
- the founders liked Islam;
- Muslims never support theocracy;
- Sharia influenced the Constitution;
- American Muslims are politically homogeneous.

The book **does** need to prove:

- Muslims were within the founding-era conceptual universe of religious liberty;
- federal religious tests were deliberately excluded;
- constitutional protection does not depend on theological approval;
- Sharia is more complex than its political caricature;
- real hard cases exist and can be addressed by constitutional supremacy;
- suspicion of a doctrine is not evidence of an individual's disloyalty;
- religious liberty loses its meaning if it protects only majority religion.

That burden is achievable on the present evidence.

---

# XXIII. MASTER NARRATIVE PRINCIPLE

The investigation should not end by telling the reader:

> You were wrong to fear Islam because Islam is harmless.

The evidence cannot sustain that universal proposition, and the argument would require defending every Islamic interpretation.

The stronger ending is:

> You were asking the wrong constitutional question.

The question is not whether every Islamic doctrine is compatible with American law.

Some are not.

The question is whether a Muslim must surrender his religion before the Constitution recognizes him as fully American.

The founding record, Article VI, the First Amendment tradition, modern constitutional law, and the logic of equal citizenship point toward the same answer:

> No.
