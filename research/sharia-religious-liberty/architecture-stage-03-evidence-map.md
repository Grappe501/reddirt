# Narrative Architecture — Stage 3: Evidence Map

## Purpose

Stage 3 translates the twenty research passes into a usable narrative evidence system.

The goal is not to summarize every pass. The goal is to answer four questions before chapter design begins:

1. What evidence answers each governing question?
2. Which evidence is strong enough to carry a major narrative reveal?
3. Where are we duplicating the same proof in multiple places?
4. What still requires interviews, specialist review, or additional primary-source work?

This document should govern later chapter architecture. A chapter should not exist merely because a research pass exists. Research modules are warehouses; chapters are arguments and stories.

---

# Evidence status key

**A — Direct/documentary anchor**
Primary source, statute, constitutional text, court opinion, direct institutional record, or direct quotation strong enough to carry a major narrative reveal.

**B — Strong supporting evidence**
Reliable scholarship, multiple strong sources, or well-supported synthesis.

**C — Qualified/contested evidence**
Useful only with careful attribution or specialist caveat.

**D — Comparative evidence**
Meaningful analogy or parallel, not genealogy.

**R — Original reporting required**
Cannot responsibly complete the claim without interviews, archival reporting, or direct observation.

**S — Specialist review required**
Substantive claim is usable but should be checked by a domain expert before publication.

# What the evidence map tells us about the future book

The research is not evenly distributed—and that is useful.

The strongest book is **not** a book primarily arguing that Islamic law resembles American law. That evidence is interesting but frequently D-grade as genealogy.

The strongest book is built around four overwhelmingly better-supported pillars:

1. **The fear is partly based on real coercive doctrines, but the word “Sharia” has been made to carry too many meanings at once.**
2. **America's founding culture was deeply Christian, yet federal religious liberty and officeholding were deliberately framed broadly enough that contemporaries explicitly discussed Muslims as beneficiaries or consequences.**
3. **The constitutional answer is not to decide which religion is true; it is to protect voluntary conscience while preventing coercive religious sovereignty.**
4. **Arkansas provides a modern laboratory where that abstract rule can be tested against actual Muslim citizens, Christian majorities, religious-liberty law, and establishment disputes.**

Those four pillars should control Stage 4.

# Stage 3 conclusion

We now have enough evidence to stop thinking in research-pass order.

Pass numbers should largely disappear from the reader-facing architecture.

The next task is **Stage 4 — Revelation Order**.

That stage should decide not merely what evidence belongs in the book, but **when the reader is allowed to learn it**.

The crucial design questions will include:

- How long do we allow the reader to think the book may be defending Sharia?
- When do we concede the hard cases?
- When do we reveal that America was deeply Christian?
- When does Article VI appear?
- When does the 1788 Muslim-president debate explode the reader's assumptions?
- When should Jefferson's Qur'an appear so it feels like evidence rather than a gimmick?
- When do enslaved Muslims enter and morally complicate founding triumphalism?
- When does Arkansas arrive?
- When does Steve walk into the mosque?
- What is the final fact, scene, or question the reader encounters before the thesis becomes unavoidable?

Stage 4 should treat information like dramatic material: some facts establish trust, some create tension, some overturn assumptions, and some should be withheld until the reader has enough context for them to land.
