## Executive Summary

This follow-on did not enlarge the site. It answered two design observations from the prior review: Meet Kelly felt like a reading assignment between visual acts, and empty endorsements felt honest but unfinished. We changed rhythm and framing — not word count targets, not architecture, not claims.

Meet Kelly is now a conversation: a principle line first, then three labeled beats (who / prepared her / why this office), with the Mena portrait given sticky breathing room on large screens. Endorsements empty states now say support is earned and published when confirmed — emptiness as policy, not a missing module.

---

## Visitor Journey Review

After primary video, Meet Kelly should feel like a short talk with Kelly’s qualifications — not a scroll of prose. The principle quote lands first; the three cards create pause points. The CTA still invites deeper experience on `/about`. Endorsements no longer read as “we forgot to fill this”; they read as “we refuse to fake this.”

## Things that still bother me

- Three experience cards can still feel a little “card-y” if overused elsewhere; watch monotony next to Government That Works.  
- Sticky portrait may fight short viewports if sticky overlap with header feels tight — watch mobile (sticky is desktop-only).  
- Empty endorsements are warmer; they still don’t create social proof until a real confirmation arrives.

## The Three Hardest Decisions

1. **Left alone:** Hero still image — still no HERO-quality trail still under curation rules.  
2. **Biggest tradeoff:** Kept Meet Kelly word substance (targets intact) and spent layout complexity (beats + principle) instead of cutting more biography. Rhythm over deletion.  
3. **Evidence that would change recommendation:** A confirmed endorsement with source would replace empty framing immediately; a stronger listening still could become Meet Kelly’s portrait if Mena is ever outranked.
