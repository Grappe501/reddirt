# Fractal model — how the layers fit

## The idea in one sentence

**Same three roles, smaller geography, more frequent touchpoints.**

## Stack (typical cascade)

```text
State team
  → County team(s)
    → City / town team(s)
      → Precinct team(s)
        → Neighborhood / block team(s)
```

Each layer is still only **three coordinators**: Events, Social, Power of 5 / relational.

## Why fractals work in the field

1. **Clarity** — volunteers are never guessing what their job is; the role survives the zoom-in.
2. **Accountability** — each layer has a triad that can report numbers without a heavy bureaucracy.
3. **Speed** — local teams decide local timing; state supplies templates, training, and escalation paths.

## Decision rule: when to spin out a new triad

Spin out when **two** of these are true:

- You have **three people** willing to own distinct lanes for ~90 days.
- You can name **a geography** the new team exclusively serves (no overlap fights).
- The parent team **cannot** cover the needed contact frequency (events, posts, relational touches).

## Anti-patterns (avoid)

- Mixing “everyone does everything” — lanes blur and throughput dies.
- Naming a team without a **recurring huddle** — you created a group chat, not a field unit.
- Skipping documentation of **who owns which geography** — duplicates or gaps appear instantly.
