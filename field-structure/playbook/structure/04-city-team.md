# City / town team — responsibilities & cadence

## Purpose

Operate the three-person model where **most volunteers actually live** — tight geography, high familiarity, fast iteration.

## City / town triad

### Events coordinator

- Run a **monthly rhythm** locals can memorize (e.g., “2nd Tuesday community coffee, 4th Saturday service project visibility”).
- Partner with **credible local hosts** (libraries, coffee shops, faith orgs, clubs).
- Always assign: **setup**, **signup flow**, **program**, **follow-up**.

### Social coordinator

- Prioritize **local faces and local stories** over stock graphics.
- Build a **bench** of 5–10 posters with clear “when you post, tag X and DM Y”.
- Maintain a **FAQ** for recurring local questions so messaging stays consistent.

### Power of 5 / relational coordinator

- Divide the town into **natural micro-communities** (school feeder zones, neighborhoods, workplaces).
- Run **relational shifts**: short windows where volunteers focus on follow-ups, not new novelty.
- Recruit **block/zone leads** before burnout hits the town coordinator.

## Weekly city checklist (the “small but real” bar)

- **One** public-facing touch (event, table, partner room).
- **Three** pieces of social activity (posts, stories, responsible shares — adjust to platform realities).
- **Five** intentional relational touches across the team (not random scrolling).

## When to go to precinct / neighborhood teams

Go deeper when:

- Response times suffer because the geography is too big for one triad.
- You have leaders who already **live/work** in distinct zones.
- You can give each child team **non-overlapping turfs** and **clear dashboards**.
